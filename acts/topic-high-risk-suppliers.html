High-risk suppliers

"High-risk supplier" is on its way to becoming a horizontal eligibility filter in EU law: it would decide who may supply key ICT assets of essential and important entities, take part in public procurement and auctions, receive public support and EU funding, and take part in European standardisation, certification and conformity assessment. No act in force defines the term. The only definition is in the Commission proposal for a Cybersecurity Act 2 (Art. 2(39)); the proposals for an Industrial Accelerator Act and a Public Procurement Act already refer to it. The law in force works one step earlier, with non-technical risk factors (NIS-2, Cyber Resilience Act) and third-country jurisdiction tests in renewable energy auctions (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1176).

Our own assessment as of 29 Sep 2026; not legal advice. Only the law in force is binding. Proposals and negotiation drafts are not law and can still change; a draft report or a compromise text is the position of one rapporteur or Presidency, not of the institution.

Questions to answer

  • Is the company an important entity under the national NIS-2 law (size, NACE division 28)?
  • Who can exercise decisive influence over the company, directly or through intermediate entities, and where are its shareholders and executive management established?
  • Which ICT assets in its own plants (PLC, SCADA, MES, remote maintenance, firewalls) could be designated key ICT assets?
  • Which components in its machines come from suppliers established in or controlled from third countries?
  • What will customers in NIS-2 sectors require by contract?

What to prepare

  • Inventory of OT and ICT components in plants and products, with manufacturer, country of establishment and control.
  • Supplier declarations on ownership and control; clauses on change of control.
  • Watch the Union-level risk assessments (NIS-2 Art. 22) for the machinery and automation supply chain.

Questions to answer

  • Is the company an important entity (NACE division 26 or 27, size)?
  • Who can exercise decisive influence over the company, directly or through intermediate entities, and where are its shareholders and executive management established?

Subsidiary in China of an EU-based group

Company established in China, owned and controlled by a multinational group headquartered in the EU, supplying components, software or services to the group and to EU customers.

RoleAppliesWhat (affected process)Why
Regulated entity (NIS-2 Annex I or II)NoNone in the subsidiary; the group's EU plants (see the other profiles)NIS-2 applies to entities in the Union; the subsidiary itself is not an entity. The group's EU plants may be (see the other profiles).

Questions to answer

  • Which of the group's EU products and services contain its components, software or services, and in which key ICT assets of customers?
  • What access does it have to updates, remote maintenance and customer data?
  • Which third-country laws can compel it to report vulnerabilities or hand over data?
  • Could the relevant functions be carried out by an EU group company instead?

What to prepare

  • Map of deliveries into EU products and services, by customer sector.
  • Separation of update, remote access and data flows from the EU; documentation of the group's control.
  • Contingency for an exemption request (CSA2 Art. 105) or a move of the functions to the EU.
