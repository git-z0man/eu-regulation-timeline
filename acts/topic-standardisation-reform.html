New Standardisation Regulation – routes to presumption of conformity

On 6 October 2026 the Commission proposed a new Standardisation Regulation (COM(2026) 780) to replace Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012. It keeps CEN, CENELEC and ETSI at the centre but adds routes to a presumption of conformity when they cannot deliver in time: harmonised standardisation deliverables as an interim step, standards from designated standards development organisations, and a horizontal power to adopt common specifications. Requests get fixed content (a deadline per standard and an expiry date), the European standardisation organisations get performance indicators, and every referenced standard becomes free to read online. The Regulation would apply six months after entry into force, expected in 2028, without transitional rules. Acts such as the CRA and the Machinery Regulation tie their presumption to harmonised standards under Regulation 1025/2012; the proposal redirects those references but does not amend them. A draft, not law.

Our own assessment as of 11 Oct 2026; not legal advice. Only the law in force is binding. Proposals and negotiation drafts are not law and can still change; a draft report or a compromise text is the position of one rapporteur or Presidency, not of the institution.

European stakeholder organisations – criteria and candidates

The proposal would have the Commission designate four European stakeholder organisations, one each for SMEs, consumers, environmental interests and social interests, by implementing act and at least every four years (Art. 18). Annex II sets the same three criteria for each: (a) a non-governmental, non-profit body, independent of business interests except for SMEs; (b) statutes aimed at representing those interests in European standardisation; (c) a mandate from national non-profit organisations in at least two thirds of the Member States, 18 of 27. The criteria are those of Annex III of Regulation 1025/2012, which today only make an organisation eligible for Union financing; the SME organisation gets two more tasks in its statutes and the environmental one must work actively for environmental sustainability. A proposal, not law.

Rules for all four

  • From eligibility to designation: today an organisation that meets the criteria may receive Union financing, after a call for proposals or as an operating grant; the proposal has the Commission designate exactly one organisation per type, by implementing act after an open, non-discriminatory and transparent process, at least every four years. (Standardisation Regulation, Art. 16, Art. 18)
  • Tasks: take part in the standardisation activities of the ESOs and of designated standards development organisations for Union legislation; inform and build awareness among those they represent; report on membership and activities to the Commission by 31 January each year. (Art. 18)
  • Participation rights: the ESOs must enable their participation at four stages of requested standards (new work items, technical discussion, comments on drafts, revision); designated standards development organisations must enable it too. No voting right follows from this, but the ESOs should do their utmost to take their comments into account. (Art. 18, Art. 27, Recital 26)
  • Financing: the Union "may" support the designated organisations under the conditions of its funding programmes; there is no longer a list of financed activities as in Art. 16 of Regulation 1025/2012. (Art. 8)
  • Whom they should include: the consumer organisation should bring in equality stakeholders, in particular women and persons with disabilities; social interests are mainly those of workers, for instance trade unions. (Recital 26)

Who could be designated

The four organisations financed today under Annex III of Regulation 1025/2012 first, then other European organisations that represent the same interests. Today the Commission invites the four by name to the yearly call of the Single Market Programme (2026: EUR 5.8 million, an action grant for SBS and operating grants for the other three). Criterion (c) counts Member States with a national organisation that has given a mandate; members outside the EU (EFTA, candidate and third countries) do not count. Organisations for persons with disabilities are not candidates of their own: Recital 26 brings them in through the consumer organisation. Participation and inclusiveness

4 provisions (in force 1 · proposals 3)

Standardisation Regulation: Arts. 5, 16-17, Annex III

Official JournalPrecursor

Today the same criteria (Annex III) only make a European stakeholder organisation eligible for Union financing (Art. 16, grants under Art. 17), with no designation and no limit of one per interest; the ESOs must facilitate the participation of the financed organisations (Art. 5(1)). Financed today: SBS, ANEC, ECOS and ETUC.

Official source · Text: EN · DE (en.md de.md)

NSR: Arts. 17-21

Commission proposalConsequence

Participation of SMEs, consumer, environmental, social and research stakeholders and public authorities; designated European stakeholder organisations at least every four years (Art. 18); at national level free or reduced-rate participation and free access to drafts (Art. 20(2)).

Official source · act page · Text: EN (en.md)

NSR: Art. 18, Annex II

Commission proposalDefinition

The Commission designates one European stakeholder organisation each for SMEs, consumers, environmental and social interests by implementing act, at least every four years, if it meets the three criteria of Annex II: non-governmental and non-profit (independent of business interests except for SMEs), statutes aimed at European standardisation, a mandate from national organisations in at least 18 Member States. The criteria matrix and the candidates are in the section 'European stakeholder organisations'.

NSR: Art. 13

Commission proposalConsequence

NSR: Art. 8

Commission proposalConsequence

The Union and the Member States 'may contribute' to the financial sustainability of the system; Union grants to ESOs, designated organisations, stakeholder organisations and national bodies under the funding programmes' conditions; ESOs report on their financial sustainability by 31 March each year. The list of financed activities of Regulation 1025/2012 (Art. 15) is not carried over.
