New Standardisation Regulation – routes to presumption of conformity

On 6 October 2026 the Commission proposed a new Standardisation Regulation (COM(2026) 780) to replace Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012. It keeps CEN, CENELEC and ETSI at the centre but adds routes to a presumption of conformity when they cannot deliver in time: harmonised standardisation deliverables as an interim step, standards from designated standards development organisations, and a horizontal power to adopt common specifications. Requests get fixed content (a deadline per standard and an expiry date), the European standardisation organisations get performance indicators, and every referenced standard becomes free to read online. The Regulation would apply six months after entry into force, expected in 2028, without transitional rules. Acts such as the CRA and the Machinery Regulation tie their presumption to harmonised standards under Regulation 1025/2012; the proposal redirects those references but does not amend them. A draft, not law.

Our own assessment as of 06 Oct 2026; not legal advice. Only the law in force is binding. Proposals and negotiation drafts are not law and can still change; a draft report or a compromise text is the position of one rapporteur or Presidency, not of the institution.

Machinery manufacturer

Manufacturer of machinery with control systems, software and connectivity, designing to harmonised standards under the Machinery Regulation, the CRA and the AI Act.

RoleAppliesWhat (affected process)Why
User of harmonised standards (manufacturer)YesDesign and conformity assessment: choice of harmonised standards, technical documentation, EU declaration of conformityPresumption of conformity under Art. 20 MR and Art. 27 CRA rests on harmonised standards; free access (Art. 5) removes purchase costs, new routes (deliverables, designated organisations, common specifications) add sources to watch.
Contributor to standardisation (experts in technical bodies)PossibleEngineering experts in CEN/TC 114 and sector committees, via the national mirror committeesRequests get deadlines per standard and performance indicators (Arts. 13, 23); the ESOs must speed up, and missed deadlines can move work to other organisations (Art. 29(1)).

Questions to answer

  • Which harmonised standards under M/605 phase 2 (due 2034) does the product range depend on, and could they be delayed by the switch to the new Regulation?
  • Would an interim deliverable or a standard from a designated organisation give a presumption under Art. 20 MR without an amendment of the MR?
  • Which purchased standards would become free, and does the company still need national adoptions (DIN EN) for contracts?

What to prepare

  • List the harmonised standards used per product family with their mandate (M/605, M/606) and status.
  • Follow the Council and Parliament positions on transitional rules for requests in progress.
  • Plan participation in the consultation on draft requests (one month on the EU Standards Platform, Art. 23(4)).

Instruments of presumption (harmonised standards, deliverables, common specifications)

5 provisions (in force 3 · proposals 2)

CRA: Art. 27(1)-(3), (6)

Official JournalCross-reference

Presumption of conformity only for harmonised standards cited in the OJ; requests under Art. 10(1) of Regulation 1025/2012; CRA common specifications only after an ESO request failed. A harmonised standardisation deliverable or a standard from a designated organisation is not named; the proposal does not amend the CRA.

MR: Art. 20(1)-(3)

Official JournalCross-reference

Same pattern as the CRA: presumption by harmonised standards cited in the OJ, requests under Art. 10(1) of Regulation 1025/2012, common specifications only after an ESO request failed.

Official source · act page · Text: EN · DE (en.md de.md)

AI Act: Art. 40

Official JournalCross-reference

Presumption by harmonised standards published 'in accordance with Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012'; requests under its Art. 10 and reporting under its Art. 24.

Official source · act page · Text: EN · DE (en.md de.md)

Standardisation Regulation (new): Art. 2(5)-(9)

Commission proposalDefinition

Referenced standards and deliverables; harmonised standards; harmonised standardisation deliverables, which give a presumption of conformity and are to be replaced by a harmonised standard (Art. 2(7)); standards in support of Union policies without a presumption; common specifications.

Official source · act page · Text: EN (en.md)

Standardisation Regulation (new): Art. 32

Commission proposalDefinition

Horizontal power to adopt common specifications by implementing act when the ESOs decline or would not deliver in time, or after a reference was withdrawn (Art. 32(2)), or when no harmonised standard is expected within a reasonable period (Art. 32(3)); repealed or amended once a standard is cited (Art. 32(8)).

Standardisation requests and deadlines

3 provisions (in force 1 · proposals 2)

Standardisation Regulation: Art. 10

Official JournalPrecursor

Requests to the ESOs by implementing decision, with deadlines set case by case; since Regulation (EU) 2022/2480, decisions on requests and on adopting the standards are taken by the national standardisation bodies' delegates only (Art. 10(2a)).

Official source · Text: EN · DE (en.md de.md)

Standardisation Regulation (new): Art. 22

Commission proposalIdentification

Before any request the Commission consults the ESOs on their ability to deliver; they reply within one month. Depending on the answer the Commission requests the ESOs, a designated standards development organisation, or adopts common specifications (Art. 22(4)).

Official source · act page · Text: EN (en.md)

Standardisation Regulation (new): Art. 23

Commission proposalConsequence

Requests by implementing act with the content requirements, a delivery deadline for each standard and an expiry date (Art. 23(2)); optionally interim deliverables, periodic revision, cooperation between ESOs and a restriction of participation (Art. 23(3)). Draft published for one month of comments (Art. 23(4)); ESOs accept within one month (Art. 23(7)); the Commission 'shall endeavour' to adopt within 12 months of the consultation (Art. 23(5)).

GPSR: Art. 48

Official JournalCross-reference

Amended Regulation 1025/2012 so that references of European standards supporting the GPSR are published without delay; repealed by Art. 42(2) of the proposal.

Standardisation Regulation (new): Art. 25

Commission proposalConsequence

The Commission assesses delivered standards within six months, publishes compliant references by implementing act or as the sectoral act provides ('shall endeavour' within three months), withdraws an interim deliverable when the harmonised standard is cited (Art. 25(7)), and may amend the request, move it to a designated organisation or adopt common specifications when delivery fails (Art. 25(8)).

Standardisation Regulation (new): Arts. 33-34

Commission proposalConsequence

Safeguard notification by a Member State, the European Parliament or the Commission replaces the formal objection; decision within six or twelve months (Art. 33). References are withdrawn when the standards body withdraws the standard (Art. 34).

Standardisation Regulation (new): Art. 13

Commission proposalConsequence

Performance indicators for the ESOs (speed, quality, inclusiveness, cooperation with national bodies, international influence) set by implementing act; annual report by 31 January; structural dialogue twice a year. Not directly linked to financing in the text.

Official source · act page · Text: EN (en.md)

Standardisation Regulation (new): Art. 8

Commission proposalConsequence

The Union and the Member States 'may contribute' to the financial sustainability of the system; Union grants to ESOs, designated organisations, stakeholder organisations and national bodies under the funding programmes' conditions; ESOs report on their financial sustainability by 31 March each year. The list of financed activities of Regulation 1025/2012 (Art. 15) is not carried over.

Official source · act page · Text: EN (en.md)

Standardisation Regulation (new): Financial statement, 3.2; Explanatory memorandum, 4

Commission proposalConsequence

Operational appropriations 'commensurate' with today's standardisation budget in the Single Market Programme (EUR 23.7 million in 2027): EUR 177.575 million for 2028-2034 in the financial statement, EUR 183 million in the explanatory memorandum. Free access may cost national bodies up to EUR 30 million a year (impact assessment); targeted support could flow through higher operating grants to the ESOs, by redeployment.
