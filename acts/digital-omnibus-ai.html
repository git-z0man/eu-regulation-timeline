    Corrigendum published 32026R1744R(01)

Dates and obligations

Not on the timeline. Proposal: 19 Nov 2025 · Adopted: 8 Jul 2026 · Published: 24 Jul 2026

What it changes in AI Act: Amends the AI Act in 43 points to simplify its implementation; among others it replaces Art. 4 (AI literacy), moves the obligations for high-risk AI systems to 2 December 2027 (Annex III) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I) (Art. 113 as amended) and gives providers of generative AI systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 until 2 December 2026 to comply with Art. 50(2) (Art. 111(4)).
What it changes in MR: Empowers the Commission to add health and safety requirements for AI systems that are high-risk under Art. 6(1) AI Act to Annex III of the Machinery Regulation by delegated act, applying by 2 August 2028 (Art. 8 and 47 amended), and adds a transitional rule on the harmonised standards and common specifications of the AI Act for such systems (new Art. 20(10)).

25 Nov 2025 · Digital Package

Commission statement · procedure 2025/2985(RSP)

Speakers: Commission and Council: Henna Virkkunen (Executive Vice-President of the Commission, spoke 2 times). Rapporteurs and shadow rapporteurs of this act: Virginie Joron (PfE, shadow rapporteur, IMCO), Piotr Müller (ECR, shadow rapporteur, IMCO), Svenja Hahn (Renew, shadow rapporteur, IMCO), Sergey Lagodinsky (Verts/ALE, rapporteur for opinion, JURI), Markéta Gregorová (Verts/ALE, shadow rapporteur, LIBE), Brando Benifei (S&D, shadow rapporteur, IMCO), Laurence Farreng (Renew, shadow rapporteur, CULT, shadow rapporteur, JURI), Kristian Vigenin (S&D, shadow rapporteur, LIBE), Michael McNamara (Renew, rapporteur, LIBE), Kim Van Sparrentak (Verts/ALE, shadow rapporteur, IMCO), Oihane Agirregoitia Martínez (Renew, shadow rapporteur, TRAN) and 24 other Members: PPE 8, S&D 6, ECR 3, Renew 3, The Left 2, Verts/ALE 2.

Our summary: Executive Vice-President Virkkunen presented the digital package the Commission had adopted the week before: the Data Union Strategy, the European Business Wallets and the Digital Omnibus, which simplifies rules on AI, cybersecurity and data. She promised savings of at least EUR 5 billion by 2029 from the Omnibus, digital solutions to end duplication in incident reporting, adjustments to the AI Act so that standards, specifications and guidelines are in place before the high-risk rules apply, and targeted amendments to the GDPR that would not lower data protection; she said the fitness check of the whole digital rulebook she had announced a year earlier had been launched. Tomas Tobé (EPP) and Svenja Hahn (Renew) welcomed the Omnibus as cutting red tape. Sergey Lagodinsky (Greens/EFA) objected that it reopens recently adopted laws without impact assessment and that the open end of the "stop-the-clock" for high-risk AI systems makes planning impossible; Markéta Gregorová (Greens/EFA) called the package a sell-out of European values to American pressure.

Full text, original language (EN): Henna Virkkunen, for the Commission

Mr President, Honourable Members, last week, the Commission adopted the first digital simplification package. Our goal is very clear: making life easier for our companies – especially the smallest ones – and for consumers, while remaining true to our values and our high standards. In Europe, we have all it takes to succeed – we have talent, we have infrastructure, and we have a large single market where businesses should be able to scale up. But our companies, especially our start-ups and small businesses, are often held back. This package is our immediate answer to calls to reduce burdens and encourage innovation. We are doing so through three measures. Firstly, we presented the Data Union Strategy to fuel the digital economy, which is an essential resource, with-high quality data for our AI development. Secondly, the European Business Wallets offer an easy digital solution for businesses to interact with authorities and other businesses across our Europe. Finally, the Digital Omnibus simplifies our rules in the areas of artificial intelligence, cybersecurity and data. This does not mean we are in any way undermining our existing digital rulebook. We can be very proud of the rules we have, which were adopted together with this House during the last mandates. Let me underline that we remain very committed to the implementation and enforcement of these rules.

The Digital Package firmly supports our high standards for privacy, fairness and security. EU regulation is a global trust mark for businesses that respect fundamental rights. This should not be a burden, but a competitive advantage for our companies. For this, we should get rid of regulatory clutter, where there is any, and we should instead focus on clear and predictable rules and solid enforcement. The Digital Package does this firstly by cutting unnecessary administrative costs for businesses by at least EUR 5 billion by 2029 through the Digital Omnibus, and by saving companies at least EUR 150 billion per year with our European Business Wallets. We cut bureaucratic obligations, in particular for SMEs and small mid-caps, and built digital solutions to cut all duplications in incident reporting. Then, on the AI Act, we made adjustments to ensure effective application. This is not about backing down from these rules, but making sure that the support tools, like standards, specifications and guidelines, are in place before high-risk rules apply.

Finally, we are stimulating innovation. We propose targeted amendments to the GDPR that aim to facilitate compliance and support technological innovation in the EU, while continuing to ensure a high level of data protection. We are simplifying certain requirements, such as data breach notifications and information obligations, which will particularly help our SMEs. We are ensuring that AI development is encouraged in Europe and clearly framed by our rules. We are also clarifying rules in the context of recent judgments from the Court of Justice of the EU and opinions from the European Data Protection Board. These targeted amendments do not lower the level of data protection across the EU. They harmonise, simplify and clarify the application of our rules.

In the Data Union Strategy, we are also proposing measures for scaling up the availability of high-quality data for AI development in the EU, and we are strengthening our approach to international data flows – stronger data sharing within the EU, while being more careful with data leakage of trade secrets to unsafe third countries. These measures should not only help our businesses, but also our consumers. We are finally now addressing the cookie banner fatigue. I think we can all agree that we have spent quite enough time accepting or rejecting cookies every time we visit a website, and at the same time, it is very important that users stay in control.

Honourable Members, our proposal is a result of broad engagement with European businesses, including our SMEs, civil society and beyond. We all know very well that very big global companies can deal with administrative burden and fragmented markets; smaller businesses cannot. We are doing this package for Europe. I count on open discussion and cooperation with you to take this package forward so that we can encourage innovation in Europe, while always protecting our fundamental rights.

Full text, original language (EN): Henna Virkkunen, for the Commission

Mr President, honourable Members, I want to thank everybody who contributed to this discussion for your very valuable inputs. Why are we proposing this package? You are very well aware about the Draghi report and the innovation gap we are facing. We are very committed as a Commission to make Europe faster and easier and simpler for businesses, especially our start-ups and SMEs, to make sure that Europe is the place to innovate and invest and scale up businesses.

But the discussion today also highlights the importance of maintaining a fair balance between practical action and protection of our safeguards here. I believe that this digital package strikes this right balance. I count on your support in advancing the proposed legislative files, the digital omnibus and the European business wallet as well. And I would also like to stress that this is the first set of urgent measures. These immediate actions are needed to bring a breath of fresh air to our businesses and the EU's competitiveness.

But this is not the last step when we speak about simplification. In my confirmation hearing one year ago at the European Parliament, I already announced a digital fitness check for our entire digital rulebook, and we launched this process in the last week. Wide consultations are ongoing, so we are collecting feedback and input from our stakeholders as to how we can simplify our digital rulebook in the future. Together, let's make it easier to innovate and invest in Europe while staying true to our values.

4 Nov 2026 · Valdis Dombrovskis, Commissioner for Economy and Productivity, Implementation and Simplification

Brussels · Remarks by Commissioners Šefčovič and Dombrovskis on the 2027 Commission work programme and 2026 Overview Report on Simplification, Implementation and Enforcement

Our summary: Dombrovskis and Šefčovič present the 2027 Commission work programme and the Overview Report on simplification, implementation and enforcement. Dombrovskis counts 12 omnibus packages proposed so far and lists digital markets among the simplification priorities for 2027; the digital omnibus is not discussed on its own.

The Commission has now proposed 12 Omnibus packages – Valdis Dombrovskis, original language (EN)

The simplification priorities for 2027 are banking, transport, digital markets, housing as well as accelerating permitting. – Valdis Dombrovskis, original language (EN)

Full text, original language (EN): Valdis Dombrovskis, Commissioner for Economy and Productivity, Implementation and Simplification, 4 Nov 2026

Commissioner Šefčovič:

Good afternoon everyone.

It is a pleasure to present to you the Commission work programme 2027, adopted by the College today alongside the Annual Overview Report on Simplification, Implementation and Enforcement, which Valdis will cover shortly.

This is a strategic, targeted, and streamlined work programme, doubling down on our political priorities and main work streams.

It shows the way forward for Europe to retain its ability to forge its own path, as we strive to become stronger and more independent.

The work programme reflects our core priorities as laid out in the Political Guidelines, and has shaped by our extensive outreach with stakeholders including the European Parliament and Member States.

As well as close coordination across the Commission and its services.

It has a clear focus on delivering on our ambitious policies, and implementing and enforcing what is already in place.

So that we translate our work into concrete results on the ground.

The key policy priorities for the next year are competitiveness and energy prices, responding to the demands of Europeans and European businesses.

In the annexes of the work programme, we present a targeted list of 23 new initiatives for next year, 10 of which have simplification as one of their key objectives.

As well as a list of pending priority proposals which are already on the table, to deliver on our commitments under the One Europe, One Market roadmap, with competitiveness and simplification at its heart.

We have an ambitious list proposing 19 files, including those where prospects for progress are limited, for withdrawal.

And 14 for repeal.

Of course, it is worth underlining that just because an initiative is not included in the work programme, does not mean that it cannot be put forward where necessary.

In conclusion, this work programme will help us deliver for Europe in 2027.

Whether it's ensuring Europe's sustainable prosperity and competitiveness.

Strengthening our social model.

Or our defence and security.

Sustaining our quality of life.

Promoting democracy and upholding our values.

Enhancing Europe's role on the global stage.

Or preparing for the EU for the future.

So in the Commission work programme 2027, I think we have a very precise compass for our work next year.

Thank you.

*****

Commissioner Dombrovskis:

Thank you. Good afternoon, everyone.

Today the Commission is presenting its Annual Overview Report on simplification, implementation and enforcement.

But our simplification agenda is more than figures in a report.

It is a story unfolding every day in the offices, factories and farms across Europe.

By the small business owner spending less time filling in forms, and more time focused on innovation and expansion.

The start-up that no longer needs a compliance department to bring its idea to market.

And the farmer who faces fewer inspections and can get on with the job of growing Europe's food.

Hours given back.

Money saved.

Confidence restored.

That is what simplification means for the people and businesses of Europe.

It is a story we are determined to keep writing, because there remains much work ahead of us.

With that in mind, let me turn now to the contents of our Report.

And to what it tells us about our progress on simplification, implementation and enforcement over the past year.

Firstly, on simplification.

The Commission has now proposed 12 Omnibus packages – including six over the period covered by the report – and several other simplification initiatives.

Their estimated annual savings exceed €22 billion, including €7.7 billion for our small and medium-sized enterprises.

This translates into almost 60% of the target reduction we set at the beginning of the mandate.

This is real progress.

Let me share with you some concrete examples:

greater coherence in data protection, cybersecurity and artificial intelligence obligations;

streamlined reporting requirements, with a particular focus on SMEs;

smarter and faster environmental assessments to facilitate housing, industrial and other essential projects;

and streamlining cross-border tax compliance procedures.

Several proposals have already entered into force. And their effects are beginning to materialise in the real economy.

Co-legislators have committed to making swift progress on all others, in line with the ‘One Europe, One Market' roadmap.

Here, I would like to stress – once again – that the success of our simplification agenda is a joint endeavour.

What emerges from the legislative process should be no less ambitious than what we have put forward.

And we must continue to deepen our simplification efforts, as outlined in this year's Commission work programme.

Our intensive engagement with stakeholders shows that there is strong support and momentum for simplification.

There is a window of opportunity.

Europe cannot afford to miss it.

Moving to implementation.

The report shows that the Commission has stepped up its efforts to support Member States in ensuring that EU laws and policies are implemented swiftly, effectively and consistently.

To provide some practical examples of this fresh emphasis in action:

on competitiveness, we have focused on preventing and removing barriers that fragment the Single Market.

on energy, we provided guidance and technical support to Member States in phasing out Russian energy imports.

and on defence, we held 200 meetings to help Member States speed up their national plans under SAFE.

We have also begun work on a toolkit of best practices and practical guidance to help Member States avoid gold-plating.

The future Pact against gold-plating will be an opportunity for Member States to work with the Commission in identifying and removing barriers, while safeguarding rights and protections.

And finally, to enforcement.

The report shows how the Commission is enforcing EU law in a more cooperative, determined and transparent manner.

This is in line with April's Communication on a simpler, clearer and better enforced EU rulebook , where we committed to enforcing EU rules better and faster, across all policy areas.

The report highlights:

the increased use of pre-infringement dialogues, with a 74% success rate;

the launch of the highest number of infringement cases in the last 10 years (874), alongside 61 referrals to the Court;

progress in securing compliance in long-standing cases;

and the benefits of enforcing our single market rulebook.

So, overall our commitment is clear:

simpler EU rules,

greater support to implement them,

and robust enforcement where they are not adhered to.

Looking ahead now, to the strong simplification dimension of the 2027 work programme, which Maroš has just presented.

It builds on the Action Plan on Regulatory Deep Cleaning that we presented in April.

Because the Commission is determined to address outdated, overlapping and fragmented rules.

The simplification priorities for 2027 are banking, transport, digital markets, housing as well as accelerating permitting.

We will put forward ambitious proposals in each of these areas to deliver simpler and more coherent rules.

Because our goal is a Single Market that is more dynamic, more integrated, more resilient and better equipped to support economic growth and high-quality jobs.

Thank you.
