European Product Act: two possible structures

The European Product Act has not been proposed yet. Two structures are possible; the forecast covers both.

One act: The European Product Act as one act that merges the New Legislative Framework, the Market Surveillance Regulation and the Standardisation Regulation. The separate proposal for a New Standardisation Regulation of 6 October 2026 would then be folded into it. Official Journal: median Aug 2028, half of the runs Jan 2028 to Oct 2029; before the 2029 elections: 72 %.
Package of two acts: Two acts negotiated side by side, the New Standardisation Regulation (proposed on 6 October 2026) and one act on market surveillance and the New Legislative Framework. The package is complete when both are published. Official Journal of both acts: median Mar 2029, half of the runs Sep 2028 to May 2030; before the 2029 elections: 53 %.

How the forecast works

Comparable laws. The European Parliament's Legislative Observatory lists the key events of every file of the ordinary legislative procedure. We take all 1005 files proposed since 2012 that were negotiated in trilogues (files withdrawn or adopted without negotiations are left out; before about 2015 the Observatory did not record trilogues, so a first reading whose plenary vote came more than 60 days after the committee vote counts as negotiated, unless it is a codification or an urgent procedure) and measure how long each step took: committee vote, start of trilogues, provisional agreement, Parliament's vote, Council adoption, Official Journal.

Of similar size. Large laws take longer: of the files since 2012, the largest quarter by size took about twice as long as the smallest (median 28 against 14 months of working time). We count the words of legal text in each Commission proposal (recitals, articles and annexes, in the English text in EUR-Lex; 972 of the 1005 files measured) and compare a file with the 40 % of comparable files nearest to it in size. A law not proposed yet, or whose proposal could not be measured yet, is compared with files of every size.

From where a file stands. For each open file we take the comparable files of similar size that reached the same step and had not moved on after as long as this file has waited. If fewer than 40 files of similar size have waited as long, files of every size count; the condition on the time waited is always kept. Files that are still open are continued with a published file that took at least as long. 4,000 runs per file give the median, the middle half and the range of eight in ten.

Calendar. Parliament does no legislative work between its last session before the European elections and the autumn, when its new committees vote again. We take these breaks of 2014, 2019 and 2024 out of the measured durations and put the break of 2029 into the forecast: a step that would fall into it moves to its end. The 2029 election dates are not set yet; we assume the calendar of 2024. Council Presidencies: Council Decision (EU) 2016/1316.

Track record. We forecast every file proposed in 2016-2021 (426 files) from the day of its proposal, with only what was known on that day. The real publication date fell in the middle half of the forecast for 48 % of the files (expected: 50 %) and in the eight-in-ten range for 80 % (expected: 80 %); the median missed by 6.9 months for the typical file. With all comparable files regardless of size, the method until 13 October 2026: 50 % in the middle half, 83 % in the eight-in-ten range, median error 8.2 months (the forecast as it was). Until 14 October 2026 a file adopted at first reading counted as negotiated only when a trilogue was recorded, which left out many negotiated files of 2012 to 2014; with them the median error fell from 7.6 to 6.9 months (the forecast as it was).

What it does not know. Political priority, a deal between the institutions or a new crisis: urgent files can be much faster. We also tested the consultations on "Have your say", the State of the Union address, the rapporteur's political group and the density of the wording: none showed a clear link with the durations once the size is taken into account. Sources: EP Legislative Observatory, the Commission proposals in EUR-Lex, the procedure registers on our procedure pages, Council Decision (EU) 2016/1316.
