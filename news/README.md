- 2026-11-10 · Standards · Cyber Resilience Act: [Cyber Resilience Act · M/606: deadlines for the harmonised standards extended](../acts/cra.html#standardisation). The Commission amended standardisation request M/606 on 10 Nov 2026 (C(2026)7861, M/606 Amd 1), replacing its Annex I: items 1 and 15 from 30 Aug 2026 to 30 Nov 2026, items 16–41 from 30 Oct 2026 to 31 Jan 2027; items 2–14 unchanged (30 Oct 2027). *What it means:* The new deadlines are conditional. Art. 2: if CEN, Cenelec or ETSI “indicate that they do not accept the amendments … within a month of receiving it”, the Decision ceases to apply and C(2025)618 in its version of 3 February 2025 continues to apply. Harmonised standards developed under the request give a presumption of conformity once cited in the Official Journal; the deadlines bind the standardisation organisations, not manufacturers.

- 2026-11-11 *new* · Legislation · Cybersecurity Act 2: [Cybersecurity Act 2: debate in Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/CRE-10-2026-11-11-ITM-019_EN.html). The Irish Council Presidency (Marian Harkin) reported progress on the three main parts of the Cybersecurity Act revision (ENISA, certification, ICT supply chains) and aims for agreement in the Council by the end of 2026, so that trilogues can start in 2027. *What it means:* Executive Vice-President Virkkunen put supply chain security at the core of the proposal, called for clear timelines to phase out high-risk suppliers, above all in 5G networks, and urged Parliament to give ENISA the mandate and resources it needs; she said full transposition and effective implementation of the NIS2 Directive could potentially have prevented some recent incidents or reduced their impact. Of the shadow rapporteurs, Tomas Tobé (EPP) asked for quick adoption with a framework to phase out high-risk suppliers, Jens Geier (S&D) for extending the scope to energy networks, and Markus Buchheit (ESN) opposed more resources for ENISA, a central reporting point and new certificates.

- 2026-11-05 · Analysis · Site: [The ECCC: EUR 730 million in cybersecurity grants, paid out slowly, and a mandate that ends in 2029](articles/eccc-money.html). The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre in Bucharest now manages 224 projects worth EUR 730 million from Digital Europe and Horizon Europe. Its budget peaked in 2024 and falls to about EUR 170 million a year; payments lag behind commitments by more than two years' worth. What comes after 2027 is open: Digital Europe is to merge into a Competitiveness Fund, and the Centre itself is set up only until the end of 2029.
- 2026-11-05 · Analysis · Site: [Is ENISA equipped for its tasks? Half its money is temporary, and the Cybersecurity Act 2 would mainly make it permanent](articles/enisa-resources.html). ENISA's own budget has grown little since 2022 while the acts giving it tasks multiplied. The growth came from time-limited agreements with the Commission: in 2025 they paid 51 % of its revenue, and they run out in 2028. The Commission's proposal for a Cybersecurity Act 2 would turn much of that money into a permanent budget, but adds little on top, and its timing, the next EU budget and the fees are open.
