- 2026-11-11 · Legislation · Cybersecurity Act 2: [Cybersecurity Act 2: debate in Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/CRE-10-2026-11-11-ITM-019_EN.html). The Irish Council Presidency (Marian Harkin) reported progress on the three main parts of the Cybersecurity Act revision (ENISA, certification, ICT supply chains) and aims for agreement in the Council by the end of 2026, so that trilogues can start in 2027. *What it means:* Executive Vice-President Virkkunen put supply chain security at the core of the proposal, called for clear timelines to phase out high-risk suppliers, above all in 5G networks, and urged Parliament to give ENISA the mandate and resources it needs; she said full transposition and effective implementation of the NIS2 Directive could potentially have prevented some recent incidents or reduced their impact. - 2026-11-13 · Committees · EU Space Act: [EU Space Act: Council working party meeting](../procedures/space-act.html). Progress report on the EU Space Act of 13 November 2026, sent by the General Secretariat of the Council to Coreper and the Council for the Competitiveness Council of 3-4 December 2026, which are invited to take note of it; it records the work of the Irish Presidency, is not a Council position and changes no text. *What it means:* It reports on the Presidency compromise text of 29 October 2026: "EUSA verification" replaces "EUSA registration" for third-country operators and international organisations, the scope excludes space objects operated by military or defence authorities for their entire lifetime or owned by military authorities, a self-certification without a qualified technical body is offered for spacecraft with governmental ownership or operation, the rules on space-based data now centre on Earth observation data bought by public contracting authorities and Union bodies, and the environmental footprint gives way to a life-cycle assessment with a voluntary Union method. On cybersecurity the Presidency did not modify the provisions; the report says the compromise text deals with the scope of the NIS-2 Directive, third-country operators and derogations through two implementing acts. All Member States keep a scrutiny reservation, and the Working Party continues with a view to reaching the Council's position.
