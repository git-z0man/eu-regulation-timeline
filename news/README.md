- 2026-11-20 · Legislation · EU Space Act: [EU Space Act: Report tabled for plenary](../procedures/space-act.html). Report of Parliament's Industry, Research and Energy committee on the EU Space Act proposal, adopted in committee on 19 November 2026 and tabled for plenary on 20 November 2026; it is the committee's text for the plenary vote, not Parliament's position and not law. *What it means:* Like the rapporteur's draft report, it removes the proposal's own cybersecurity regime for space operators (risk management, incident reporting and the resilience annex) and instead extends the space sector of the NIS 2 Directive to all space activities, space services and Union-owned assets, with transposition 24 months after entry into force; unlike the draft, it keeps the link to NIS 2 as a without-prejudice clause and drops the reduced duty to report incidents. It also cuts the authorisation deadline to six months with tacit approval where national law allows (not for launch services), adds a security and defence filter for authorisations, exempts SMEs and research institutions from the environmental footprint rules and deletes the Union Space Label; none of the tracked concepts is touched.

- 2026-11-17 · Legislation · Cloud and AI Development Act: [Cloud and AI Development Act: CJ18 draft report](../procedures/cloud-ai-development-act.html). Joint draft report of 17 November 2026 of the co-rapporteurs of the Industry, Research and Energy and the Internal Market committees on the proposed Cloud and AI Development Act, with 366 amendments to the Commission proposal; it is the rapporteurs' draft, not the position of the committees or of Parliament, and not law. *What it means:* On data centres it would make the designation of acceleration zones optional and add an impact analysis, grid, water and local benefit requirements, anti-speculation rules for grid reservations and a time-limited priority for Union operators not controlled from third countries. Progress report on the EU Space Act of 13 November 2026, sent by the General Secretariat of the Council to Coreper and the Council for the Competitiveness Council of 3-4 December 2026, which are invited to take note of it; it records the work of the Irish Presidency, is not a Council position and changes no text. *What it means:* It reports on the Presidency compromise text of 29 October 2026: "EUSA verification" replaces "EUSA registration" for third-country operators and international organisations, the scope excludes space objects operated by military or defence authorities for their entire lifetime or owned by military authorities, a self-certification without a qualified technical body is offered for spacecraft with governmental ownership or operation, the rules on space-based data now centre on Earth observation data bought by public contracting authorities and Union bodies, and the environmental footprint gives way to a life-cycle assessment with a voluntary Union method.
