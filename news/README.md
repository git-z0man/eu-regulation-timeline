- 2026-11-05 · Analysis · Site: [The ECCC: EUR 730 million in cybersecurity grants, paid out slowly, and a mandate that ends in 2029](articles/eccc-money.html). The European Cybersecurity Competence Centre in Bucharest now manages 224 projects worth EUR 730 million from Digital Europe and Horizon Europe. Its budget peaked in 2024 and falls to about EUR 170 million a year; payments lag behind commitments by more than two years' worth. What comes after 2027 is open: Digital Europe is to merge into a Competitiveness Fund, and the Centre itself is set up only until the end of 2029.
- 2026-11-05 · Analysis · Site: [Is ENISA equipped for its tasks? Half its money is temporary, and the Cybersecurity Act 2 would mainly make it permanent](articles/enisa-resources.html). ENISA's own budget has grown little since 2022 while the acts giving it tasks multiplied. The growth came from time-limited agreements with the Commission: in 2025 they paid 51 % of its revenue, and they run out in 2028. The Commission's proposal for a Cybersecurity Act 2 would turn much of that money into a permanent budget, but adds little on top, and its timing, the next EU budget and the fees are open.

- 2026-10-27 · Legislation · NIS 2 Directive: [NIS 2 Directive: Commission plans an implementing act](../acts/nis2.html). The NIS2 Directive sets out obligations on essential and important entities in 18 critical sectors in the EU for the reporting of significant cybersecurity incidents. This act will provide common incident notification templates for notifications under the NIS2 Directive, thereby streamlining the format and the type of information to be notified across all 27 Member States and supporting the Commission’s broader simplification agenda. *What it means:* Formal title: Commission Implementing Regulation further specifying the type of information, the format and the procedure of a notification submitted pursuant to Article 23(1) of Directive (EU) 2022/2555. The Commission adopts it after the opinion of the Member States in the Cybersecurity Committee, under the examination procedure (Art. 5, 6 and 7 of Regulation (EU) No 182/2011). A draft is to be published for feedback on Have your say.

- 2026-10-29 · Legislation · EU Space Act: [EU Space Act: Presidency compromise text - clean version](../procedures/space-act.html). Presidency compromise text on the EU Space Act of 29 October 2026, a clean version without change marks sent by the General Secretariat of the Council for the meeting of 3 November 2026; all delegations have a scrutiny reservation, so it is the Presidency's draft, not a Council position and not law, and it can still change. *What it means:* Compared with the clean text of March 2026, it adds market surveillance by Member States under Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, lets a Member State self-certify spacecraft with substantial governmental involvement without a qualified technical body, narrows the obligation on space-based data to Earth observation data supplied to public contracting authorities and Union bodies (plus a landing-rights criterion for satellite communication), replaces the defence exclusions, and turns the environmental footprint into a life-cycle assessment with a standardisation request for the Union method. The cybersecurity provisions, which oblige space operators that are NIS-2 entities to follow the NIS-2 implementing act for space and apply equivalent measures to the other operators, and the incident reporting rules are unchanged apart from wording and corrected cross-references.
