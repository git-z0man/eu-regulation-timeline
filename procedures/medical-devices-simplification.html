28 May 2026ST 9114/26Council: progress reportRegulation to simplify rules on medical and in vitro diagnostic devices - Progress report
Our summary: Progress report of the Cyprus Presidency of 28 May 2026 on the proposal to simplify the Medical Devices and In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulations, for Coreper to invite the Council to take note; it is not a Council position. Delegations welcome the aims of the proposal but see further work as necessary before a general approach, notably on the roles of the European Medicines Agency and the Medical Device Coordination Group, expert panels, well-established technologies, the oversight and fees of notified bodies, and the new powers of the Commission to amend definitions and annexes by delegated act. Several delegations support limiting the application of the AI Act to medical devices to avoid overlaps, some want to complement the sectoral powers to lay down AI requirements, and how far cybersecurity requirements of the Cyber Resilience Act should be reflected in the sectoral rules is still open.

30 Jun 2026PE787.987EP draft report (rapporteur)DRAFT REPORT on the proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council amending Regulations (EU) 2017/745 and (EU) 2017/746 as regards …
Our summary: Draft report of 30 June 2026 by the rapporteur of the Committee on Public Health on the proposal to simplify the Medical Devices and In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulations, with 131 amendments; the rapporteur's draft, not the position of the committee or of Parliament, and not law. No amendment touches the proposal's links to the AI Act and the Cyber Resilience Act: the move of both Regulations to Section B of Annex I of the AI Act and the reporting of actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to the CSIRTs and ENISA stay as proposed. Against the Commission text, the draft keeps more safeguards on clinical evidence, liability, implant cards and periodic safety reports, adds an insurance duty and a pathway for niche devices, gives expert panel opinions more weight in classification, limits the reopening of the regulatory status of CE-marked products and sets fixed deadlines for designating and monitoring notified bodies. None of the tracked concepts (high-risk suppliers, standardisation, product framework) is touched.
