3 Mar 2026PE784.432EP draft report (rapporteur)DRAFT REPORT on the proposal for a regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on the safety, resilience and sustainability of space activities in …
Our summary: Draft report of March 2026 by the rapporteur of Parliament's Industry, Research and Energy committee on the EU Space Act proposal, with 677 amendments; it is the rapporteur's text, not the position of the committee or of Parliament, and not law. It deletes the proposal's own cybersecurity and resilience regime for space operators (risk management, cryptography, testing, supply chain, the incident reporting details and the resilience annex) and instead extends the space sector of the NIS 2 Directive to all space activities, space services and Union-owned assets, with its own transposition deadline, so that NIS 2 would govern cybersecurity in the sector; only a reduced duty to report significant incidents and the resilience network stay in the Space Act. It also lightens authorisation (shorter deadlines, tacit approval, simplified regimes for SMEs and research), replaces fixed dates by periods counted from entry into force and deletes the Union Space Label; none of the tracked concepts is touched.

30 Mar 2026ST 7806/26Council: Presidency compromise textPresidency compromise text - clean version
Our summary: Presidency compromise text on the EU Space Act of 30 March 2026, a clean version without change marks prepared for the meeting of 21 April 2026; it is the Presidency's draft under a scrutiny reservation of all delegations, not a Council position and not law, and it can still change. Compared with the clean Presidency text of December 2025, it replaces the authorisation under the Act by an "EUSA certification", adds rules on suspending the certification of Union-owned assets and on providing space-based data in the Union, lets only the certifying authority apply stricter national requirements, and gives harmonised standards a presumption of conformity, with common specifications only in exceptional cases; the provisions on the Union Space Label and on Agency fees are no longer in the text. The cybersecurity provisions, which oblige space operators that are NIS-2 entities to follow the NIS-2 implementing act for space and apply equivalent measures to the other operators, are unchanged apart from wording. Their justification is condensed: the recital on the relationship with NIS-2 is rewritten, the recitals on a bespoke resilience baseline, lifecycle risk management and cooperation under the CER Directive are no longer in the text, and the definition of cyber threat referring to the Cybersecurity Act is deleted.

8 May 2026ST 8861/26Council: progress reportPreparation of the Council (Competitiveness (Internal Market, Industry, Research and Space)) on 28-29 May 2026 Regulation on the safety, resilience and …
Our summary: Progress report of the Cyprus Presidency of 8 May 2026 on the EU Space Act, for the Competitiveness Council of 28-29 May 2026 to take note of; it is not a Council position and changes no text. Building on the work of the Danish Presidency, it records the compromise text of 30 March 2026: new terms (EUSA certification, EUSA registration, entry in the Union Repository of Space Activities), the certification arrangements of national authorities left to Member States, and the environmental footprint declaration, the Union space label and the Agency fees deleted. On cybersecurity the Presidency kept the provisions as they were and only updated the recital, because they still have to be aligned with the incident reporting regime proposed in the Digital Omnibus; scope, dual use, governance and third-country equivalence remain open.
