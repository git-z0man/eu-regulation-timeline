## Recitals

<a id="rec-1"></a>
(1) Reporting requirements play a key role in ensuring the proper monitoring and the correct enforcement of legislation. The overall costs of reporting are balanced by the benefits they bring, in particular in ensuring compliance with key policy measures. However, in order to ensure that the intended purpose of reporting requirements is fulfilled and in order to limit the administrative burden, it is important to streamline those requirements.

<a id="rec-2"></a>
(2) In its communication of 16 March 2023 entitled ‘Long-term competitiveness of the EU: looking beyond 2030’, the Commission has committed to rationalising and simplifying reporting requirements, with the aim of reducing the burdens created by such requirements by 25 %, without undermining the related policy objectives.

<a id="rec-3"></a>
(3) In its Better Regulation Guidelines of 3 November 2021, the Commission promotes the ‘digital by default’ principle to support digital transformations by facilitating digital-ready policies which consider the fast-evolving world of digitalisation and technology, and which are digital, interoperable, future-proof and agile by default.

<a id="rec-4"></a>
(4) The increasing importance of digitalisation in simplifying regulatory frameworks necessitates the reduction, harmonisation and modernisation of reporting requirements and the obligations of economic operators. In line with the efforts to accelerate digitalisation, it is essential to fully digitalise business-to-authority reporting and the obligations of economic operators when those obligations do not affect the protection and safety of consumers or place an excessive burden on economic operators. Embracing digitalisation is expected not only to simplify compliance procedures but also to enhance the overall efficiency of the regulatory framework, ultimately benefiting both businesses and authorities alike. A smooth transition towards digitalisation should be pursued.

<a id="rec-5"></a>
(5) The Commission and the Member States are encouraged to provide guidance and practical support to companies, in particular small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and micro-enterprises, and to promote the development of streamlined and standardised digital tools, with a view to facilitating the digital transition of those companies.

<a id="rec-6"></a>
(6) A number of sectoral Union legal acts lay down harmonised rules regarding the obligations of economic operators when placing a product on the market or putting it into service. Those legal acts include Directives 2000/14/EC^(4), 2011/65/EU^(5), 2013/53/EU^(6), 2014/29/EU^(7), 2014/30/EU^(8), 2014/31/EU^(9), 2014/32/EU^(10), 2014/33/EU^(11), 2014/34/EU^(12), 2014/35/EU^(13), 2014/53/EU^(14), 2014/68/EU^(15) and 2014/90/EU^(16) of the European Parliament and of the Council (the ‘Directives concerned’). Most of the Directives concerned are based on the principles of the ‘new approach’, which is the legislative technique for technical harmonisation, and are ▌ aligned with the reference provisions laid down in Decision No 768/2008/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council^(17).

<a id="rec-7"></a>
(7) In accordance with the Directives concerned, manufacturers are to draw up an EU declaration of conformity stating that the fulfilment of applicable essential requirements has been demonstrated. In order to enable seamless electronic processes, the EU declaration of conformity should be drawn up in electronic form only.

<a id="rec-8"></a>
(8) Directives 2000/14/EC, 2013/53/EU, 2014/32/EU, 2014/33/EU, 2014/34/EU and 2014/53/EU require that a copy of the EU declaration of conformity accompanies a product. Considering the evolution of digitalisation, it is essential that that obligation be modernised by requiring that such an EU declaration of conformity accompany the product in electronic form. The manufacturer should therefore ensure that the EU declaration of conformity is accessible directly through an internet address or a machine-readable code, meaning that intermediate steps, such as the number of landing pages, should be minimised as much as possible. That direct access should be free of charge, without the need to use login procedures, to provide any personal data, or to download or use additional applications specific to the economic operator or the obligation to register solely to access the EU declaration of conformity.

<a id="rec-9"></a>
(9) Taking into account that, in 2024, no less than 94 % of Union households had access to the internet, the availability of product instructions in electronic form offers an opportunity to align with current technologies and to contribute to green objectives. In that regard, the possibility for manufacturers to provide instructions in electronic form should be introduced in the Directives concerned, should they wish to do so. Where manufacturers choose to provide instructions in electronic form, specific safeguards for consumers should be in place to ensure that they, as non-professional users, are still able to access and understand the information, which is in contrast to professional users, who are expected to possess the expertise and knowledge necessary in order to use and handle the products correctly. Therefore, where a product is used solely by professional users, namely persons acquiring the product as professional end-users in the course of their industrial or professional activities, safety information, including instructions relating to the safe use of the product, might be provided in electronic form. In contrast, the safety information should always be provided in paper format or marked on the product where it is reasonably foreseeable that a product, even if primarily intended for professional use, could also be used by consumers. That requirement ensures that all consumers, including vulnerable consumers such as elderly persons, persons with disabilities or those with limited digital literacy, are able to access and understand the safety information. Moreover, end-users should be able to obtain a paper copy of the instructions or safety information, upon request, at the time of the purchase of the product and for a certain period of time after that purchase.

<a id="rec-10"></a>
(10) The concept of ‘consumer’ forms part of the term ‘end user’ as defined in Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 of the European Parliament and of the Council^(18), which applies to the Directives concerned, and refers to any natural person acting for purposes outside their trade, business, craft or profession.

<a id="rec-11"></a>
(11) In order to ensure that all consumers, including those who might not have regular access to digital tools, can effectively exercise their right to obtain the instructions or safety information in paper format, manufacturers should still be allowed to provide additional accessible means of communication for submitting such requests, including by telephone. That is particularly important for elderly consumers and other groups which are at risk of digital exclusion. End-users should also be clearly informed, in the safety information accompanying the product, of their right to request the instructions or safety information in paper format.

<a id="rec-12"></a>
(12) Manufacturers should, where relevant, ensure that instructions and safety information provided in electronic form take into account the requirements set out in Annex I to Directive (EU) 2019/882 of the European Parliament and of the Council^(19), insofar as the products fall within the scope of that Directive, so that all end-users, including persons with disabilities, are able to access, understand and use the safety information and instructions. Other categories of end-users, such as elderly persons or persons with limited digital literacy, might also benefit from those provisions.

<a id="rec-13"></a>
(13) Manufacturers can offer to deliver the instructions in paper format directly to end-users by an alternative means, free of charge and without imposing any obligation on distributors who are not required to print the instructions, unless they choose to do so voluntarily.

<a id="rec-14"></a>
(14) When providing the instructions and safety information in electronic form, the manufacturer should ensure that those instructions are, and that safety information is, accessible directly through an internet address or a machine-readable code, meaning that intermediate steps, such as the number of landing pages, should be minimised as much as possible. That direct access should be free of charge, without the need to use login procedures, to provide any personal data, or to download or use additional applications specific to the economic operator or the obligation to register solely to access the instructions and safety information. That is in line with the overarching vision of digital inclusion, as set out in the Joint Declaration of 23 January 2023 entitled ‘European Declaration on Digital Rights and Principles for the Digital Decade’^(20), in particular Chapter II on Solidarity and inclusion.

<a id="rec-15"></a>
(15) Given the shift towards digital information for consumers, when evaluating the implementation of the Directives concerned, the Commission should also assess, where appropriate, whether the provisions concerning instructions or safety information in electronic form are effectively ensuring that those instructions and that information remains easily accessible and comprehensible for consumers.

<a id="rec-16"></a>
(16) The possibility for the end-user to request the instructions or safety information in paper format should not preclude manufacturers from providing supplementary information materials in an electronic form only, such as video or audio content, which cannot be reproduced in paper format. Where content is provided in paper format and in electronic form, the content should be the same.

<a id="rec-17"></a>
(17) Directive 2014/53/EU provides for the possibility for manufacturers to provide a simplified EU declaration of conformity in an electronic form. Considering that this Directive should introduce a format of the EU declaration of conformity which is digital by default, the provisions on the simplified EU declaration of conformity become redundant. It is therefore necessary to delete those provisions from Directive 2014/53/EU.

<a id="rec-18"></a>
(18) In order to facilitate communication between economic operators and competent national authorities and end-users, providing the details of a digital contact for the manufacturer on the product and in the EU declaration of conformity is necessary to enhance the effectiveness of market surveillance and to expedite the process of tracing non-compliant products. Currently, economic operators are required to indicate their postal address on the product, but this is not always sufficient to ensure that competent national authorities can establish rapid contact. It is therefore necessary to require that economic operators provide both a postal address and a digital contact on the product and in the EU declaration of conformity. Such a digital contact should be defined in the Directives concerned. The digital contact should allow consumers and competent national authorities to contact economic operators directly, and should be up to date and accessible free of charge, without the need to use login procedures, to provide any personal data, or to download or use additional applications specific to the economic operator or the obligation to register solely to contact the economic operator. Such a digital contact can include, for example, an email address or a contact form on a website, or any other direct means of digital communication, without intermediate steps, which allow for traceability of exchanges and should be interpreted in a technologically neutral manner. However, automatic replies to queries, chatbots, fax numbers or telephone lines should not be considered as constituting a digital contact.

<a id="rec-19"></a>
(19) The term ‘digital contact’, similarly to the term ‘electronic address’ in Regulation (EU) 2023/988 of the European Parliament and of the Council^(21), should be interpreted in a technologically neutral manner, one which is capable of evolving with future technological developments, and should cover all forms of direct digital communication.

<a id="rec-20"></a>
(20) The Directives concerned require that economic operators provide, upon a reasoned request from a competent national authority ▌ , all information and documentation, in paper format or electronic form, necessary to demonstrate the conformity of the respective products with the Directives concerned. The paper format is becoming less necessary, while electronic communication enhances interaction between authorities and businesses, streamlining processes and reducing the administrative burden. In order to achieve the digitalisation of reporting requirements and to reduce the administrative burden for economic operators, in particular SMEs, and competent national authorities, economic operators should be required to provide the necessary information and documentation in electronic form only. Information and documentation provided in electronic form should be able to be made available, for example, in a digital format which allows for the possibility to print, download and save the information and documentation on an electronic device.

<a id="rec-21"></a>
(21) Directive 2014/90/EU contains certain sectoral specificities, including the unique requirements for ensuring the safety of marine equipment on board EU ships. Due to those specificities, that Directive differs from other pieces of Union legislation that are aligned to the New Legislative Framework. Specifically, it foresees the obligation for a paper copy of the EU declaration of conformity to be on board an EU ship until the marine equipment is removed. However, given the availability of the European Maritime Safety Agency’s database to Member State flag states and market surveillance authorities, that obligation should be satisfied by uploading a copy of the EU declaration of conformity to that database. That would enable the necessary checks and controls of the marine equipment on the EU ship to be carried out electronically and ensure uniform application and practices under Directive 2014/90/EU.

<a id="rec-22"></a>
(22) The current Union standardisation framework, which is based on Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 of the European Parliament and of the Council^(22), is the default framework by which standards that provide for a presumption of conformity with the relevant essential requirements or with other requirements are developed. However, where harmonised standards do not exist or where they are insufficient, the Commission should be able to adopt implementing acts establishing common specifications for the essential requirements or other requirements, as an exceptional fall-back solution to facilitate the manufacturer’s obligation to comply with those essential requirements or other requirements. Several legislative acts already contain such an option to address the issue of the lack of harmonised standards, thereby providing businesses with legal predictability and allowing them to prove a product’s compliance with Union law. Compliance with common specifications remains purely voluntary for manufacturers, who can rely on those common specifications in order to benefit from a presumption of conformity or who can choose other appropriate methods to demonstrate compliance with Union requirements.

<a id="rec-23"></a>
(23) Where the lack of cooperation by international standardisation organisations prevents European standardisation organisations from adopting relevant international standards as harmonised standards, and where such a situation cannot be adequately mitigated by alternative measures, implementing powers should be conferred on the Commission to adopt, as a measure of last resort, common specifications without making a prior request to one or more European standardisation organisations to draft or to revise harmonised standards. In view of the upcoming revision of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012, it is appropriate to limit the duration of empowerment of the last resort clause to a limited period of time. When the Commission drafts such implementing acts, all relevant information concerning the third parties contracted to support the drafting process of the implementing act is expected to be made public in a timely manner.

▌

<a id="rec-24"></a>
(24) As the digital product passport is foreseen in certain pieces of Union legislation, such as Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 of the European Parliament and of the Council^(23), it is essential to require ▌ economic operators to store the information contained in the declaration of conformity and instructions ▌ in the digital product passport where a product is covered by multiple pieces of legislation. That approach would reduce the administrative burden on manufacturers, as they would no longer need to maintain separate storage locations for compliance documents, such as declarations of conformity required under the various pieces of legislation that can apply to the same product, thereby upholding the principle of a single declaration of conformity. In addition, instructions provided in electronic form should be stored together with the declaration of conformity. By storing the documentation in one place, all necessary documents demonstrating product compliance would be easily accessible, thereby ensuring transparency and facilitating compliance. That streamlined approach would enhance the overall efficiency of the regulatory framework, and it aligns with the principle that, where several pieces of Union legislation apply to a product, the manufacturer or ▌ other economic operator, where appropriate, is to provide a single EU declaration of conformity.

<a id="rec-25"></a>
(25) Since the objectives of this Directive, namely to reduce reporting requirements and to pursue the digital transition, cannot be sufficiently achieved by the Member States as this Directive amends the Directives concerned which harmonise product legislation but can rather, by reason of harmonisation of Union rules applicable to products, be better achieved at Union level, the Union may adopt measures, in accordance with the principle of subsidiarity as set out in Article 5 of the Treaty on European Union. In accordance with the principle of proportionality, as set out in that Article, this Directive does not go beyond what is necessary in order to achieve those objectives.

<a id="rec-26"></a>
(26) To ensure a smooth and effective transition, to minimise disruptions, and to provide a reasonable timeframe for industries to adjust to the new requirements, the application of the transposition measures concerning digitalisation should be deferred.

<a id="rec-27"></a>
(27) In order to enable economic operators to supply any stock of products that have been placed on the market before the date of application of the national measures transposing this Directive concerning digitalisation, it is necessary to provide for reasonable transitional arrangements that do not impede the making available on the market of products that have been placed on the market in accordance with the Directives concerned before the date of application of the national measures transposing this Directive.

<a id="rec-28"></a>
(28) Directives 2000/14/EC, 2011/65/EU, 2013/53/EU, 2014/29/EU, 2014/30/EU, 2014/31/EU, 2014/32/EU, 2014/33/EU, 2014/34/EU, 2014/35/EU, 2014/53/EU, 2014/68/EU and 2014/90/EU should therefore be amended accordingly,

HAVE ADOPTED THIS DIRECTIVE:

### Article 1 – Amendments to Directive 2000/14/EC

Directive 2000/14/EC is amended as follows:

(1) in Article 3, the following point is added:

‘(i) “digital contact” means any online communication channel that is up to date and accessible free of charge, such as an email address, through which an economic operator can be contacted without the need to register or to download or use additional applications specific to the economic operator;’;

(2) in Article 4(1), the third indent is replaced by the following:

‘– the equipment bears CE marking and the indication of the guaranteed sound power level and is accompanied by the internet address or machine-readable code at which or through which the EC declaration of conformity can be accessed directly. ▌’;

(3) in Article 5, paragraph 1 is replaced by the following:

‘1. Member States shall take appropriate measures to ensure that equipment referred to in Article 2(1) may be placed on the market or put into service only if it conforms to the provisions of this Directive, bears the CE marking and the indication of the guaranteed sound power level and is accompanied by the internet address or machine-readable code at which or through which the EC declaration of conformity can be accessed directly. ▌’;

(4) in Article 6, paragraph 1 is replaced by the following:

‘1. Member States shall not prohibit, restrict or impede the placing on the market or putting into service in their territory of equipment referred to in Article 2(1) which complies with the provisions of this Directive, bears the CE marking and the indication of the guaranteed sound power level and is accompanied by the internet address or machine-readable code at which or through which the EC declaration of conformity can be accessed directly. ▌;

(5) Article 7 is replaced by the following:

‘Article 7

Presumption of conformity

Member States shall presume that equipment referred to in Article 2(1) bearing the CE marking and the indication of the guaranteed sound power level and which is accompanied by the internet address or machine-readable code at which or through which the EC declaration of conformity can be accessed directly, conforms to all the provisions of this Directive. ▌’;

(6) Article 8 is amended as follows:

(a) paragraph 1 is replaced by the following:

‘1. The manufacturer, or his authorised representative established in the Community, of equipment referred to in Article 2(1) shall, in order to certify that an item of equipment is in conformity with the provisions of this Directive, draw up an EC declaration of conformity, in electronic form, for each type of equipment manufactured; the minimum content of this declaration of conformity is laid down in Annex II.’;

(b) the following paragraph is added:

‘4. Where another piece of Union legislation applicable to the equipment referred to in Article 2(1) requires that the economic operator include information that the product complies with the requirements set out in that piece of legislation in a digital product passport or requires that the economic operator upload instructions in a digital product passport, the information set out in Annex II to be included in the EC declaration of conformity and the instructions referred to in Article 11(5), where provided in electronic form, shall be provided in that digital product passport.

Where another piece of Union legislation applicable to the equipment referred to in Article 2(1) requires that the economic operator upload the EU declaration of conformity or instructions in a digital product passport, the EC declaration of conformity required by this Article and the instructions referred to in Article 11(5), where provided in electronic form, shall be provided in that digital product passport.’;

(7) Article 14 is amended as follows:

(a) paragraph 3 is replaced by the following:

‘3. Member States shall ensure that the Commission and any other Member State may, on a reasoned request, obtain all information, in electronic form, used during the conformity assessment procedure concerning a type of equipment and in particular the technical documentation provided for in Annex V item 3, Annex VI item 3, Annex VII item 2, and Annex VIII items 3.1 and 3.3.’;

(b) the following paragraph is added:

‘4. Where the manufacturer provides the notified body carrying out the conformity assessment procedure with the information and documentation relating to conformity assessment procedures, all such information and documentation shall be provided in electronic form.’;

(8) Annex II and Annexes V to VIII are amended in accordance with Annex I to this Directive.
