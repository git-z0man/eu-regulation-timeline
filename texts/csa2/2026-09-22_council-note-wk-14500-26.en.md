IE PRES REV1 - Title IV Security of ICT Supply Chains -Explanatory Note

Introduction

The Presidency issued guiding questions in July on Title IV Security of ICT Supply Chains of the CSA2 proposal, which were discussed at the Horizontal Working Party on Cyber Issues on 07 September. The Presidency is grateful for contributions from Member States on this Title. The answers to said questions and previous comments provided by Member States have been used to inform the drafting of a REV 1 text.

The intention of this explanatory note is to provide a high-level summary of the approach the Presidency has taken to the development of this REV1 text based on Member States’ written comments and responses to the guiding questions. We have considered the views provided and endeavoured to accommodate where possible. As always, we welcome further engagement and collaboration with Member States to ensure the further progression of this important file.

Security of ICT Supply Chains

At a high level, there is broad overall support for the necessity for the measures contained in this Title. There was a clear call from Member States to see greater clarity in the articles with regard to their operation and their interoperability. It was also clear Member States sought more involvement in the processes within these articles, either directly, or through the NIS Cooperation Group. As a result, the Presidency has made a number of changes to the structure and detail of this text, particularly Chapter 1, with a view to providing the further clarity sought by Member States and to give Member States greater input into the processes.

Summary of Changes

Title IV Security of ICT Supply Chains

Chapter I: ICT supply chain security framework

While the overall purpose and general measures within this chapter have not significantly changed from the original Commission proposal; this chapter has seen a reorganisation of the articles and the addition of new articles in order to provide clarity on the operation of the framework. In addition, some articles have been renamed to more clearly state their purpose or reflect changes to the text. In order to assist Member States, a flow chart of the articles in this chapter is attached at annex 1 which gives a graphical representation of the text in this section.

Article 99 - Security risk assessments of critical ICT supply chains

This chapter commences with Article 98 setting out the scope of this Title and is followed by Article 99 which sets out the parameters of the security risk assessments required for the operation of this Title.

As previously, Article 99 sets out, when requested, the NIS Cooperation Group may conduct a security risk assessment in accordance with the first paragraph (Article 99(1)) and that, in exceptional circumstances, the Commission may carry out a security risk assessment in accordance with the third paragraph (Article 99(3)). There is a requirement within both paragraphs for the identification of key assets within the critical ICT supply chain undergoing the security risk assessment.

As a result, a structural change within this article is the introduction at paragraph 3a of the elements that should be taken into account by both the NIS Cooperation Group and the Commission when identifying the key assets. This text was taken from Article 102(2) of the original Commission proposal. It has been placed here as it is important to understand the key ICT assets that form part of a critical ICT supply chain, in order to be able to proceed with identifying the risks within that supply chain. As such, providing the criteria at paragraph 3a will assist in the identification of those key ICT assets.

In response to the submissions received from Member States to the guiding questions,

Article 99 now requires the NIS Cooperation Group to develop a common risk assessment methodology and places a similar type of requirement on the Commission with regards to the security risk assessments to be conducted under paragraph 3

Article 101 - General ICT supply chain security mechanism (DELETED)

Article 101 originally set out the general provisions that allow the Commission to take certain measures in this chapter. When considering how it may be possible to offer further clarity in the operation of the articles in this chapter, the Presidency introduced changes to the text in the articles that set out the interactions between the different articles. As a result, there was no longer a need for Article 101 and so it has been deleted.

Article 102 - Designation of key ICT assets

Article 102 has been renamed to “Designation of key ICT assets”. This is in recognition that the key ICT assets are first identified and assessed during the security risk assessments carried out pursuant to Article 99(1) or Article 99(3). Following this identification process, the Commission shall adopt implementing acts pursuant to Article 102(1) designating them as key ICT assets for the purposes of the framework and for the articles to follow. It is worth noting paragraph 2 of the original Commission proposal remains in this article and is a mirror of the new language contained in Article 99(3a). The reason for this is that when adopting the implementing act at paragraph 1 of this article, the Commission must retain its own legal right of initiation as such as to retain the ability to be able to conduct an assessment of the key ICT assets being designated in this article.

Chapter III: Competent authorities, supervision and enforcement, jurisdiction, rights of defence

The changes within this chapter reflect the comments and suggestions from Member States. They primarily reflect the necessity to align the language in this chapter with the language in the NIS2 Directive. A key consideration in this chapter which will require further consideration and discussion centres on the overall scope of this title. At the moment, the scope of this title applies to entities “of the type” in the Annexes to the NIS2 Directive.

While it may be preferrable to retain a level of flexibility in the application of this framework to entities which were not originally captured under the NIS2 Directive, it does pose questions for the ability of NIS2 Competent Authorities to be able to identify and supervise those entities based on the provisions contained in this chapter and in the NIS2 Directive.

A notable change in this chapter is contained in Article 115. Where there was once only a percentage turnover amount for consideration for the penalties, there is now also a stipulated Euro amount. The addition of the Euro amount is at the suggestion of Member States who highlighted that Public Administration Entities do not have a turnover as they do not generate profit. Therefore, under the original text it would not have been possible to apply a penalty to them.

Title VI – Final Provisions

The Presidency has also considered the comments provided on Title VI Final Provisions. In particular, Member States made comments on the committee procedures in Article 118. In light of the changes that have been made throughout the Title IV, particularly in Chapter I, the Presidency has chosen to not make changes to this article at this time until Member States have an opportunity to consider the proposed changes.
