**LIMITE EN** **German Non-Paper on the ECCF (compromise text)**

Germany thanks the former Presidency (CYP) for drafting the compromise text of the European Cybersecurity Certification Framework (ECCF) under the revision of the Cybersecurity Act (CSA 2). Due to the approaching deadline for comments for the compromise text, we see this non paper as an opportunity to share and address further important issues that are still of concern to us for the proper functioning of the ECCF and the internal market. Please look for more details, also into our comments.

**1. Alignment with New Legislative Framework (NLF)**

The New Legislative Framework is an established and well-functioning system on the internal market. The proposed CSA 2 makes use of some elements of the NLF but deviates from the NLF by introducing a more restricted Market Surveillance as well as an additional Authorization.

**Market Surveillance, Art. 88**

The CSA 2 plays an important role in other EU-legislation, especially the CRA, where certification will give presumption of conformity to the essential cybersecurity requirements. However, responsibilities remain unclear regarding which supervisory body takes the lead or the level of involvement of other bodies. We would welcome the alignment of processes, rights, and obligations across the NLF, the CSA 2, and other regulations that reference the CSA 2. While the CSA 2 has a much wider scope than the NLF, which only covers products, it is even more important to ensure the effectiveness of market surveillance.

Therefore, CSA market surveillance should not be divided by borders, but rather **Market Surveillance** **Authorities should be able to monitor certificates that are not only issued, but also made available** **on their respective territory** (hence “market surveillance”).

**Proposed amendment:**

Article 88 para. 6 (b) and 8c):

Deletion of ‘[…] that have been issued in their respective territories[…]’

Deletion of ‘[…]that are established in their respective territories[…]’

In addition, the **lack of (re)financing of supervisory measures in cases of non-compliance**, that the NLF allows, creates an imbalance in the resources available to individual National Cybersecurity Certification Authorities (NCCA). Consequently, robust market surveillance acts as an economic

**Proposed amendments:**

Article 88 new para. 7 g (1) and (2):

‘**1. Member States may authorise their market surveillance authorities to reclaim from the** **relevant economic operator the totality of the costs of their activities with respect to instances** **of non-compliance.**

**2. The costs referred to in paragraph 7 (g) 1 of this Article may include the costs of carrying out** **testing, the costs of storage and the costs of activities relating to products that are found to be** **non-compliant and are subject to corrective action prior to their release for free circulation or** **their placing on the market.**’ disadvantage for a Member State, potentially leading to a preference for certifications in states with less active market surveillance. Such mechanisms in turn weaken the system as a whole.

**Authorization, Art. 92**

We see the need for further alignment between the CSA 2 with the NLF and the CRA with regard to Art. 92 of the current CSA 2 proposal. In the submitted wording the CSA 2 will not achieve harmonization with the CRA, which will lead to interoperability issues for Conformity Assessment Bodies operating under both regulations. Moreover, it will result in market fragmentation. Therefore, we suggest **replacing authorization with notification in the text to ensure alignment with the NLF**.

**Proposed amendment:**

Change term ‘authorisation’ to ‘notification’ in all concerning text passages e.g. Article 92.

In addition, the proposed cross-border authorization model conflicts with the NLF. Under the CSA 2, assessments conducted by an NCCA in another Member States territory encroach upon the sovereign powers of the local NCCA as the competent authority. The **CSA 2 should therefore not establish any** **legal avenue for transferring sovereign powers between NCCAs other than under a bilateral** **agreement**.

**Proposed amendments:**

Article 92 amendment of para. 3 including deletion of para. 3(a), 3(b), 4 and 5.

New para. 3:

‘A conformity assessment body may request ~~authorisation~~ **notification** by a national cybersecurity certification authority other than that referred to in paragraph 2 ~~in any one of the following~~ ~~situations:~~ **~~unless~~** **if a bilateral agreement is in place.**’

**2. Fees**

The introduction of fees for ENISA’s role in maintaining certification schemes on EU level – as intended e.g. in Article 79 1. (b) - would be an **additional burden for the Member States** where already established certification ecosystems exist. As regulated under the CSA 2, when implementing EU certification schemes, the national certification schemes must phase out. With the introduction of fees, the Member States would have to run the certification infrastructure and additionally pay fees to ENISA for the maintenance of EU certification schemes. Moreover, this is also an **additional burden** **to private participants** (certification bodies and conformity assessment bodies) in the ECCF and will likely raise the costs for manufactures and vendors when applying for certification.

**Proposed amendment:**

Deletion of Article 47 and Article 79 para. 2 (b).

**3. Certification of persons**

The proposal for the CSA 2 introduced a novel ‘Skills Attestation Framework’ with a complex description of an attestation system to denote the qualification of persons for certain cybersecurityrelated professions and skills. Particularly given the importance of the existing skills shortage, we **propose a different system for addressing this concern - certification of persons**. The **certification of** **persons** is already **well-established and functioning on national and international level**. There are existing structures as well as participants on the market of certification of persons. Furthermore, already accepted rules for personnel certification (DIN EN ISO/IEC 17024) exist. The ECCF also gives the **opportunity of a self-declaration** – the sensible first step of certification of persons.

The Skills Attestation Framework on the other hand is a framework that is not yet established. The framework would bring parallel and new definitions and additional administrative procedures. This would not just lead to **legal uncertainties**, but also to **inefficient processes** with regard to the verification of skills of personnel as well as a **further fragmentation of the market**.

Looking at ENISA’s scarce resources, a **Skills Attestation Framework under the CSA 2 would tie up** **more of ENISA’s resources which cannot be used for other tasks instead – including certification and** **the further implementation of the ECCF**.

Thus, we propose to **fully integrate the certification of persons into the European Cybersecurity** **Certification Framework**. The certification of persons (including self-declaration) would be developed in the common certification ecosystems using an ad hoc group and the already established certification bodies. This would avoid further duplication and parallel structures, but rather strengthen the ECCF.

**Proposed amendments:**

New para. 2 (d) in Article 71:

‘**(d) that the certification of persons has been evaluated in accordance with such schemes** **complies with specified cybersecurity requirements.**’

Add the term ‘persons’ in all concerning articles mentioning the objectives of the ECCF. For example Article 73 (1):

‘The Commission may request ENISA to prepare a candidate European cybersecurity certification scheme for ICT products, ICT services, ICT processes, managed security services or cyber posture of entities **or persons**.’

**4. Certification of service providers**

We also propose to extend the ECCF to the certification of service provider. This addition would lead to scalable effects on the certification of services and an existing structure for the certification of service providers is already in place.

**Proposed amendment:**

Add the term ‘ICT-service provider’ in all concerning articles mentioning the objectives of the ECCF. For example Article 73 (1):

‘The Commission may request ENISA to prepare a candidate European cybersecurity certification scheme for ICT products, ICT services, **ICT service providers**, ICT processes, managed security services or cyber posture of entities **or persons**.’

**5. Security objectives of European cybersecurity certification schemes**

Art. 80 defines the security objectives of European cybersecurity certification schemes and contains an extensive list of objectives, including redundancies. These objectives have their basis in different regulations, such as the CRA. As the aim of the CSA 2 is a harmonization of existing EU regulation, we urge that the **security objectives will be further aligned between CRA and the CSA 2**.

Furthermore, we emphasize that **updating the security objectives should be done through** **Implementing Acts**. Since a lot of expertise in this field lies with the Member States and security objectives touch upon national security, we believe that Member States should be closely involved in the process, which can only be ensured through implementing acts.

**Proposed amendment:**

Amendment of Article 80 (2):

‘The Commission is empowered to adopt ~~delegated~~ **implementing** acts ~~in accordance with Article~~ ~~119~~ to amend paragraph 1 of this Article by adding or modifying security objectives in order to ensure that they reflect the latest technological development and new related threats ~~as well as~~ ~~adoption of new Union legislation setting out the demonstration of compliance and the~~ ~~presumption of conformity through European cybersecurity certification with relevant~~ ~~cybersecurity requirements of that legislation.~~ When preparing ~~delegated~~ **implementing** acts updating security objectives, the Commission shall consult ENISA **and the ECCG**. **Those** **implementing acts shall be adopted in accordance with the examination procedure referred to** **in Article 118(2).**’
