**_____________________** ++**BELGIUM**++ BELGIQUE/BELGIË

++**1/ GDPR**++

Belgium welcomes a number of improvements introduced in the latest compromise text. In particular, Belgium highly appreciates the revisions made to **Article 9(2)(k) and paragraph 5,** which help restore a more appropriate balance between simplification objectives and the protection of fundamental rights. Belgium remains open to include **further safeguards** in this area, in line with suggestions made by several Member States such as the Netherlands.

With regard to **pseudonymisation and Article 25a**, Belgium recognises several positive developments. In particular, Belgium welcomes the deletion of the reference to the likelihood of identification in paragraph 3, the removal of the distinction between the controller and other persons in paragraph 5, and the replacement of the term “person” with “third party” in paragraph 2. These changes contribute to a clearer and more coherent framework.

Notwithstanding these improvements, Belgium share the concerns expressed by several delegations regarding the possible creation of an **intermediate status** between personal data and anonymous data. Belgium also considers that further clarification is needed as to when and under which conditions controllers would be expected to assess whether processing activities fall back within the scope of the GDPR. This to increase legal certainty.

Furthermore, Belgium proposes:

- • **Paragraph 2**: A clarification of the notion of “third party”. Belgium supports the proposal of the Netherlands in this view.

- • **Paragraph 3**: To include the controller in the cope « Where a third party ++**or the controller**++ discloses, transmits or otherwise makes such data available to another third party and, (…)».

- • **Paragraph 4**: To have a more consistent wording “Where a processor processes personal data on behalf of a controller, that information **data** shall be considered personal data for the processor, and the processor continues to be subject to the obligations applicable under this Regulation.”

Regarding **article 88bis**, Belgium supports calls for the inclusion of additional safeguards. Artificial Intelligence, and its training or processing, cannot be perceived as a legitimate interest in itself.

Regarding **Article 13**, Belgium believes that the current proposal weakens the right to information and leaves excessive room for interpretation by controllers. An absolute minimum would be to bring clarifications in a recital on when efforts could be seen as being disproportionate, in order to enhance legal certainty and ensure a harmonised interpretation across Member States. Our clear preference is returning to the compromise text under the former CY Presidency or the Commission proposal.

++**2/ ePrivacy**++ Belgium would appreciate if the Presidency could build in more certainty that the technical conditions for the exception on audience measurement will be controllable. Belgium supports the balance you found in 5(3). The cookie fatigue should be properly addressed. Belgium notes that it still regrets the move away from GDPR to ePrivacy.

++**3/ Data Act**++ Please find below 1 political element, followed by 3 technical corrections and one question coming from experts.

++**3.1/ Political element**++ Belgium reiterates its position on **trade secrets** and insists on the need to ensure an appropriate balance between protection of trade secrets and data sharing. Belgium wishes to reintroduce the case-by-case assessment and strongly urges not to widen the window to refuse access any further.

++**3.2/ Technical corrections**++

- • **Concerning article 10.3 regarding Amendments, repeals and transitory clauses** Belgium welcomed the increased time period, proposed by the previous compromise text, for Member States to take and prepare all necessary measures and legislation to be in conformity with the text of the new Data Act of the proposed article 32i.10 (Chapter VIIc Re-use of data and documents held by public sector bodies).

++**However**++ article 10.3 regarding Amendments, repeals and transitory clauses, should also be modified accordingly.

Currently article 10.3 still reads: 3. Chapter I and II of Regulation (EU) 2022/868 and Directive 2019/1024 are repealed [**18 months** after the publication in the Official Journal of the European Union]. The “18 months” should be reworded and read “**24 months**” as well, following the proposed modification of article 32i of the proposed Data Act.

- • **Concerning article 32la (4)**

There seems to be a comma missing between the “1” and the “2” in the first sentence of this paragraph (which makes “12”, which is obviously and error). It should probably be “Any natural or legal person directly affected by a decision as referred to in ++**paragraphs 1, 2 or 3**++ shall have an effective right of redress” instead of “Any natural or legal person directly affected by a decision as referred to in ++**paragraph 12 or 3**++ shall have an effective right of redress”.

- • **Concerning the heading of Section 2 Re-use of public sector open data**

Should the heading or title of section 2 not be “Section 2 Re-use of public sector open data ++**or**++ ++**documents**++” since it describes the general regime for open and non-confidential public sector data or documents?

Similarily regarding the heading of Article 32n – “General principle for re-use of public sector open data”: It seems that in the heading or ‘title’ of the proposed article 32n of subsection 1 (of section 2) it should be “Article 32n General principle for re-use of public sector open data ++**or documents**++” since it describes the general principles both valid for section 2 (data and/or documents) and section 3 (certain categories of protected data (not documents).

Same goes for Article 32q “Principles governing charging for public sector open data”. Should it not be “Principles governing charging for public sector open data ++**or documents**++” since the article applies both to ++**data and documents**++?

++**3.3/ Question**++ **Concerning the modifications in the** ++**proposed article 32la.2a**++

Article 32la, (2), (b) sets the time period for the decision on the request for re-use of certain categories of protected data. Processing requests for re-use consists of the adoption of a decision on the request on the one side, and, if positive, on the provision of or granting access to that data on the other side. Especially for this second phase public sector bodies need a reasonable period of time for properly and safely delivering of and/or providing access to protected data to be able to be in full conformity with the proposed art. 32w).

In light of the previous, **Belgium would like to receive clarifications** from the Presidency regarding the proposed removal of the last phrase of the version of article 32la, (2), (b) as proposed by the previous version of the compromise text: “The time limit for granting access may be extended within reasonable limits.”.

It seems also important to point out that **No time limit** has currently been fixed for the **delivery of** **the protected data** (or granting access to protected data) under the DGA. If the Presidency insists on the removal of this phrase in the proposed article 32la, (2), (b),

- - should it then be understood that within the time limit of two months of receipt, a decision on the request for re-use of certain categories of protected data should be adopted AND if that decision is positive, access should be granted to the data ALSO within that same period of two months ? (all the while in accordance with the modalities referred to in Article 32w, which seems unlikely to be practicable and realistic considering the time that these types of measures take) ?

- - does the “where possible” at the end of this phrase mean that, if it is not possible to adopt a decision and grant access within those two months, that the time limit for both the adoption of the decision and for providing access to the data can be extended by with another 30 days?

- - **Or** should it be understood that there is **no time limit for granting access to protected** **data**? As is currently the case under the DGA.

++**CZ comments on Digital Omnibus afte the AGS meeting on 25 September**++ Bellow, please find CZ substantive comments and drafting suggestions on Digital Omnibus (document ST 13112/26). Red lines are highlighted.

Data Act

Very large enterprise – Recital 25

**Drafting Suggestion:** “… ++**~~Member States may further specify the criteria for what constitutes a~~**++ ++**~~very large enterprise for the purpose of Regulation (EU) 2023/2854~~**++ ….”

**CZ Comment:** CZ remains concerned about allowing individual Member States to further specify the criteria for what constitutes a “very large enterprise” for the purposes of the Data Act. Such an approach could lead to divergent approaches across Member States and create additional uncertainty for businesses operating across the internal market. **CZ would therefore prefer to** **delete this sentence** and maintain a common EU-level approach. Alternatively, CZ would welcome further justification for this change. Trade secrets – Articles 4(8) and 5(11)

**CZ Comment:** CZ supports the careful balance between access to data and the protection of trade secrets established by the Data Act. We recognise that trade secrets may constitute essential and highly sensitive information and that they need to be adequately protected through an effective hand-brake mechanism.

Our preference is for the trade-secret safeguard to remain proportionate, justified and applied on a case-by-case basis, and to be used only where genuinely necessary. We are cautious about changes that could shift the balance established by the Data Act.

CZ preference would be wording found in the compromise from June 2026, however **CZ can** **support the current Presidency text in Articles 4(8) and 5(11).** In particular, we welcome that the text maintains the requirements for a objective justification, demonstration of a specific risk and notification obligation. Commission guidelines – third-country government requests - Articles 4(8) and 5(11)

**Drafting suggestion:** “…The Commission shall issue guidelines, in consultation with the European Data Innovation Board (EDIB), on the application of this paragraph, such as the assessment of serious economic damage, the enforceability of trade secrets protection in third countries, ++**and on**++ ++**the assessment of the conditions applicable to requests by third-country governments for access**++ ++**to non-personal data under this Regulation**++.”

**CZ Comment:** CZ welcomes the Commission guidelines on the assessment of risks relating to the protection of trade secrets in third countries. At the same time, CZ considers it useful for the guidelines to also **address the assessment of the conditions applicable to requests by third-** **country governments** for access to non-personal data under the Data Act. This would contribute to a more consistent interpretation and application of the relevant safeguards.

Template for data requests – Article 17(6)

**CZ Comment:** CZ **disagrees with the deletion of former Article 17(6),** which provides for a Commission-developed model template for data requests under Chapter V. A common template would contribute to harmonising procedures across Member States, reduce the administrative burden for businesses and enhance legal certainty. Different formats and requirements for data requests could otherwise create unnecessary administrative burden and uncertainty for businesses.

GDPR

Scientific research:

Regulation of scientific research should respect inherently uncertain nature of scientific efforts, including the role of enterprises large and small. In certain areas, EU is barely keeping pace with most advanced countries. We should better err on the side of dynamism than caution. Thus, CZ proposes:

**Recital 28** – to delete the words “**and adherence to the principles of transparency, reliability,** **accountability, oversight, verifiability,**“, because too many qualifiers would in practice undermine streamlined treatment of research in GDPR. It is strange enough that data protection authorities would consider “the purpose of the research” as if that were relevant for its scientific nature.

**Article 4(38) (RED LINE)** – to add sentence at the end: **This does not exclude that the research** **may also aim to further a commercial interest.** That is important to balance the words “in an autonomous and independent manner” in the preceding sentence – as long as those are kept. From the perspective of AI, research, development and innovation, it is important that the classification of an activity as scientific research should not, in practice, depend on its non-commercial nature or on the institutional status of the entity carrying it out.

Special categories of data ++**(RED LINE)**++

CZ is wary of terms with unforeseen or serious but vague consequences such as “manifestly disproportionate effort” or “strictly necessary”. However, in the spirit of compromise, **CZ could** **accept “manifestly disproportionate effort” in Article 9(5)** if the word **“strictly” is not included** **in Article 9(2)(l)** on biometric verification, as there is no reason for such vague and serious limitation given that verification is under sole control of data subject.

In article 9(5), CZ warns about the last sentence, which at the same time allows and prohibits (“being further processed”) even storage or any internal usage of residual sensitive data. CZ supports the aim of DE proposal for a new sentence to allow such AI systems and models to run after all: **Where, despite the implementation of measures referred to in sentence 1, the** **controller identifies remnants of special categories of data residually retained in the AI system** **or AI model, the controller shall prevent such data from being disclosed or otherwise made** **available to third parties.**

Data subject rights

CZ would fully support the Presidency proposal of **Article 12(5) if limitation to “requests under** **Article 15” was deleted**. It is quite possible to issue other requests with abusive intention (e.g. for deletion of data).

Pseudonymisation **CZ supports the direction of Article 25a** in general and is flexible to minor adjustments and specifications. However, we believe that **implementing act pursuant to Article 93(2) should be** **used**, because rules of such significance should be adopted in a binding, formal and predictable way. Mere opinion of EDPB is not acceptable and will be repeatedly tested in courts and difficult to enforce. Standard comitology is much preferred.

AI ++**(RED LINE)**++

The Presidency has a difficult task that should have been solved by AI Act – to regulate usage of personal data in AI systems and models. CZ, considering AI both opportunity and challenge, is therefore quite flexible in finding a way to providing such basic regulation.

**Article 88bis** – CZ supports it strongly, if only to establish basic level of legal certainty. But CZ is open to clarifying, here or in a recital, that Article 6(1)(a)(c) or (e) could also apply. CZ is also open to clarifying, here or in a recital, that **processing of personal data for the training and technical** **operation of an AI system should be deemed to be a legitimate interest,** without prejudice to the balance of interests, of course. Given the special nature of training of AI system, CZ is also open to **adding more safeguards in Article 88bis(3) and referring to this Article in Art. 5(1)(b) with** **favourable treatment of compatibility presumption for AI training**.

Tax cooperation

**Recital 40b** – CZ is flexible as regards various proposals (ES, FR, NL) as long as the recital refers not only to repetitive nature, but also to **systematic and automated** nature of data transfers.

**CZ Comment:** The narrow exemptions of Article 5 of the ePrivacy Directive (2002/58/EC) creates significant difficulties for vehicle manufacturers seeking to transfer data out of vehicles; any time the onboard telematics unit or sensors send data externally, it may qualify as “accessing/storing information” under Article 5(3). This challenge is compounded by the fact that a vehicle is rarely used by a single individual; it may be driven by the owner, a family member, an employee, or a short-term renter, with passengers also potentially affected. Road and vehicle safety may require continuous or immediate access to vehicle data to detect hazards, prevent accidents and address vulnerabilities. Obtaining consent from successive users could delay these necessary activities. Any exemption should therefore be strictly limited to what is necessary for these purposes and should not permit unrelated uses of the information. In this regard, and given the importance of automotive sector for the EU, including the R&D based in the EU**, we support explicit, narrowly defined** **exemption for access to or storage of information that is strictly necessary to improve road or** **transport safety or to maintain, improve or restore the safety of terminal equipment,** **including in vehicles.**

++**GDPR**++

1. Recital 40b - proposed amendment: delete Recital 40b.

We maintain our previous comment to delete the recital.

Article 49 GDPR provides for derogations from the general mechanisms for transfers of personal data to third countries laid down in Articles 45 and 46 GDPR and should retain its exceptional character. Stating in Recital 40b that transfers based on Article 49(1) may be repetitive and, in particular, systematic would, in practice, lead to those derogations being used as a regular mechanism for transfers of personal data to third countries.

If there is a specific need to establish a legal basis for regular and repetitive transfers of personal data between public authorities, including tax administrations, pursuant to bilateral or multilateral international agreements, such a solution should be considered as part of a separate legislative initiative. This would allow for a proper assessment of its legal implications, its impact on the system of transfers of personal data to third countries and the necessary data protection safeguards, rather than addressing this issue through an interpretation that extends the scope of the derogations provided for in Article 49 GDPR.

2. Article 25a(2) - ensuring the security of pseudonymised data.

We maintain our previous comment concerning the need to ensure the security of pseudonymised data in the situation covered by Article 25a(2).

Pseudonymisation is a measure intended to reduce the risks associated with the processing of personal data and to enhance their security. However, the proposed Article 25a(2) may lead to a situation where, once pseudonymised data are transferred to a third party that is unable to identify the data subject, this data is no longer considered personal data in relation to that third party and, consequently, the security obligations arising from Article 32 GDPR no longer apply to that party.

This may create a security gap. The loss, theft or unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, such data may result in the data coming into the possession of an entity that has the means to identify the natural person. The mere fact that the third party itself is unable to identify the data subject therefore does not mean that the absence of appropriate safeguards on the part of that third party cannot create risks for the persons whose data have been pseudonymised.

For this reason, we consider that an appropriate obligation to ensure the security of such information should continue to apply also in the situation covered by Article 25a(2). The simplest solution would be to maintain the application of Article 32 GDPR. This would not entail establishing the same level of security in all cases, since Article 32 is based on a risk-based approach and takes into account the circumstances of the particular processing.

**In order to address this concern, we propose supplementing Article 25a(2) with the following** **sentence:**

**„Notwithstanding the first subparagraph, the third party shall apply Article 32 to such** **information.”**

3. Article 25a(5) - means enabling identification available to other entities.

We also propose clarifying Article 25a(5) as regards the means that should be taken into account when determining whether a natural person is identifiable.

This determination should not be limited to the means formally available to the controller or another person referred to in Article 25a(5). It should also take into account means available to other entities where, due to existing organisational, contractual or other relationships, the use of those means to identify the natural person is reasonably likely.

Such clarification is important in order to prevent circumvention of the GDPR through the creation of artificial organisational or contractual arrangements designed to separate the entity carrying out the processing operations from the entity holding additional information or other means enabling identification. In practice, this could result in the artificial creation of conditions in which identification is not possible at the level of a particular entity, solely for the purpose of benefiting from the solution provided for in Article 25a(2).

In particular, the proposed provision should not enable separate entities to be established or used for the purpose of carrying out, including on a large scale, operations on pseudonymised data outside the scope of the GDPR by formally separating the entity carrying out the processing from the entity holding the means enabling identification.

At the same time, the proposed clarification does not mean that means available to any other entity should be taken into account when determining whether a natural person is identifiable. The decisive criterion would remain whether the use of those means is reasonably likely. The existence of relevant organisational, contractual or other relationships would constitute a factor in determining whether, in the particular circumstances, the use of means available to another entity is reasonably likely.

**In order to reflect this approach explicitly, we propose supplementing Article 25a(5) with the** **following sentence:**

**„Account shall also be taken of means available to other entities where, due to organisational,** **contractual or other relationships between those entities and the controller or another person,** **the use of such means to identify the natural person is reasonably likely.”**

Finland can mostly support the Presidency’s compromise proposal although few amendments and clarifications are proposed in relation to the GDPR and ePrivacy rules.

1. GDPR provisions

++**Article 3(10) / Application of pseudonymisation and identification of a natural person [GDPR**++ ++**Article 25a]:**++

Finland thanks the Presidency for its efforts in finding a balanced solution to bring legal clarity concerning pseudonymisation and anonymisation. **In general, Finland supports the proposal for** **Article 25a and the corresponding recitals but provides few proposals to further clarify the** **text.**

    - • Finland prefers the wording "third party", as it is already used in the GDPR (it also affects the Finnish language version].

    - • In para 3 Finland finds it important to clarify that the GDPR remains to apply to controllers. Finland welcomes that this is clarified in recital 27b. In addition, it is important to replace wording "such data" with "pseudonymised data" in para 3. These clarifications would be in line with the CJEU SRB Judgement [See C-413/23 P, para 83].

**Para 3 could be amended in a following way:** Where ++**a controller or**++ a third party discloses, transmits or otherwise makes ++**pseudonymised**++ data available to another third party and, in light of all relevant circumstances, that other third party possesses or can obtain means reasonably likely to enable the data subject to be identified, both the transmission of the data to that other third party and the subsequent processing of the data by that other third party is processing of personal data.

    - • In recital 27b, Finland has two remarks: o 1) Finland welcomes that the Presidency has removed from the article the wording "where the likelihood of identification is insignificant in practice" but ponders whether **the wording “if the likelihood of identification appears in reality to be** **insignificant” should be deleted from the recital text** altogether**.** However, FI can remain flexible, as the recital text provides further clarification on what is meant by "the likelihood of identification" and because the proposed wording reflects more to the CJEU wording [see C-413/23 P (SRB), para 82]. o 2) Finland finds it important to evaluate all relevant circumstances and welcomes the Presidency to clarify that the evaluation should be made from a particular entity's perspective. This would respect the CJEU case law and the recent EDPB guidelines.

**Recital 27b the following sentence could be amended in a following way:** Whether means are reasonably likely to be used will depend on ++**all**++ relevant circumstances, which include++**, from a particular perspective of the entity,**++ the specific nature and context of the processing, such as who has access to or control over the data, whether the data is being transmitted from one party to another, the relationship between those parties, and whether a receiver of the data might process it on behalf of another party.

++**Article 3(15) / Processing in the context of the development and operation of AI [GDPR**++ ++**Article 88bis]**++ Finland can support paras 1 and 2 in Article 88bis as well as recital 33a. **However, Finland finds it** **important to add additional safeguards in para 3.** Finland proposes to reinstate the original safeguards of the Commission’s proposal. **Especially** ++**unconditional right to object**++ **as well as** **protection against the non-disclosure of residually retained data are needed to ensure** **adequate level of protection.**

**Para 3 could be amended in the following way:**

- (3) Regardless of the lawful ground of processing pursuant to Article 6, processing in the context of the development and operation of AI shall be subject to appropriate technical and organisational measures and safeguards ++**to ensure respect for the principle of data minimisation during the**++ ++**selection of data sources and the training and testing of an AI system or AI model, to protect**++ ++**against non-disclosure of residually retained data in the AI system or AI model, to ensure**++ ++**enhanced transparency to data subjects and to provide data subjects with an unconditional right**++ ++**to object to the processing of their personal data.**++

**Furthermore, Finland welcomes the Presidency to clarify in Article 88(5) of the GDPR that** **the infringements of Article 88bis, especially the additional safeguards, shall be subject to** **administrative fines**. This is also what the EDPS-EDPS JO recommended. It is important that there is no room for possible litigation.

++**Other provisions:**++

++**Recital 40b:**++ As already commented, Finland does not support recital 40b as it goes against the nature of Article 49 and recital 111 of the GDPR. However, Finland can show flexibility to find a wording that would be in line with the GDPR (for instance Finland could support the proposal by Netherlands in Antici 25th September).

++**Article 3(3) / Derogations to process special categories of personal data [GDPR Article 9(2)(k)**++ ++**and 9(5):**++ Finland welcomes that the Presidency has reinstated wording "incidental" and "manifestly" These are important to keep.

++**Article 3(5) / Information to be provided where personal data are collected from the data**++ ++**subject [GDPR Article 13(5)]:**++ Finland welcomes that the Presidency has reinstated the logic of high-risk processing in the paragraph. It is important to refer to Article 35. FI would still welcome mentioning automated decision-making in para 5.

++**Article 97(2)(c) GDPR / Low-risk processing and exemptions from the requirements of the**++ ++**GDPR:**++ In general, Finland supports the efforts to reduce unnecessary administrative burden especially for SMEs and small midcaps and to provide further clarity on the risk-based nature of the GDPR. Finland welcomes that the Presidency is mindful of introducing significant further amendments to the GDPR in the absence of a comprehensive impact assessment. Finland considers that this is a matter which the Commission could further evaluate. Finland does not support continuing the discussions on “low risk controllers” during Digiomnibus negotiations.

**Finland could show flexibility towards** ++**a neutral amendment**++ **to Article 97(2)(c) of the GDPR,** **but prefers this matter to be addressed in the Digital Fitness Check and/or the next GDPR-** **evaluation.** Regarding a possible Commission evaluation, Finland points out that “low risk processing” should be evaluated from the perspective of the data subject. The severity of the risk cannot be directly assessed in relation to the size of the undertaking or the other nature of its operations. Possible exemptions to the core GDPR requirements should be analyzed thoroughly to ensure that the GDPR remains to provide harmonized rules for data protection in the EU. Finally, Finland points out that these types of amendments could also have implications for adequacy decisions concerning third countries as well as the requirements stemming from Convention 108+.

2. **E-privacy provisions**

Finland thanks the Presidency for considering several Finland’s previous comments. Finland can generally support the approach of the compromise proposal but wishes to raise points on centralised and automated consent and safeguarding the vitality of the media sector and request few clarifications. While none of these points are red lines for us, we may wish to revisit them during the trilogues should our drafting suggestions not be reflected in the text at this stage.

  - • **Finland still sees deleting Article 88b as a missed opportunity and would be willing to** **discuss this matter, especially now that additional time is available to identify the best** **results.**

  - • **Additionally, further consideration should be given to safeguarding the vitality of the** **media sector.** We reiterate our previous proposal clarify the text regarding the single click requirement and the time limits for new consent requests.

++**Recital 44g / Further processing of personal data and the interplay with the GDPR:**++

We are still unsure about the compatibility of the amended article 5(3) together with the recital 44g.

  - • According to the proposed new Art 5(3) storage and access as well as subsequent data processing is allowed only when solely related and strictly necessary for the listed purposes.

  - • In addition, in recitals 44d, 44e and 44f it is explained that the data used for audience measurement or measurement of the performance of contextual advertisement cannot be further processed for another purposes.

  - • However, recital 44g still states, as a general rule, that for the further processing of personal data for other purposes than those defined in the limitative list GDPR Article 6 applies.

  - • **Any clarifications on the intended interpretation would be appreciated.**

++**Article 5(2) & recital 45 / Single click requirement and time limits for new consent requests**++ ++**(ePrivacy Article 5(3)**++

We reiterate our proposal to clarify the text of recital 45 regarding the single click requirement and the time limits for new consent requests, particularly to support the operating conditions of the media.

Drafting suggestion:

- (45) ++**Dark patterns in cookie banners are problematic as they manipulate users into making**++ ++**choices that are not genuinely voluntary or informed. To tackle the use of problematic consent**++ ++**requests, the subscriber or user shall be able to refuse requests in a user-friendly manner.**++ ++**However, this does not preclude the possibility of the service provider to inform the user of**++ ++**the use of cookies.**++ Subscribers or users that have refused a request for consent are often confronted with a new request to give consent each time they visit the same provider’s online service again. This may have detrimental effects to the subscribers or users which may consent just in order to avoid repeating requests. The provider should therefore be obliged to respect the subscriber or users' choices to refuse a request for consent for at least a certain period. This obligation is applicable to any provider that accesses or stores information in the terminal equipment of a subscriber or user, including so-called third-party cookie. ++**However, the controller may request consent within the**++ ++**period if the development of the service, a change in the purpose of processing, or another**++ ++**comparable and justified reason makes a renewed request necessary. The directive does not**++ ++**require the service provider to attempt to identify the user for the whole period if the cookie**++ ++**or other technology is no longer valid for reasons beyond the**++ service provider’s control, such as when the user has deleted the cookies or similar identifiers.

To safeguard the operating conditions of journalistic media in the digital age, in particular, Finland can be flexible regarding the period during which the provider shall not make a new request for consent for the same purpose (last paragraph of Article 5(3)), provided that the period is not so short as to deprive the provision of any practical effect for the user.

++**Article 5(2) & recital 44c / Service explicitly requested by user or subscriber [ePrivacy Article**++ ++**5(3)(b)]:**++

Finland considers that the definition of “functionality of a service” leaves room for interpretation. We suggest that recital 44c be amended to explicitly state that functionalities necessary for the service requested by the user or subscriber cannot include personalised advertising or related profiling

++**Article 5(2) & recitals 44d & 44e / Audience measurement [ePrivacy Article 5(3)(c) & (d)]:**++

We consider it important to retain a time-related requirement for pseudonymisation/anonymisation in para 5(3)(d) on audience measurement in compliance with EMFA and para 5(3)(c) on audience measurement.

As the requirement of anonymisation has been changed to requirement of pseudonymisation in para 5(3)(d), we see that the requirement for immediate action could be returned and the text would still be aligned with EMFA. Drafting suggestion:

Thus, measuring audiences of media services or of content on online platforms by collecting data about the number and characteristics of users of media services or users of content on online platforms should be possible without consent, provided that data collected are not processed for another purpose, are ++**immediately**++ pseudonymised and that audience measurement reports generated do not contain personal data’ Similarly, now that the audience measurement has been divided into two paragraphs, the requirement of immediate action could be returned to para 5(3)(c).

++**Article 5(2) & recitals 44f / Maintaining or restoring the security of a service [ePrivacy Article**++ ++**5(3)(e)]:**++

The recital still has the term “information society service” which was deleted from the corresponding article. Additionally, the recital gives the impression that security updates are the only way to maintain or restore the security of a service. Maintaining or restoring the security of a service can also be conducted through detecting and preventing security threats.

- (44e) Maintaining or restoring the security of a service ~~provided by an information society service~~ ~~provider and~~ requested by the subscriber or user, or the terminal equipment used for the provision of such service, should only be allowed without consent to the extent that ++**the security measures**++ ++**such as**++ security updates are strictly necessary, and proportionate, [---]

3. **Data Act provisions**

Finland appreciates the Presidency’s engagement and efforts to find a balanced approach regarding the protection of trade secrets. We can accept the proposed articles 4(8) and 5(11). However, it is important that the change in the burden of proof is not understood as altering the overall balance of the Regulation.

4. **Cyber provisions**

In general, Finland can support the Presidency’s compromise proposal on amendments to NIS 2 directive on incident reporting and related provisions. Finland thanks the Presidency for avoiding unnecessary administrative burden and creating basis for further incident notification harmonization in the future.

Finland notes, however, that the Article 11 (3) on transitional provisions should be updated to reflect the revised wording of Article 23b of the NIS2 Directive and nature of the national entry point for incident reporting.

*** ++**SWEDEN**++ SVERIGE

SE written comments September 29th – Omnibus VII Digital

Regarding art 33 and 35 GDPR

  - - SE thanks the presidency for their work with this article and would like to underline the need to uphold the compromise text as it stands. To SE, it is important that the COM is given a margin of discretion to review the proposals submitted by the EDPB before adopting them by way of an implementing act in accordance with Article 93(2). SE therefore welcomes the amendments made by the Presidency in this regard.

Regarding art 88bis ePrivacy

  - - Not a red line, but while SE is in favour of the ambition to bring greater legal certainty to the development and use of AI, SE considers that the proposed provision would be suitable in a recital, with regards to its informative structure and content.

  - - Not a red line but with a view to the upcoming continued process of trilogues regarding this proposal. Notwithstanding SE position on the overall placement of the said provision, SE is positive to the efforts made to clarify the applicability of other legal grounds for the processing of AI systems. SE recalls that it is important that any proposed clarification in this regard is not interpreted in a way which may alter or affect the possibilities available to the public sector to process data for the said purpose.

Regarding art 33 GDPR in relation to NIS2 and the Single Entry Point

  - - SE would like to emphasise the need for consistency between the wordings in art 23 NIS2 and art 15 CER, and where applicable, equivalent texts in sector specific acts.

Regarding article 25a.2

  - - SE would consider it beneficial to continue the discussions in this regard in order to fully grasp the material scope of the provision and whether the proposed amendment could risk limiting the scope.
