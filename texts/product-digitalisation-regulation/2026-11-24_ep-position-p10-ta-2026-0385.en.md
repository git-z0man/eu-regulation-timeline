## Recitals

<a id="rec-1"></a>
(1) Reporting requirements play a key role in ensuring the proper monitoring and the correct enforcement of legislation. The overall costs of reporting are balanced by the benefits they bring, in particular in ensuring compliance with key policy measures. However, in order to ensure that the intended purpose of reporting requirements is fulfilled and in order to limit the administrative burden, it is important to streamline those requirements.

<a id="rec-2"></a>
(2) In its communication of 16 March 2023 entitled ‘Long-term competitiveness of the EU: looking beyond 2030’, the Commission has committed to rationalising and simplifying reporting requirements, with the aim of reducing the burdens created by such requirements by 25 %, without undermining the related policy objectives.

<a id="rec-3"></a>
(3) In its Better Regulation Guidelines of 3 November 2021, the Commission promotes the ‘digital by default’ principle to support digital transformations, by facilitating digital-ready policies which consider the fast-evolving world of digitalisation and technology, and which are digital, interoperable, future-proof and agile by default.

<a id="rec-4"></a>
(4) The increasing importance of digitalisation in simplifying regulatory frameworks necessitates the reduction, harmonisation and modernisation of reporting requirements and the obligations of economic operators. In line with the efforts to accelerate digitalisation, it is essential to fully digitalise business-to-authority reporting and the obligations of economic operators when those obligations do not affect the protection and safety of consumers or place an excessive burden on economic operators. Embracing digitalisation is expected not only to simplify compliance procedures but also to enhance the overall efficiency of the regulatory framework, ultimately benefiting both businesses and authorities alike. A smooth transition towards digitalisation should be pursued.

<a id="rec-5"></a>
(5) The Commission and the Member States are encouraged to provide guidance and practical support to companies, in particular small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) and micro-enterprises, and to promote the development of streamlined and standardised digital tools, with a view to facilitating the digital transition of those companies.

<a id="rec-6"></a>
(6) A number of sectoral Union legal acts lay down harmonised rules regarding the obligations of economic operators when placing a product on the market or putting it into service. Those legal acts include Regulations (EU) 2016/424^(4), (EU) 2016/425^(5), (EU) 2016/426^(6), (EU) 2023/1230^(7), (EU) 2023/1542^(8) and (EU) 2024/1781^(9) of the European Parliament and of the Council (the ‘Regulations concerned’). The Regulations concerned are based on the principles of the ‘new approach’, which is the legislative technique for technical harmonisation, and are aligned with the reference provisions laid down in Decision No 768/2008/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council^(10).

<a id="rec-7"></a>
(7) In accordance with the Regulations concerned, manufacturers are to draw up an EU declaration of conformity stating that the fulfilment of applicable essential requirements has been demonstrated. In order to enable seamless electronic processes, the EU declaration of conformity should be drawn up in electronic form only.

<a id="rec-8"></a>
(8) Regulations (EU) 2016/424, (EU) 2016/425, (EU) 2016/426 and (EU) 2023/1230 require that a copy of the EU declaration of conformity accompanies a product. Considering the evolution of digitalisation, it is essential that that obligation be modernised by requiring that such an EU declaration of conformity ▌ accompany the product in electronic form. The manufacturer should therefore ensure that the EU declaration of conformity is accessible directly through an internet address or a machine-readable code, meaning that intermediate steps, such as the number of landing pages, should be minimised as much as possible. That direct access should be free of charge, without the need to use login procedures, to provide any personal data, or to download or use additional applications specific to the economic operator or the obligation to register solely to access the EU declaration of conformity.

<a id="rec-9"></a>
(9) Taking into account that, in 2024, no less than 94 % of Union households had access to the internet, the availability of product instructions in electronic form offers an opportunity to align with current technologies and to contribute to green objectives. In that regard, the possibility for manufacturers to provide instructions in electronic form should be introduced in the Regulations concerned, should they wish to do so. Where manufacturers choose to provide instructions in electronic form, specific safeguards for consumers should be in place to ensure that they, as non-professional users, are still able to access and understand the information, which is in contrast to professional users, who are expected to possess the expertise and knowledge necessary in order to use and handle the products correctly. Therefore, where a product is used solely by professional users, namely persons acquiring the product as professional end-users in the course of their industrial or professional activities, safety information, including instructions relating to the safe use of the product, might be provided in electronic form. In contrast, the safety information should always be provided in paper format or marked on the product where it is reasonably foreseeable that a product, even if primarily intended for professional use, could also be used by consumers. That requirement ensures that all consumers, including vulnerable consumers such as elderly persons, persons with disabilities or those with limited digital literacy, are able to access and understand the safety information. Moreover, end-users should be able to obtain a paper copy of the instructions or safety information, upon request, at the time of the purchase of the product and for a certain period of time after that purchase.

<a id="rec-10"></a>
(10) The concept of ‘consumer’ forms part of the term ‘end user’ as defined in Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 of the European Parliament and of the Council^(11), which applies to the Regulations concerned, and refers to any natural person acting for purposes outside their trade, business, craft or profession.

<a id="rec-11"></a>
(11) Regulation (EU) 2016/425 contains certain sectoral specificities. In order to protect the safety of consumers, where the product is intended for, or can be reasonably foreseen to be used by, consumers, all the instructions and information related to the safe use of the product as set out in point 1.4 of Annex II to that Regulation, should be provided in paper format or made visible on the packaging.

<a id="rec-12"></a>
(12) In order to ensure that all consumers, including those who might not have regular access to digital tools, can effectively exercise their right to obtain the instructions or safety information in paper format, manufacturers should still be allowed to provide additional accessible means of communication for submitting such requests, including by telephone. That is particularly important for elderly consumers and other groups which are at risk of digital exclusion. End-users should also be clearly informed, in the safety information accompanying the product, of their right to request the instructions or safety information in paper format.

<a id="rec-13"></a>
(13) Manufacturers should, where relevant, ensure that instructions and safety information provided in electronic form take into account the requirements set out in Annex I to Directive (EU) 2019/882 of the European Parliament and of the Council^(12), insofar as the products fall within the scope of that Directive, so that all end-users, including persons with disabilities, are able to access, understand and use the safety information and instructions. Other categories of end-users, such as elderly persons or persons with limited digital literacy, might also benefit from those provisions.

<a id="rec-14"></a>
(14) Manufacturers can offer to deliver the instructions in paper format directly to end-users by an alternative means, free of charge and without imposing any obligation on distributors who are not required to print the instructions, unless they choose to do so voluntarily.

<a id="rec-15"></a>
(15) When providing the instructions and safety information in electronic form, the manufacturer should ensure that those instructions are, and that safety information is, accessible directly through an internet address or a machine-readable code, meaning that intermediate steps, such as the number of landing pages, should be minimised as much as possible. That direct access should be free of charge, without the need to use login procedures, to provide any personal data, or to download or use additional applications specific to the economic operator or the obligation to register solely to access the instructions and safety information. That is in line with the overarching vision of digital inclusion, as set out in the Joint Declaration of 23 January 2023 entitled ‘European Declaration on Digital Rights and Principles for the Digital Decade’^(13), in particular Chapter II on Solidarity and inclusion.

<a id="rec-16"></a>
(16) Given the shift towards digital information for consumers, when evaluating the implementation of the Regulations concerned, the Commission should also assess, where appropriate, whether the provisions concerning instructions or safety information in electronic form are effectively ensuring that those instructions and that information remains easily accessible and comprehensible for consumers.

<a id="rec-17"></a>
(17) The possibility for the end-user to request the instructions or safety information in paper format should not preclude manufacturers from providing supplementary information materials in an electronic form only, such as video or audio content, which cannot be reproduced in paper format. Where content is provided in paper format and in electronic form, the content should be the same.

<a id="rec-18"></a>
(18) In order to facilitate communication between economic operators and competent national authorities and end-users, providing the details of a digital contact for the manufacturer on the product and in the EU declaration of conformity is necessary to enhance the effectiveness of market surveillance and to expedite the process of tracing non-compliant products. Currently, economic operators are required to indicate their postal address on the product, but this is not always sufficient to ensure that competent national authorities can establish rapid contact. It is therefore necessary to require that economic operators provide both a postal address and a digital contact on the product and in the EU declaration of conformity. Such a digital contact should be defined in the Regulations concerned. The digital contact should allow consumers and competent national authorities to contact economic operators directly, and should be up to date and accessible free of charge, without the need to use login procedures, to provide any personal data, or to download or use additional applications specific to the economic operator or the obligation to register solely to contact the economic operator. Such a digital contact can include, for example, an email address or a contact form on a website, or any other direct means of digital communication, without intermediate steps, which allow for traceability of exchanges and should be interpreted in a technologically neutral manner. However, automatic replies to queries, chatbots, fax numbers or telephone lines should not be considered as constituting a digital contact.

<a id="rec-19"></a>
(19) The term ‘digital contact’, similarly to the term ‘electronic address’ in Regulation (EU) 2023/988 of the European Parliament and of the Council^(14) , should be interpreted in a technologically neutral manner, one which is capable of evolving with future technological developments, and should cover all forms of direct digital communication.

<a id="rec-20"></a>
(20) The Regulations concerned require that economic operators provide, upon a reasoned request from a competent national authority, all information and documentation, in paper format or electronic form, necessary to demonstrate the conformity of the respective products with the Regulations concerned. The paper format is becoming less necessary, while electronic communication enhances interaction between authorities and businesses, streamlining processes and reducing the administrative burden. In order to achieve the digitalisation of reporting requirements and to reduce the administrative burden for economic operators, in particular SMEs, and competent national authorities, economic operators should be required to provide the necessary information and documentation in electronic form only. Information and documentation provided in electronic form should be able to be made available, for example, in a digital format which allows for the possibility to print, download and save the information and documentation on an electronic device.

<a id="rec-21"></a>
(21) The current Union standardisation framework, which is based on Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 of the European Parliament and of the Council^(15), is the default framework by which standards that provide for a presumption of conformity with the relevant essential requirements or with other requirements are developed. However, where harmonised standards do not exist or where they are insufficient, the Commission should be able to adopt implementing acts establishing common specifications for the essential requirements or other requirements, as an exceptional fall-back solution to facilitate the manufacturer’s obligation to comply with those essential requirements or other requirements. Several legislative acts already contain such an option to address the issue of the lack of harmonised standards, thereby providing businesses with legal predictability and allowing them to prove a product’s compliance with Union law. Compliance with common specifications remains purely voluntary for manufacturers, who can rely on those common specifications in order to benefit from a presumption of conformity or who can choose other appropriate methods to demonstrate compliance with Union requirements.

<a id="rec-22"></a>
(22) Where the lack of cooperation by international standardisation organisations prevents European standardisation organisations from adopting relevant international standards as harmonised standards, and where such a situation cannot be adequately mitigated by alternative measures, implementing powers should be conferred on the Commission to adopt, as a measure of last resort, common specifications without making a prior request to one or more European standardisation organisations to draft or to revise harmonised standards. In view of the upcoming revision of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012, it is appropriate to limit the duration of empowerment of the last resort clause to a limited period of time. When the Commission drafts such implementing acts, all relevant information concerning the third parties contracted to support the drafting process of the implementing act is expected to be made public in a timely manner.

▌

<a id="rec-23"></a>
(23) As the digital product passport is foreseen in certain pieces of Union legislation, such as Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, it is essential to require economic operators to store the information contained in the EU declaration of conformity and instructions in the digital product passport where a product is covered by multiple pieces of legislation. That approach would reduce the administrative burden on manufacturers, as they would no longer need to maintain separate storage locations for compliance documents, such as EU declarations of conformity required under the various pieces of legislation that can apply to the same product, thereby upholding the principle of a single EU declaration of conformity. In addition, instructions provided in electronic form should be stored together with the EU declaration of conformity. By storing the documentation in one place, all necessary documents demonstrating product compliance would be easily accessible, thereby ensuring transparency and facilitating compliance. That streamlined approach would enhance the overall efficiency of the regulatory framework, and it aligns with the principle that, where several pieces of Union legislation apply to a product, the manufacturer or other economic operator, where appropriate, is to provide a single EU declaration of conformity.

<a id="rec-24"></a>
(24) To ensure regulatory continuity and to allow sufficient time for the adoption of acts relating to the products identified in Article 79 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, it is appropriate to prolong the validity of the continued transitional application of certain provisions of Directive 2009/125/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(16) until 31 December 2028. That prolongation would also support a coherent and effective transition towards the new digital instruments established by Regulation (EU) 2024/1781, in particular the digital product passport.

<a id="rec-25"></a>
(25) The extension of the continued transitional application of certain provisions of Directive 2009/125/EC should not prevent the Commission from incorporating into future implementing measures, where technically feasible, information requirements and data transparency objectives reflected in the digital product passport in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2024/1781.

<a id="rec-26"></a>
(26) Since the objectives of this Regulation, namely to reduce reporting requirements and to pursue the digital transition, cannot be sufficiently achieved by the Member States as this Regulation amends the Regulations concerned which harmonise product legislation but can rather, by reason of harmonisation of Union rules applicable to products, be better achieved at Union level, the Union may adopt measures, in accordance with the principle of subsidiarity as set out in Article 5 of the Treaty on European Union. In accordance with the principle of proportionality, as set out in that Article, this Regulation does not go beyond what is necessary in order to achieve those objectives.

<a id="rec-27"></a>
(27) To ensure a smooth and effective transition, to minimise disruptions, and to provide a reasonable timeframe for industries to adjust to the new requirements, the application of the amendments to the Regulations concerned concerning digitalisation should be deferred. ▌

<a id="rec-28"></a>
(28) In order to enable economic operators to supply any stock of products that have been placed on the market before the date of application of amendments to the Regulations concerned concerning digitalisation, it is necessary to provide for reasonable transitional arrangements that do not impede the making available on the market of products that have been placed on the market in accordance with those Regulations in their version applicable before that date.

<a id="rec-29"></a>
(29) Regulations ▌ (EU) 2016/424, (EU) 2016/425, (EU) 2016/426 ▌, (EU) 2023/1230, (EU) 2023/1542 and (EU) 2024/1781 should therefore be amended accordingly,

## Annex V

Annexes VIII, IX and XIII to Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 are amended as follows:

(1) in Annex VIII, Part B, Module D1: Quality assurance of the production process, point 5.1, second subparagraph, point (a) is replaced by the following:

‘(a) the name, postal address and digital contact of the manufacturer and, if the application is lodged by the manufacturer’s authorised representative, its name, postal address and digital contact as well,’;

(2) in Annex IX, point 2 is replaced by the following:

‘2. Name, postal address and digital contact of the manufacturer and, where applicable, its authorised representative:’;

(3) in Annex XIII, point 1, the following point is added:

‘(t) clear, understandable and readable instructions and safety information in a format that makes it possible to print, download and save them on an electronic device so that the end-user can access them at all times, in particular during a breakdown of the battery (only for stationary battery energy storage systems).’.
