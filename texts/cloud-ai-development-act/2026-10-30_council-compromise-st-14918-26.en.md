# Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL establishing a framework of measures for strengthening Europe’s cloud and AI ecosystem (Cloud and AI Development Act)

**Council of the** **European Union** **Brussels, 30 October 2026** **(OR. en)**

**14918/26** **Interinstitutional File:** ++**2026/0138 (COD)**++ **LIMITE** **TELECOM 502** **CYBER 430** **COMPET 1251** **MI 1064** **IA 274** **CODEC 1982** ++**NOTE**++ From: General Secretariat of the Council To: Delegations Subject: Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL establishing a framework of measures for strengthening Europe's cloud and AI ecosystem (Cloud and AI Development Act) - Presidency compromise text

**INTRODUCTION** 1. The Commission adopted the proposal for a Regulation establishing a framework of measures for strengthening Europe’s cloud and AI ecosystem (Cloud and AI Development Act) on 4 June 2026 ^(1).

2. The examination of the proposal in the WP Telecom started under the Cyprus Presidency on 16 June 2026. The Irish Presidency continued with the analysis of the text during four meetings of the WP Telecom on 7 July, 8 September, 22 September and 29 September, and it completed the first read-through of the entire proposal.

3. In addition, the Irish Presidency organised two online workshops on 21 September and 5 October, which allowed delegations, including capital-based experts, to raise specific questions and seek further clarifications directly from the Commission on the entire text of the proposal.

4. After the first online workshop, on 23 September, the Irish Presidency requested Member States to provide their initial drafting suggestions and written comments on Title IV (Autonomy) and Annexes II and III of the proposal. Title IV (Articles 16-44) and Annexes II and III correspond to Pillar 3 of the proposal.

5. Based on the input submitted by the Member States, the Irish Presidency has drafted the first, partial compromise text of the Cloud and AI Development Act proposal covering Pillar 3, which can be found in the Annex to this document. The Presidency has revised Articles 16-44, Annexes II and III, and some relevant recitals.

6. The Irish Presidency invites the delegations to discuss the changes proposed in this first partial compromise text during the WP Telecom meeting on 10 November 2026.

7. The changes in the document compared with the Commission proposal are marked as follows: additions are marked with **bold**, deletions are indicated with ~~strikethrough~~.

**II. MAIN CHANGES**

1. **Chapter I, Section 1 – Union assurance levels (Articles 16-18)** 1.1 The changes in ++**Article 16**++ clarify that the Union assurance levels recognition process is designed for specific services rather than providers. It has also been made clear that the review process, which is now referred in ++**paragraph 2**++, would precede the adoption of delegated acts, and on the basis of technological developments, these acts would only amend some of the criteria for the Union assurance levels and not the levels themselves.

1.2 ++**Article 17**++ has been redrafted to make the process of recognition of cloud computing services clearer, to eliminate repetitions, and to streamline the terminology used to describe the process. Notably, in ++**Article 17(3)**++ it is clarified that in the case of SMEs applying for Union assurance level 1, the evaluating competent authorities should register the EU statement of conformity in the central repository. ++**Point (b) in Article**++ ++**17(5)**++ has been moved to the new ++**paragraph 5a**++ in ++**Article 17**++, and another new ++**paragraph 7a**++ has been added to clarify that providers need to ensure continued compliance for services recognised at a specific Union assurance level. The modifications in ++**Article 17(9)**++ make it clear that competent authorities do not have to respond to reasoned objections individually, and the content of ++**paragraph 13**++ on the provision of information to the Commission has now been moved to ++**paragraph 10**++ of ++**Article 17**++.

1.3 A new ++**Article 17a**++ has been added to describe in detail the process of revocation of recognition of a cloud computing service, which is referred to in ++**Article 17(11)**++.

1.4 ++**Article 18**++ has been redrafted to provide more clarity on the process of identifying associated third countries. In particular, the second sentence in ++**point (c) of Article**++ ++**18(1)**++ has been deleted because the policy objective is achieved with the first sentence of that point. The reference to implementing acts in ++**point (f)**++ has been moved to the new ++**Article 18(2c)**++. Two ++**new paragraphs 2a and 2b**++ have been added to clarify the consequences of the Commission’s revocation or suspension of the implementing act identifying a third country as associated, and to indicate that three or more Member States may request the Commission to review the associated status of a third country.

2. **Chapter I, Section 2 – conformity assessment procedures (Article 19)** 2.1 ++**Article 19**++ has been updated to ensure consistency with the redraft of ++**Article 17**++.

2.2 A new ++**point 3a**++ has been added in ++**Article 19**++ to lay down a requirement for the Commission to publish a standard uniform template for the EU statement of conformity.

3. **Chapter I, Section 3 – Independent third-party audits (Articles 20-24)** 3.1 The last sentence of ++**Article 20(1)**++ has been deleted to ensure clarity that each Union assurance level stands on its own and that providers do not need to complete the levels below if they wish their service to be recognised at levels 2-4. The last sentence in ++**Article 20(3)**++ on sharing information has been deleted because information sharing is now covered by the new ++**Articles 28a and 29b on professional secrecy and**++ ++**information sharing systems**++. In ++**Article 20(4) point (a)**++ the 10-year period during which auditing organisations are expected not to have provided auditing services to the provider has been shortened to 5 years, in order to provide a more flexible framework for the rotation of auditors. In ++**Article 20(7)**++ it has been clarified that only substantial mistakes of providers should lead to the revocation of a positive opinion of an auditing organisation, and in ++**Article 20(8)**++ there is a modification of the requirement to submit the audit opinion for review – it would need to be done every two years instead of annually, to alleviate the burden for providers.

3.2 The last sentence in ++**Article 21(1)**++ has been deleted as it is a duplication with ++**Article 16**++.

3.3 The deletion of the first sentence in ++**Article 22(3)**++ clarifies that audit reports and opinions should not be registered in the repository.

3.4 In ++**Article 23(1)**++, a deadline of 7 days has been added for the provider to notify any material change in circumstances to the auditing organisation and the competent authority of establishment. The last sentence in ++**Article 23(3)**++ has been deleted because the revocation process is now covered by the new ++**Article 17a**++.

3.5 In ++**Article 24 a new paragraph 2a**++ has been added to indicate the maximum ceilings for administrative fines that can be imposed for infringements of the provisions on the cloud computing sovereignty framework.

4. **Chapter I, Section 4 – National competent authorities (Articles 25-26)** 4.1 In ++**Articles 25 and 26**++ (and throughout the text) the term ‘national competent authorities’ has been replaced with ‘competent authorities’, in line with existing legislation (e.g. the DSA).

5. **Chapter I, Section 5 – Mutual assistance and cooperation (Articles 27-28b)** 5.1 ++**Article 28**++ has been revised to clarify the mechanism for cross-border cooperation. In particular, in ++**paragraph 3**++ and the new ++**paragraph 3a**++, further details have been added on the requests to the competent authority of establishment to assess compliance and take enforcement measures.

5.2 New ++**Articles 28a and 29b**++ have been added containing requirements to ensure professional secrecy and to use reliable and secure information sharing systems for all communications pursuant to the provisions on the cloud computing sovereignty framework.

6. **Chapter II, Section 1 – Public procurement (Articles 29-30)** 6.1 In ++**Article 29 (1) point (a)**++, national security and defence have been deleted to clarify that these two areas would not be covered by the provisions on risk assessments. The wording in ++**point (a) of Article 29(2)**++ has been aligned with the wording of the annexes and the last sentence has been deleted as it is covered by the GDPR. In ++**Article**++ ++**29(3)**++ a deadline has been introduced for the Commission to publish the risk assessment methodology and it has been clarified that the aim to the methodology is guidance for the Member States. The addition in ++**paragraph 4**++ clarifies that Member States’ reporting obligations should follow the principles of necessity and data minimisation and must not create additional security exposure. ++**Article 29(5)**++ has been revised to remove the possibility for the Commission to adopt implementing acts on the Union assurance levels identified for the public sector activities by the Member States, replacing it with Commission recommendations, to which the Member States would be obliged to respond within 3 months. In ++**Article 29(6)**++ the period for the migration to another cloud computing service which may be mandated has been extended from 12 to 18 months.

6.2 ++**Article 30 (1)**++ has been modified to extend the scope of the provisions on public procurement to contracting entities covered by Directive 2014/25/EU (private companies that procure under public procurement rules). Contracting entities are also included in the scope of the proposals for Chips Act 2 and the Industrial Accelerator Act, and these sectors are considered critical for public order (e.g. electricity and water supply). The text of the remaining paragraphs in ++**Article 30**++ has also been updated to align it with the wording of the proposals for Chips Act 2 and the Industrial Accelerator Act. Moreover, in ++**Article 30(2)**++ it has been clarified that public order criterion also applies to Union assurance level 1.

7. **Chapter II, Section 2 – Private sector entities (Article 31)** 7.1 In ++**Article 31 paragraph 3**++ has been deleted to clarify that there is no empowerment for the Commission to extend the scope to the private sector through a delegated act.

8. **Chapter II, Section 3 – Other procurement-related measures (Article 32-33)** 8.1 In ++**Article 32**++ the entire ++**paragraph 2**++ has been deleted because it largely reproduces requirements that already apply to award criteria under the existing EU public procurement framework. The content of this paragraph has been transferred to ++**Recital 67**++, but without the content of ++**point (d)**++, which concerns the requirement that Union Added Value criteria should not be “decisive” in the award of a contract, and which could create uncertainty in the context of existing public procurement rules.

8.2 In ++**Article 33**++, the entire ++**paragraph 2**++ has been deleted because similar provisions on identifying barriers for SMEs in procurement procedures are included in the Public Procurement Directive. In ++**paragraph 3**++, the frequency of Member States’ reports to the Commission on their use of procurement of innovation in cloud computing services and AI systems has been reduced from annually to every three years, and in ++**paragraph 4**++ the exact percentage for the Member States to pursue as objective with regard to innovative SMEs and public procurement for cloud computing services and AI systems has been replaced with the phrase ‘adequate percentage’. ++**Point (a) in Article 33(5)**++ has been deleted because preliminary market consultations is a horizontal requirement to be covered in the Public Procurement Act.

9. **Chapter III – European public sector cloud federation (Articles 34-36)** 9.1 In ++**Article 34(3)**++ some further specifications for the platform for the EuroCloud Federation to be established by the Commission have been added. Moreover, it has been clarified in ++**Recital 69**++ that EuroCloud aims to bring together existing initiatives and that participation is voluntary.

9.2 ++**Article 35 (1)**++ has been revised to clarify the criteria the Commission will apply when assessing a sharing entity's control over an intermediate legal entity and to indicate that ownership is not a condition, but rather operational control. In ++**paragraph 2**++ it has been specified what measures the sharing entity should put in place to ensure an effective, secure and resilient provision of services, with the wording based on the NIS Directive. In paragraph 4, additional elements have been added on the need to inform the Commission of material changes and the possibility for the Commission to withdraw the authorisation to share services.

9.3 In ++**Article 36(1)**++ a clarification has been added on the characteristics of the fees to be charged by the Commission to the members of the EuroCloud Federation, based on the existing text of ++**Article 40**++.

10. **Chapter IV – Procurement of data centre services, cloud computing services,** **software and AI systems by the Commission (Articles 37-40)** 10.1 In ++**Article 37(1)**++ AI models have been added to the list of items that the Commission may procure for itself and for Union entities and for contracting authorities of Member States. The modification in ++**Article 37(5)**++ is an alignment with the proposed amendment to ++**Article 38(10)**++, so that both provisions consistently limit mandatory ancillary support to services that are objectively necessary for the procurement activity concerned. In ++**Article 37(6)**++ a reference to the interoperability of the common procurement platform has been added.

10.2 In ++**Article 38**++, ++**paragraph 2**++ has been deleted and its content has been included in ++**paragraph 1**++. In ++**paragraph 3**++ it has been clarified that the division of responsibilities between the Commission and participating entities will be done in the agreement, and the last sentence on the Commission’s responsibility for the operation and management of procurement activities has been moved to ++**paragraph 5**++. In addition, ++**paragraph 5**++ also contains a sentence moved from ++**Article 39(4)**++ on the Commission’s right to launch a procurement procedure open to participating entities without a prior specific request from them. In ++**paragraph 7**++ a safeguard has been included concerning the participation of a contracting authority of a Member State without that Member State’s participation, and in ++**paragraph 8**++ a clarification has been made to ensure that smaller Member States are not discriminated with regard to access to procurement agreements.

10.3 In ++**Article 39(3)**++ a clarification has been made that a contracting authority that has acquired services and supplies from the Commission as a central purchasing body may offer those services and supplies to other contracting authorities without applying the public procurement procedures.

10.4 In ++**Article 40(5) point (d)**++ has been deleted as it is a duplication of ++**point (c)**++. It has also been clarified which elements the Commission may take into account in the implementing act when setting the fees for participation in procurement activities.

11. **Chapter V – Open source (Articles 41-44)** 11.1 The title of ++**Article 41**++ has been updated to refer only to the promotion of open source solutions.

11.2 In ++**Article 43(1)**++ the term ‘centralised’ has been replaced with ‘federated’ as it describes more accurately the nature of the EU OSS Catalogue.

12. **Annex II** 12.1 At the beginning of ++**Annex II**++, a list of definitions has been added, so that the definitions of various terms that appear across the four assurance levels do not have to be repeated.

12.2 In ++**point 1.1 (c)**++ a cybersecurity derogation from the requirement for customer data to remain in the Union has been added. This has been replicated across levels 2 and 3.

12.3 In ++**points 1.1 (ga) and (gb)**++ the requirements for standards-based integration of external identity providers for authentication and access management and for the capability to record, retain, and review logs of management and data access activities have been added. This has been replicated across all levels.

12.4 In ++**point 2.1 subpoint (g) (iv)**++ has been deleted in line with changes in Article 18. Also, the scenario described in this subpoint is already covered by subpoint (iii). The same change has been made for level 3.

12.5 The modification in ++**point 2.1 (i)**++ clarifies that open source is not covered by the measures concerning software supply chain. The same change has been made for levels 3 and 4.

12.6 ++**Point 4.1 (j)**++ has been updated to expand the scope also to proprietary software.

13. **Annex III** 13.1 In the ++**first paragraph of Annex III**++ it has been clarified that the Annex is not exhaustive and that auditing organisations should take into account existing evidence for the purpose of the audits.

13.2 In ++**points 5 and 6 of criterion A**++ the references to payroll records, personnel timesheets, employment contracts and purchase orders have been removed.

13.3 In ++**criterion B**++ clarifications concerning location of infrastructure have been added in points ++**1(a) and 1 (ca)**++.

13.4 In ++**criterion C point 4**++ a reference to evidence demonstrating the audited organization has established a service agreement with each of its customers entities of the service has been added.

13.5 In ++**criterion E point 1**++ the changes are meant to ensure respect for data minimisation principle. In ++**point 3(b)**++ it has been clarified that a valid certificate should be issued under the national cybersecurity certification scheme in place in the Member State.

13.6 In ++**criterion G**++ additional requirements concerning the absence of third-country control or third-country entity control have been added in ++**points 1 and 2**++.

13.7 Additional minor clarifications have been added in ++**criteria H-K**++.

14. **Recitals** 14.1 ++**Recitals 37, 55, 59-64b, 67, 69, 71, 74, 76, 78-80, 84**++ have been aligned to reflect the changes in ++**Articles 16-44**++. This includes new ++**Recitals 61a and 64b**++.

14.2 The text deleted from ++**Recital 64**++ has been updated and added in the new ++**Recital 64a**++.

(37) Data centres are critical infrastructure for the Union, and can create substantial economic value, including valuable investments and jobs, and may support innovation ecosystems – especially if they are ~~integrated~~ **aligned** with local needs and follow best practices. If properly managed, the expansion of data centre capacity in the Union can bring significant economic and strategic benefits, help modernise the energy system, support clean energy growth and the sustainable use of energy. (55) Independent audits are an important tool for monitoring the compliance of cloud ~~compuring~~ **computing** services provided by cloud computing service **providers**. To ensure that audits are carried out in an effective, efficient and timely manner, cloud computing service providers should provide the necessary cooperation and assistance to the organisations carrying out the audits, including by giving the auditing organisations access to all relevant data and premises necessary to perform the audit properly and answering oral or written questions. Auditing organisations should have the necessary expertise in risk management and technical competence to audit cloud computing services. They should comply with core independence requirements for prohibited nonauditing services, firm rotation and non-contingent fees. If their independence or technical competence of auditing organisations is not beyond doubt, they should abstain or resign from the audit engagement.

## Chapter V – Open source

<a id="art-41"></a>
### Article 41 – Promoting open source solutions ~~andopen source first~~

The Union and Member States shall take the necessary measures to encourage Union entities and public sector bodies to use and facilitate the reuse of open standards and components released under an open source licence when building their cloud and AI ecosystem or stack, taking into account functionalities, including security, total cost, **interoperability** and other relevant, duly justified objective criteria.

<a id="art-42"></a>
### Article 42 – Share and reuse of software

When making software to which they hold intellectual property rights available for reuse under an open source licence, a Union entity or public sector body shall do so using a catalogue or repository that is connected to, and made accessible through, the EU OSS Catalogue referred to in Article 43.

<a id="art-43"></a>
### Article 43 – EU Open Source Solutions Catalogue

1. The Commission shall provide and maintain an EU Open Source Solutions Catalogue (‘EU OSS Catalogue’) as a ~~centralised~~ **federated** catalogue to access software made available for reuse by Union entities and public sector bodies.

2. The EU OSS Catalogue shall be hosted on the Interoperable Europe portal referred to in Article 8 of Regulation (EU) 2024/903 and shall be accessible electronically free of charge.

3. The Commission shall, on the basis of objective and relevant criteria, decide on the request of any Union entity or public sector body owning or maintaining a catalogue or repository to have that catalogue or repository connected to and made accessible through the EU OSS Catalogue.

<a id="art-44"></a>
### Article 44 – Network of Open Source Programme Offices

1. The Commission shall establish a network of Open Source Programme Offices (‘OSPO Network’) to facilitate cooperation on the implementation of the obligations under this Chapter.

2. Open Source Programme Offices established by public sector bodies at local, regional or national level in a Member State, and those established by Union entities, may request from the Commission to join the OSPO Network.

3. The OSPO Network shall have the following tasks:

- (a) facilitating the exchange of information, experience and best practices between Member States and the Commission, in particular by discussing common technical, legal and organisational challenges, including those related to licensing, security, maintenance and procurement of open-source software;

- (b) promoting the sharing and reuse of open-source software by public sector bodies;

- (c) contributing, on a voluntary and non-binding basis, to the development of guidance, templates or recommendations on the sharing and reuse of open-source software;

- (d) collaborating on and exchanging open-source projects of common interest to Union entities and public sector bodies.

4. The Commission shall support and coordinate the OSPO Network.

5. The Commission shall convene and chair a meeting of the members of the OSPO Network at least twice a year. The meetings of the OSPO Network may be organised online.

- (1) The audited provider should ensure transparency through remote access and source code auditability by:

  - (a) making available to the auditing organisation a list of evidence to prove that there is no use of any remote features or mechanisms that could be used to materially tamper with or disrupt a device, system, or software. This should include:

i. evidence related to the testing of the software component to prevent the use of any remote features or mechanisms that could be used to materially tamper with or disrupt a device, system, or software (test procedure, test reports, test plan, etc.);

ii. evidence that the organisation’s change management procedures include any change in firmware, bios and software updates as well as integration of new components to prevent the use of any remote feature or mechanism;

iii. evidence that the maintenance procedure is updated to include preventing any remote feature or mechanism that could be used to materially tamper with or disrupt a device, system or software;

- (2) The audited provider should provide:

  - (a) evidence of a risk-based process to identify and mitigate: (i) a weak ecosystem and community support of the OSS; (ii) a failure to continuously monitor the updates released; (iii) cases where the OSS is deprecated or is no longer maintained.

11. Audit criterion K – Global services and subsidiaries in third-countries

The auditing organisation should assess the audit criterion listed under Union assurance levels 2, 3, and 4 paragraph (k) of Annex II based on the following evidence:

- (1) The auditing organisation should verify that ~~the subsidiary is legally and operationally~~ ~~independent from~~ **effective legal, technical and organisational separation is maintained** **between** the audited provider **and the third-country subsidiary in accordance with Annex** **II**.

- (2) The audited provider must demonstrate that the subsidiary has no access to systems processing or storing the customer data.

- (3) The audited provider must demonstrate that the subsidiary has no privileged accounts within the Union production environments, including cloud administration, Identity and Access Management (IAM), Privileged Access Management (PAM), monitoring or database administration privileges.

- (4) The auditing organisation should verify that the personnel of the subsidiary cannot obtain access to Union customer data.

- (5) The auditing organisation should verify that the subsidiary has no authority to instruct Union operational staff to disclose **Union** customer data or bypass **Union** security procedures.

- (6) The auditing organisation should verify that all foreign government requests **concerning** **Union public sector bodies and** received by the subsidiary are formally redirected to the competent Union entity for legal assessment under Union and Member State law.
