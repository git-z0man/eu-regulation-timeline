# DRAFT REPORT on the proposal for a regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on a framework of measures for strengthening the Union’s semiconductor ecosystem, repealing Regulation (EU) 2023/1781 (Chips Act 2.0)

Committee on Industry, Research and Energy · Procedure 2026/0139(COD) · 9.11.2026 · Rapporteur: Oliver Schenk

<a id="draft-european-parliament-legislative-resolution"></a>
## DRAFT EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT LEGISLATIVE RESOLUTION

on the proposal for a regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on a framework of measures for strengthening the Union’s semiconductor ecosystem, repealing Regulation (EU) 2023/1781 (Chips Act 2.0)

(COM(2026)0504 – C100130/2026 – 2026/0139(COD))

(Ordinary legislative procedure: first reading)

The European Parliament,

– having regard to the Commission proposal to Parliament and the Council (COM(2026)0504),

– having regard to Article 294(2) and Articles 114 and 173(3) of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, pursuant to which the Commission submitted the proposal to Parliament (C100130/2026),

– having regard to Article 294(3) of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union,

– having regard to the budgetary assessment by the Committee on Budgets,

– having regard to the opinion of the European Economic and Social Committee of ... ^(2),

– having regard to the opinion of the of the Committee of the Regions^(3)

– having regard to Rules 60 and 58 of its Rules of Procedure,

– having regard to the opinions of the Committee on the Internal Market and Consumer Protection and the Committee on Legal Affairs,

– having regard to the report of the Committee on Industry, Research and Energy (A100000/2026),

1. Adopts its position at first reading hereinafter set out;

2. Calls on the Commission to refer the matter to Parliament again if it replaces, substantially amends or intends to substantially amend its proposal;

3.. Instructs its President to forward its position to the Council, the Commission and the national parliaments.

## EXPLANATORY STATEMENT

Semiconductors are essential to Europe's industrial base and to the green and digital transitions. They are also at the centre of increasingly complex and concentrated global supply chains. Recent disruptions have exposed the risks arising from critical dependencies, while growing international competition is making it more difficult to attract investment and retain technological capabilities. Europe has important strengths in research, equipment, materials and specialised technologies, but these strengths do not always translate into industrial production and market uptake within the Union.

The rapporteur therefore welcomes the Commission's proposal to build on the first European Chips Act. However, strengthening Europe's semiconductor ecosystem requires more than supporting new production facilities. Persistent challenges include the gap between research and commercial deployment, insufficient coordination between producers and users, difficult access to finance, skills shortages and lengthy administrative procedures. The amendments seek to address these obstacles through a more coherent approach covering the entire semiconductor value chain.

The purpose is to translate the Union's objectives of competitiveness, technological sovereignty, resilience and security of supply into practical results. Public support should target clearly identified capability gaps, reinforce areas where Europe already has a competitive advantage and generate benefits beyond individual companies or Member States. Reducing strategic dependencies must also go hand in hand with diversification and reliable international partnerships, rather than attempting to reproduce every part of the global supply chain in Europe.

For the Chips for Europe Initiative 2.0, the rapporteur places particular emphasis on the transition from research to industrial application. Despite substantial scientific expertise and public investment, innovative technologies too often face difficulties in reaching commercial scale. The amendments therefore strengthen technology transfer, access to pilot lines and design tools, advanced packaging, photonics, quantum technologies, materials and manufacturing equipment. They also seek to make better use of existing capacities and to involve industrial users, SMEs and small mid-caps more closely. Continuity of funding beyond 2027 is important because semiconductor development and industrial scale-up require long investment horizons.

A second challenge is that technological capability does not automatically create a market. Without credible demand, companies may be reluctant to invest in developing or manufacturing innovative chips in Europe. The rapporteur accordingly strengthens demand accelerators and the demand forum and proposes first-buyer commitments through innovation procurement. These instruments aim to connect semiconductor producers with downstream sectors, support initial market deployment and reduce investment uncertainty, while respecting competition, public procurement and State aid rules.

Industrial investment must also become easier to undertake and more closely linked to demonstrable European added value. The amendments recognise the expansion and upgrading of existing facilities alongside new projects and provide for independent expert advice in the assessment of strategic projects. Requirements concerning commercial viability, cross-border benefits and supply-chain resilience are intended to improve the quality of public investment decisions. More predictable permitting procedures, one-stop shops and improved access to finance should help prevent viable projects from being delayed by avoidable administrative obstacles.

The rapporteur also considers that semiconductor policy must reflect the diversity of regional ecosystems. Established clusters need to maintain their strengths, while emerging regions should have opportunities to develop specialised capabilities. The proposed regional support mechanisms, together with stronger measures on skills and cooperation between industry, research organisations and education providers, seek to broaden participation and address shortages of qualified workers.

Finally, greater preparedness should not come at the expense of proportionality or commercial confidentiality. Better strategic mapping, industry involvement and coordinated crisis responses can help identify vulnerabilities earlier but monitoring and information requests must remain necessary and targeted.

A European Semiconductor Strategy would bring these measures together, improve coordination between Union and national policies and provide a longer-term direction for investment.

The rapporteur's amendments are therefore intended to make the revised Chips Act a more practical framework for strengthening Europe's industrial capabilities and resilience.
