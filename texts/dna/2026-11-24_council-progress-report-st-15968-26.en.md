# Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on digital networks, amending Regulation (EU) 2015/2120, Directive 2002/58/EC and Decision No 676/2002/EC and repealing Regulation (EU) 2018/1971, Directive (EU) 2018/1972 and Decision No 243/2012/EU (Digital Networks Act)

Council of the European Union Brussels, 24 November 2026 (OR. en)

1 5968/26 Interinstitutional File: 2026/0013 (COD) TELECOM 562 CYBER 466 COMPET 1381 MI 1210 FIN 1631 ESPACE 190 CODEC 2192 I A 317 NOTE From: General Secretariat of the Council To: Council No. Cion doc.: 5600/26 + ADD 1 Subject: Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on digital networks, amending Regulation (EU) 2015/2120, Directive 2002/58/EC and Decision No 676/2002/EC and repealing Regulation (EU) 2018/1971, Directive (EU) 2018/1972 and Decision No 243/2012/EU (Digital Networks Act) (Text with EEA relevance) - Progress report

I. INTRODUCTION 1. The Commission adopted the proposal for a Regulation on digital networks, amending Regulation (EU) 2015/2120, Directive 2002/58/EC and Decision No 676/2002/EC and repealing Regulation (EU) 2018/1971, Directive (EU) 2018/1972 and Decision No 243/2012/EU (Digital Networks Act), on 21 January 2026 ^(1).

2. The Digital Networks Act (DNA) proposal, based on Article 114 TFEU, aims to foster the Union’s digital transformation and connectivity objectives by supporting the development of a robust, fast, secure, resilient, cutting-edge digital networks infrastructure. The proposed Regulation seeks to streamline and further harmonise the rules applicable to electronic communications networks and services, facilitate cross-border business, promote the availability and take-up of gigabit networks, and ensure high-quality, affordable electronic communications services for end-users, while safeguarding consumer protection.

3. The “One Europe, One Market” roadmap, signed by the President of the Republic of Cyprus, representing the rotating Presidency of the Council of the European Union, and by the Presidents of the European Parliament and the European Commission on the sidelines of the informal meeting of Heads of State or Government held in Cyprus on 24 April 2026, includes this proposal among the priority deliverables, with a target for adoption in Q4 2027.

4. In the European Parliament, the Committee for Industry, Research and Energy (ITRE) has been nominated as the committee responsible for the negotiations on the Digital Networks Act, with MEP Michał Kobosko (Renew, Poland) as Rapporteur. The Committee on the Internal Market and Consumer Protection (IMCO) and the Committee on Civil Liberties, Justice and Home Affairs (LIBE) are associated.

5. The European Economic and Social Committee and the European Committee of the Regions adopted their opinions on 18 June 2026 and on 13 October 2026, respectively.

6. On 18 March 2026 the European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS) issued an opinion on the proposal ^(2).

II. STATE OF PLAY WITHIN THE COUNCIL 7. In the Council, the examination of the DNA proposal has been carried out in the Working Party on Telecommunications and Information Society (WP Telecom). Starting on 29 January 2026, under the Cyprus Presidency, the Commission first presented the main elements of the proposal. Delegations broadly supported its overall objectives and welcomed the preservation of key elements of the current framework, while stressing the need to carefully assess the proportionality of some of the proposed measures and the balance between harmonisation and national specificities.

8. Significant time was then devoted to the detailed examination of the following parts of the DNA proposal: Part I - Scope, objectives and definitions, Part II – Resilience, Part III – Single Market authorisation and passporting, Part VI – Services and Part VIII – General and final provisions, as well as Articles 13–16, 36–52 of Part IV – Resources (Radio Spectrum and Numbering).

9. In addition, four online workshops were held in March 2026 that allowed delegations, including capital-based experts, to raise specific questions and seek further clarifications directly from the Commission on the provisions related to copper switch-off and access, spectrum and satellite authorisation, general authorisation, Single Passport and numbering, and governance.

10. On 27 April 2026, the Cyprus Presidency requested Member States to provide their initial drafting suggestions and written comments on Parts I-III and VI (Articles 1-12 and 87-114), while continuing with the examination of Part IV of the Commission proposal (Resources – Radio Spectrum and Numbering) that was finalised under the Irish Presidency.

11. On 15 July 2026, the Irish Presidency requested Member States to send comments and drafting suggestions on Parts IV – Resources and VIII – General and Final provisions by 4 September 2026.

12. In parallel, on 17 and 18 September 2026, in order to advance the discussions, the Irish Presidency hosted a meeting of the Directors General for Communications, the first since 2019. The meeting brought together delegations from Member States, the Commission, the European Free Trade Association, BEREC and the RSPG to hold a strategic exchange views on spectrum and resilience.

13. In September 2026, WP Telecom resumed the article-by article examination of the Commission proposal, addressing first Part V – Transition to fibre, Markets functioning and Competition. The main exchanges focused on the scope of Member States’ flexibility regarding copper switch-off, imposition of access remedies on undertakings with significant market power and adoption of market-shaping measures. The Irish Presidency set a deadline for Member States’ written contributions on this Part by 20 November 2026.

14. In the meantime, in the light of Member States’ input, the Irish Presidency drafted the first, partial compromise text of the DNA proposal covering Parts I-III and VI, which was presented in WP Telecom on 22 October 2026. The main elements of this partial compromise text can be summarised as follows:

a) with regard to the scope and objectives in Part I, the amendments aimed at clarifying that the Regulation is without prejudice to Member States’ competence in the areas of public order, public security and defence. Also, the promotion of competition amongst the general objectives was given prominence;

b) in the provisions of Part II, related to resilience, the objectives of the changes were to ensure consistency with Directive (EU) 2022/2555 (NIS2) and Directive (EU) 2022/2557 (CER), avoid overlaps with other structures and mechanisms created to manage cybersecurity crises and clearly distinguish the responsibilities of the relevant authorities and bodies;

c) concerning Part III, the scope of the general authorisation regime was better defined, while the articles related to the Single Passport procedure were restructured, to provide for a clearer and simpler mechanism of notifications and to address concerns related to national authorities’ enforcement powers; d) as regards Part VI on services, the scope of Universal Service and its affordability and availability were clarified. Moreover, the proposed modifications addressed the possible tension between the proposed harmonisation of rights of consumers and the rules currently in force at national and Union level, such as Directive 2011/83/EU (the Consumer Rights Directive). Amendments also responded to the call for ensuring the effective functioning of emergency communications and public warnings.

15. Throughout October and November, the WP Telecom has been examining Part VII – Governance and the proposed changes to the governance structure. Discussions are ongoing on the potential overall impact of the proposed new governance structure. Further exchanges were also held on Part V of the proposal to discuss how advanced Member States are in their copper switch-off plans.

16. Based on Member States’ input on Part IV of the Commission proposal (Resources – Radio Spectrum and Numbering), the Irish Presidency is working on the second, partial compromise text, with the intention to address the proposed shift in the balance of competences between the national and the Union level and the scope of the provisions related to radio spectrum resources.

17. The Irish Presidency has aimed to contribute to building the Digital Networks Act into a credible and balanced Council compromise, advancing a stronger EU single market for connectivity while safeguarding essential national competences and interests. Through the completion of the article-by-article examination of the proposal and the preparation of compromise texts, the Irish Presidency has strived to achieve a common understanding among Member States across some of the main pillars in DNA.

18. The Presidency aims to present this second, partial compromise text to WP Telecom in December 2026.

III. CONCLUSION In light of the above, the Council is invited to take note of the progress made in the examination of the proposal.
