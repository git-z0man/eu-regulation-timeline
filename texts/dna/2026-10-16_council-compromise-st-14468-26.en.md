# Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on digital networks, amending Regulation (EU) 2015/2120, Directive 2002/58/EC and Decision No 676/2002/EC and repealing Regulation (EU) 2018/1971, Directive (EU) 2018/1972 and Decision No 243/2012/EU (Digital Networks Act) -

**Council of the** **European Union** **Brussels, 16 October 2026** **(OR. en)**

**14468/26** **Interinstitutional File:** ++**2026/0013 (COD)**++ **LIMITE** **TELECOM 481** **CYBER 410** **COMPET 1202** **MI 1022** **FIN 1454** **ESPACE 163** **CODEC 1901** **IA 265** ++**NOTE**++ From: Presidency To: Delegations No. Cion doc.: 5600/26 + ADD 1 Subject: Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on digital networks, amending Regulation (EU) 2015/2120, Directive 2002/58/EC and Decision No 676/2002/EC and repealing Regulation (EU) 2018/1971, Directive (EU) 2018/1972 and Decision No 243/2012/EU (Digital Networks Act) - Presidency compromise text - Articles 1-12 and 87-114

**I. INTRODUCTION** 1. The Commission adopted the proposal for a Regulation on digital networks, amending Regulation (EU) 2015/2120, Directive 2002/58/EC and Decision No 676/2002/EC and repealing Regulation (EU) 2018/1971, Directive (EU) 2018/1972 and Decision No 243/2012/EU (**Digital Networks Act**), on 21 January 2026 ^(1).

2. Under the Cyprus Presidency, the WP TELECOM devoted significant time to the examination of the following parts of the DNA proposal: Part I - Scope, objectives and definitions, Part II - Resilience, Part III - Single Market authorisation and passporting,

Part VI – Services, and Part VIII - General and final provisions, as well as Articles 13– 16, 36–52 of Part IV – Resources (Radio Spectrum and Numbering).

3. In addition, four online workshops were held in March that allowed delegations, including capital-based experts, to raise specific questions and seek further clarifications directly from the Commission on the provisions related to copper switch-off and access, spectrum and satellite authorisation, general authorisation, Single Passport and numbering, and governance.

4. On 27 April 2026, the Cyprus Presidency requested Member States to provide their initial drafting suggestions and written comments on Parts I-III and VI (Articles 1-12 and 87-114). As regards Article 2, Member States were asked to focus on the most relevant definitions.

5. Based on the input submitted by the Member States, the Irish Presidency has drafted the first, partial compromise text of the DNA proposal, contained in the Annex to this document.

6. The Irish Presidency invites the delegations to discuss the proposed changes in the above-mentioned Articles, including relevant definition only in Article 2, and in a number of specific recitals during the WP TELECOM meeting on 22 October 2026.

7. The changes in the document compared with the Commission proposal are underlined, additions are marked with **bold**, deletions with ~~strikethrough~~.

**II. MAIN CHANGES** 1. **Part I – Scope, Objectives and Definitions** 1.1 The new ++**Article 1(3)**++ aims at recalling explicitly that the Regulation is without prejudice to Member States’ competence in the areas of public order, public security and defence. This general principle is already referred to in several recitals, and relevant text was added also in ++**Recital 27**++. 1.2 Two definitions were added in ++**Article 2**++: ‘providers of emergency communications’ (++**2(39a)**++) and ‘information society service’(++**2(50a)**++). 1.3 In ++**Article 3(1)**++, the promotion of competition was given prominence amongst the general objectives, through the addition of ++**point (-a)**++ and the related deletion of ++**point**++ ++**(e)**++. Other amendments concern ++**points (c)**++, ++**(d)**++ and ++**(g)**++, to clarify their scope.

2. **Part II – Resilience** 2.1 ++**Articles 4-8**++ were amended with the objective of ensuring consistency with Directive (EU) 2022/2555 (NIS2) and Directive (EU) 2022/2557 (CER). 2.2 In ++**Article 4**++ and in the following ones, “associated facilities” were included next to electronic communications networks and services, as appropriate. 2.3 The text of ++**Article 6(2)**++ was adapted to clarify the scope of the Union Preparedness Plan for Digital Infrastructures (‘the Plan’) and to avoid overlaps with other structures and mechanisms created to manage cybersecurity crisis. In ++**Article**++ ++**6(1)**++, the deadline for BEREC to adopt the Plan was extended to 18 months. Additional clarifications were added in ++**Recital 27**++.

3. **Part III – Single Market Authorisation and Passporting** 3.1 Changes in ++**Article 9(1)**++ are aimed at clarifying what kind of services fall within the scope of the general authorisation. Extensive explanations and relevant examples are provided in ++**Recitals 43, 43a**++ and ++**43b**++. 3.2 With regard to the Single Passport procedure, ++**Article 10**++ was restructured and heavily amended to provide for a clearer and simpler mechanism of notifications relating to the commencement, modification, expansion or cessation of the provision of electronic communications networks or services directly and only to the ODN. ++**New**++ ++**Article 9a**++ is devoted to the Union Digital Notifications Platform as the single electronic interface for the submission, processing and management of such notifications. The corresponding ++**Recitals 44, 45, 46**++ and ++**52**++ were amended accordingly. 3.3 New text in ++**Article 11**++ is meant to address concerns related to national authorities’ enforcement powers in view of the proposed Single Passport procedure. In the light of these changes, the concept of ‘main establishment’ was introduced to link a provider to the national regulatory authority having the competence to suspend or withdraw that provider’s right to operate in one or more Member States in case of a serious breach of the general authorisation conditions. ++**Article 12**++ was also amended, as appropriate, and additional information was included in ++**new Recitals 61a, 61b**++ and in the amendments to ++**Recitals 62 and 65**++.

4. **Part VI - Services** 4.1 Some clarifications were included in ++**Article 87**++ about the scope of Universal Service and in ++**Articles 88, 89**++ and ++**90**++ concerning its affordability and availability. ++**Recital 231**++ now includes text to better explain the Commission’s empowerment related to the definition of adequate internet access, while ++**new Recitals 237a**++ and ++**244a**++ regard the possibility for Member States to grant compensation to the providers entrusted with the provision of universal service as a service of general economic interest. 4.2 ++**Articles 93**++ and ++**94**++ on open internet access were slightly amended to address the risk of excessive administrative burden. A sentence was added at the end of ++**Recital 254**++ to refer to the statement of reasons of Regulation (EU) 2015/2120 (the Open Internet Regulation). 4.3 With regard to end-users’ rights, a ++**new Article 94a**++ was drafted – and relevant text moved from ++**Articles 95(4)**++ and ++**(7), Article 96(2)**++ and ++**Article 97(6)**++ - to address the possible tension between the proposed harmonisation of rights of consumers and the national rules currently in force. The new text also aims to clarify the interplay with Directive 2011/83/EU (the Consumer rights Directive). Additional text in ++**Recital 258**++ further clarifies the interaction with national rules in the area of contract law. 4.4 A sentence was added in ++**Recital 259**++ to explicitely reference the protection of rights of microenterprises under the DNA, which should be similar to that enjoyed under Directive (EU) 2018/1972 (the Code). 4.5 The structure of ++**Article 100**++ was reviewed to better distinguish and clarify the provisions related to provider switching and number portability. Text was added in new ++**Article 100(1a)**++ to include a reference to provision of adequate information also for the porting process, and in ++**Recital 279**++ regarding the issue of double billing. 4.6 Changes to ++**Article 103**++ and ++**Recital 284**++ aim at clarifying the scope of the information gathering and the competences at national and EU level, and the relationship with the PSR cooperation platform and with pre-existing national measures. 4.7 In relation to the wider scope of ++**Article 105**++, explanations about the distribution of public interest information were included in ++**Recital 287.**++

4.8 ++**Articles 106, 107**++ and ++**108**++ were amended to address the need to ensure the effective functioning of emergency communications and public warnings, as well as of the relevant restrictions through e.g. the possibility for PSAPs to call back end-users free of charge, a more nuanced obligation concerning the use of the Digital Identity Wallets, the obligation for providers of emergency communications to inform end-users of possible limitations of access to emergency services or provision of caller location information, and the possibility to trace back the originating point of the communication. These issues were also addressed by more detailed explanations in ++**Recitals 289**++, ++**289a**++, ++**293**++, ++**293a, 296, 298, 300a**++ and ++**301**++. 4.9 In ++**Article 111(1)**++, both points (a) and (b) were amended to ensure that providers meet the requirements of Directive (EU) 2019/882 (European Accessibility Act). 4.10 ++**Article 112(3)**++ was slightly amended to ensure that technical, contractual or practical restrictions cannot be used to circumvent the right to a free and easy return of digital television equipment.

**1. PART I - SCOPE, OBJECTIVES AND DEFINITIONS**

<a id="art-1"></a>
### Article 1

**Subject matter** 1. This Regulation establishes rules for the provision of electronic communications networks, electronic communications services, associated facilities and associated services, the strategic planning and management of radio spectrum and certain aspects of terminal equipment.

2. This Regulation also establishes a governance framework for the electronic communications sector composed of the national regulatory and other competent authorities, the Body of European Regulators for Electronic Communications (‘BEREC’), the Radio Spectrum Policy Body (‘RSPB’), and the Office for Digital Networks (‘ODN’), laying down the tasks of BEREC, the RSPB and the ODN as well as those of national regulatory authorities and, where applicable, of other competent authorities, and establishes a set of procedures for the application of the relevant legal framework throughout the Union.

3. ++**This Regulation is without prejudice to actions taken by Member States for public order**++ ++**and public security purposes and defence.**++

<a id="art-2"></a>
### Article 2

**Definitions** For the purposes of this Regulation, the following definitions apply:

- (1) ‘electronic communications network’ means transmission systems, whether or not based on a permanent infrastructure or centralised administration capacity, and, where applicable, switching or routing equipment and other resources, including network elements which are not active, which permit the conveyance of signals by wire, radio, optical or other electromagnetic means, including satellite networks, fixed and mobile networks, electricity cable systems, to the extent that they are used for the purpose of transmitting signals, networks used for radio and television broadcasting, and cable television networks, irrespective of the type of information conveyed;

- (2) ‘gigabit network’ means either an electronic communications network which consists wholly of optical fibre elements up to the network termination point, or an electronic communications network which is capable of delivering, under usual peak-time conditions, similar network performance in terms of available downlink and uplink bandwidth, resilience, error-related parameters, and latency and its variation;

This definition replaces the definition of ‘very high capacity network’ of Article 2(2) of Directive (EU) 2018/1972;

- (3) ‘electronic communications service’ means a service normally provided for remuneration via electronic communications networks, which encompasses, with the exception of services providing or exercising editorial control over content transmitted using electronic communications networks and services, the following types of services:

  - (a) internet access service;

  - (b) interpersonal communications service;

  - (c) services consisting wholly or mainly in the conveyance of signals such as transmission services used for the provision of machine-to-machine services and for broadcasting;

**4.2. TITLE II: OPEN INTERNET ACCESS**

<a id="art-93"></a>
### Article 93

**Safeguarding of open internet access** 1. End-users shall have the right to access and distribute information and content, use and provide applications and services, and use terminal equipment of their choice, irrespective of the end-user’s or provider’s location or the location, origin or destination of the information, content, application or service, via their internet access service. This paragraph is without prejudice to Union law, or national law that complies with Union law, related to the lawfulness of the content, applications or services.

2. Agreements between providers of internet access services and end-users on commercial and technical conditions and the characteristics of internet access services such as price, data volumes or speed, and any commercial practices conducted by providers of internet access services, shall not limit the exercise of the rights of end-users laid down in paragraph 1.

3. Providers of internet access services shall treat all traffic equally, when providing internet access services, without discrimination, restriction or interference, and irrespective of the sender and receiver, the content accessed or distributed, the applications or services used or provided, or the terminal equipment used.

The first subparagraph shall not prevent providers of internet access services from implementing reasonable traffic management measures. In order to be deemed to be reasonable, such measures shall be transparent, non-discriminatory and proportionate, and shall not be based on commercial considerations but on objectively different technical quality of service requirements of specific categories of traffic. Such measures shall not monitor the specific content and shall not be maintained for longer than necessary.

Providers of internet access services shall not engage in traffic management measures going beyond those set out in the second subparagraph, and in particular shall not block, slow down, alter, restrict, interfere with, degrade or discriminate between specific content, applications or services, or specific categories thereof, except as necessary, and only for as long as necessary, in order to:

- (a) comply with Union legislative acts, or national legislation that complies with Union law, to which the provider of internet access services is subject, or with measures that comply with Union law giving effect to such Union legislative acts or national legislation, including with orders by courts or public authorities vested with relevant powers;

- (b) preserve the integrity and security of the network, of services provided via that network, and of the terminal equipment of end-users;

- (c) prevent impending network congestion and mitigate the effects of exceptional or temporary network congestion, provided that equivalent categories of traffic are treated equally.

4. Any traffic management measure may entail processing of personal data only if such processing is necessary and proportionate to achieve the objectives set out in paragraph 3. Such processing shall be carried out in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council. Traffic management measures shall also comply with Directive 2002/58/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council.

5. Providers of electronic communications to the public, including providers of internet access services, and providers of content, applications and services shall be free to offer services other than internet access services which are optimised for specific content, applications or services, or a combination thereof, where the optimisation is necessary in order to meet requirements of the content, applications or services for a specific level of quality. Providers of electronic communications to the public, including providers of internet access services, may offer or facilitate such services only where the network capacity is sufficient to provide them in addition to any internet access services provided. Such services shall not be usable or offered as a replacement for internet access services, and shall not be to the detriment of the availability or general quality of internet access services for end-users.

6. The Commission may++**, after consulting BEREC,**++ adopt implementing acts detailing the conditions referred to in this Article for the offering of services other than internet access services which are optimised for specific content, applications or services, or a combination thereof.

Those implementing acts shall be adopted in accordance with the procedure referred to in Article 199(4).
