## Recitals

<a id="rec-1"></a>
(1) Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(2) and Decision No 768/2008/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(3) bring together, in what is called the new legislative framework, all the elements required for a comprehensive regulatory framework to operate effectively for the safety and compliance of industrial products, and for the proper functioning of the internal market. One of the main objectives of the new legislative framework is to ensure a robust and reliable conformity assessment procedure for products in the Union. Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 establishes the legal basis for accreditation and market surveillance. Decision No 768/2008/EC consolidates the technical instruments for Union harmonisation legislation and, in particular, the criteria for designation of conformity assessment bodies as well as the conformity assessment procedures and the rules for their use. Decision No 768/2008/EC requires Union legislation harmonising the conditions for the marketing of products to incorporate, as far as possible, the reference provisions set out in Annex I to that Decision.

<a id="rec-2"></a>
(2) In accordance with Article 11(1) of Regulation (EC) No 765/2008, national accreditation bodies that demonstrate conformity with the criteria laid down in the relevant harmonised standard, the reference of which has been published in the Official Journal of the European Union, by having successfully undergone peer evaluation under Article 10 of Regulation (EC) No 765/2008, are to be presumed to fulfil the requirements laid down in Article 8 of that Regulation.

<a id="rec-3"></a>
(3) In point (10) of Article 2 of Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 ‘accreditation’ is defined as an attestation by a national accreditation body that a conformity assessment body meets the requirements set by harmonised standards and, where applicable, any additional requirements including those set out in relevant sectoral schemes, to carry out a specific conformity assessment activity. A ‘conformity assessment body’ is defined in point (13) of Article 2 of that Regulation as a body that performs conformity assessment activities including calibration, testing, certification and inspection.

<a id="rec-4"></a>
(4) Union legislation incorporating the reference provisions included in Annex I to Decision No 768/2008/EC provides, in certain cases, for the intervention of third party conformity assessment bodies in the relevant conformity assessment procedures. Furthermore, such legislation incorporates Articles R17 and R18 of Annex I to Decision 768/2008/EC and sets out the requirements that conformity assessment bodies must meet. Such legislation provides that where a conformity assessment body demonstrates its conformity with the criteria laid down in the relevant harmonised standards or parts thereof, the references of which have been published in the Official Journal of the European Union, it is to be presumed to comply with the requirements set out in that Union act, insofar as the applicable harmonised standards cover those requirements.

<a id="rec-5"></a>
(5) There is also Union legislation which does not incorporate Article R17 of Annex I to Decision 768/2008/EC but that requires the intervention of third party conformity assessment bodies and provides for their accreditation in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 765/2008.

<a id="rec-6"></a>
(6) Some Union legislation, which does not incorporate Article R17 of Annex I to Decision 768/2008/EC, includes provisions requiring the intervention of a third party conformity assessment body and allowing submission of documentation delivered in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 in support of its application for designation as a notified body.

<a id="rec-7"></a>
(7) Harmonised standards help to ensure a high technical capacity of the accredited conformity assessment bodies and thus ensure the precision and reliability of the checks provided for in the sectoral legislation, and thus contribute to the protection of public health and safety of products in the internal market.

<a id="rec-8"></a>
(8) Several harmonised standards have been drafted by the European Committee for Standardisation (CEN) and the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation (Cenelec) on the basis of standardisation mandate M/417 of 4 December 2007 addressed to CEN, Cenelec and the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) in the field of accreditation and conformity assessment.

<a id="rec-9"></a>
(9) The Union’s conformity assessment policy as reflected in the new legislative framework also has an important international dimension. In particular, the use of conformity assessment and accreditation as tools developed at international level ensures compatibility with international practice and supports the competitiveness of European industry and business by promoting the recognition and acceptance of conformity assessment results as widely as possible beyond the borders of the internal market. Accreditation and conformity assessments are based on international standards of the International Organization for Standardisation (ISO) and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC). Those standards serve as a basis for harmonised standards at Union level. It is thus imperative to ensure that the harmonised standards continue to be as much as possible in line with international standards. In this respect it is necessary to update or revise harmonised standards whenever the relevant international standards are updated or revised. International standards are updated or revised on a regular basis in order to take account of changes in the ‘know-how’.

<a id="rec-10"></a>
(10) It is also necessary to adopt new harmonised standards where additional ‘know-how’ is necessary and base those standards on relevant international standards where such standards are available.

<a id="rec-11"></a>
(11) The intention to request the drafting of harmonised standards as regards accreditation and conformity assessment is stated in point 11 of the table entitled "Standardisation requests for development/revision of harmonised European standards in support of Union legislation" set out in the document accompanying the annual Union work programme for European standardisation for 2020 ^(4).

<a id="rec-12"></a>
(12) CEN and Cenelec have indicated that the work covered by the request falls within their area of competence.

<a id="rec-13"></a>
(13) It is therefore appropriate to request CEN and Cenelec to draft new harmonised standards and to revise existing harmonised standards in support of Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 as regards standards for accreditation and conformity assessment.

<a id="rec-14"></a>
(14) Harmonised standards should be developed according to best practices available. They should fulfil the requirements set out in the relevant Union legislation.

<a id="rec-15"></a>
(15) Harmonised standards should include detailed technical specifications of the relevant requirements set out in the Regulation (EC) 765/2008 for accreditation bodies and in the relevant parts of the sectoral legislation for conformity assessment bodies. They should also indicate clearly which requirements they aim to cover.

<a id="rec-16"></a>
(16) Information as to which requirements for accreditation bodies and conformity assessment bodies are covered by a harmonised standard is necessary when assessing, in accordance with Article 10(5) of Regulation (EU) 1025/2012, the compliance of the documents drafted by European standardisation organisations (ESOs). Such information is also necessary before the publication of references of harmonised standards in the Official Journal of the European Union in accordance with Article 10(6) of Regulation (EU) 1025/2012. In each harmonised standard CEN and Cenelec should therefore describe the extent to which it aims to cover one or several requirements set out in Regulation (EC) 765/2008 and in relevant sectoral legislation.

<a id="rec-17"></a>
(17) The ESOs have agreed to follow the Guidelines for the execution of standardisation requests ^(5).

<a id="rec-18"></a>
(18) In order to ensure transparency and to facilitate the execution of the requested standardisation activities, CEN and Cenelec should prepare a work programme and submit it to the Commission.

<a id="rec-19"></a>
(19) In order to enable the Commission to better monitor the requested standardisation work, CEN and Cenelec should provide the Commission with access to an overall project plan containing detailed information on the execution of the standardisation request and should report regularly on the execution of that request.

<a id="rec-20"></a>
(20) Experience shows that during execution of the standardisation request, it may be necessary to adjust the scope of the request or the deadlines set therein. CEN and Cenelec should therefore promptly report to the Commission if they consider that more time is required to draft the standards than initially foreseen or that it is appropriate to adapt the scope of the request, in order to allow the Commission to take appropriate action.

<a id="rec-21"></a>
(21) In accordance with Article 10(3) of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 each standardisation request is subject to acceptance by the relevant European standardisation organisation. It is therefore necessary to provide for the rules on validity of this request if it is not accepted by CEN or Cenelec.

<a id="rec-22"></a>
(22) In order to ensure legal certainty as to the validity of the request after its execution, it is appropriate to provide for a date of expiry of this Decision.

<a id="rec-23"></a>
(23) Given that several harmonised standards have been drafted on the basis of standardisation mandate M/417, and that this Decision provides for a basis to complete work on draft harmonised standards based on that mandate, it is appropriate to provide for the end of validity of the standardisation mandate M/417.

<a id="rec-24"></a>
(24) The ESOs, the relevant European stakeholders' organisations receiving Union financing, European cooperation for Accreditation established by Article 14 of Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 and the expert group on the Internal Market for Products have been consulted.

<a id="rec-25"></a>
(25) The measures provided for in this Decision are in accordance with the opinion of the Committee established by Article 22 of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012,

HAS ADOPTED THIS DECISION:

<a id="art-1"></a>
### Article 1 – Requested standardisation activities

The European Committee for Standardisation (CEN) and the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation (Cenelec) are requested to draft new harmonised standards in the areas listed in Table 1 of Annex I to this Decision and to revise existing standards listed in Table 2 of Annex I to this Decision, in support of Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 and the Union legislation listed in point 2 of Annex II to this Decision, as regards accreditation bodies and conformity assessment bodies, by the deadlines set in Annex I.

The standards referred to in Table 2 of Annex I shall be based on standards of International Organization for Standardisation (ISO) and of the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC). The execution of this request in relation to the standards concerned shall be subject to the availability of new or revised standards adopted by ISO or IEC in time to complete the work on the relevant standard.

The standards referred to in the first paragraph shall meet the requirements set out in Annex II.

CEN and Cenelec shall provide the Commission with the titles of the requested harmonised standards in all the official languages of the Union.

<a id="art-2"></a>
### Article 2 – Work programme

CEN and Cenelec shall prepare a joint work programme indicating all the standards listed in Annex I, the responsible technical bodies and a timetable for the execution of the requested standardisation activities in line with the deadlines set out in that Annex.

CEN and Cenelec shall submit the draft joint work programme to the Commission by 10 months after the adoption of this Decision by the Commission. CEN and Cenelec shall inform the Commission of any amendments to the joint work programme.

CEN and Cenelec shall provide the Commission with access to an overall project plan.

<a id="art-3"></a>
### Article 3 – Reporting

1. CEN and Cenelec shall report annually to the Commission on the execution of the request referred to in Article 1, indicating the progress made in the implementation of the work programme referred to in Article 2.

2. CEN and Cenelec shall submit the first joint annual report to the Commission by 18 months after the adoption of this Decision by the Commission. Subsequent joint annual reports shall be submitted to the Commission by 28th February each year.

3. CEN and Cenelec shall provide the Commission with the final joint reports by 28th February 2031.

4. CEN and Cenelec shall promptly report to the Commission any major concerns relating to the scope of the request referred to in Article 1 or the deadlines set out in Annex I.

<a id="art-4"></a>
### Article 4 – Validity of the standardisation request

If CEN or Cenelec do not accept the request referred to in Article 1 within a month of receiving it, the request may not constitute a basis for the standardisation activities referred to in that Article.

This Decision shall expire on 31 December 2030.

<a id="art-5"></a>
### Article 5 – Expiry of existing standardisation request

The standardisation mandate M/417 of 4 December 2007 shall expire.

<a id="art-6"></a>
### Article 6 – Addressees

This Decision is addressed to the European Committee for Standardisation and the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation.

Done at Brussels, 17.12.2021

## Annex II – General requirements for standards listed in Annex I

1. Legal requirements to be supported by the requested standards

The requested standards shall support the application of relevant requirements to accreditation bodies and conformity assessment bodies set out in the Regulation (EC) 765/2008 and in the Union legislation listed in point 2 of this Annex.

The standards shall provide detailed technical specifications of requirements with regard to accreditation bodies and conformity assessment bodies, with the purpose of allowing compliance with relevant requirements of the Union harmonisation legislation.

The structure of a harmonised standard shall be such that a clear distinction can be made between those clauses and sub-clauses, which are necessary for compliance with the legal requirements and those which are not. The requirements shall be taken into account from the beginning of and throughout the entire process of developing standards.

When a standard does not cover all the legal requirements which are applicable to the accreditation and conformity assessment bodies falling under its scope, the standard shall indicate the legal requirements applicable to those accreditation and conformity assessment bodies that are not covered by it. Where a standard contains technical specifications, which do not support the application of legal requirements as set out in Regulation (EC) 765/2008 nor in sectoral legislation listed in point 2 of this Annex, such technical specifications shall be clearly distinguished from the specifications supporting the legal requirements.

CEN and Cenelec shall include in each harmonised standard a description of the relationship between its content and this standardisation request. Each harmonised standard developed on the basis of the request referred to in Article 1 of this Decision shall refer to this Decision.

For each harmonised standard CEN and Cenelec shall describe the extent to which it aims to cover one or several requirements set out in Regulation (EC) 765/2008 and in relevant Union acts listed in point 2 of this Annex.

CEN and Cenelec shall include in each revised standard, information on all changes that were introduced in that standard.

Standards shall be developed or revised according to the best practices available.
