WK 17096/2026 INIT **EU Space Act proposal – October 2026 Presidency compromise text –** **explanations**

**Overview of main changes**

Major changes and policy choices in this third compromise text include the following.

- • **Broader carve-outs from for scope.** The criterion in Article 2(3) of ‘exclusively used for defence or national security purposes’ is now supplemented by others:

o ‘operated by or under the control of military or defence authorities …’

o ‘owned by the military or defence authorities’

o transit flights corresponding to operational space services beyond the graveyard orbit.

- • **Exemption for temporary use for military or defence.** Article 2(3a) contains an exemption from EUSA’s technical requirements for ‘space objects … that are used temporarily for conducting defence or military operations or are placed temporarily under operational control of military, defence or national security authorities … for the duration of those operations’.

- • **New optional self-certification regime.**

o A new Article 7a introduces an option for a Member State to self-certify compliance of certain spacecraft or constellations with the technical requirements of the EU Space Act without involving a Qualified Technical Body.

o Compared to the annex to the IE PRES ‘orientation paper’ of July 2026, Article 7a widens the availability of the self-certification option, by adding to the list of criteria opening the self-certification option:

  - ▪ effective control by a governmental entity over a constellation; and ▪ joint missions involving third-country public entities.

- • **Reworked regime for space-based data**.

o The former distinction between, on the one hand, the sole service or decisive part of services and, on the other, ‘ancillary’ parts of services was deleted. It was considered likely to prove in practice to be an unworkable means of ensuring proportionality. Instead, two workable control channels were introduced to guarantee proportionality in practice, as follows.

  - ▪ Article 23a(1) and (3) now focus solely on the provision of space-based data concerning Earth observation to Member State contracting authorities as defined in EU public procurement rules. That category of space-based data accounts for a significant portion of the number of satellites in orbit.

  - ▪ Article 23a(4) requires the Member-State authorities that have competence in respect of landing rights to take into consideration, when considering whether to issue general authorisations or to grant individual rights of use, whether the space activities of the space object generating given satellite communications services are registered in URSA. o Article 23a(2) retains the exclusion of the provision of space-based data for purely research or educational purposes.

- • **Technical requirements are clarified and streamlined**.

o Safety requirements have been streamlined for simplicity, flexibility and practicability.

  - ▪ For example, space debris mitigation requirements have been shortened significantly.

o Concerning the environmental sustainability of space activities, life-cycle assessment obligations are limited to ‘accepted international practices’, while Union space operators may choose voluntarily – perhaps for ‘EU Taxonomy’ reasons – to calculate the life-cycle assessment in accordance with the EU environmental footprint method. If that method were to be recognised in the future by international standardisation organisations, it would become obligatory.

- • **More is left to the procedural autonomy of each Member State**.

o Article 30, concerning Member States’ supervisory powers, is shortened significantly, with Article 30(3), (4a), (7a) and (7b) being deleted.

o Article 34, on the process for becoming a national Qualified Technical body, is deleted in its entirety. Article 32a(4) is also deleted.

o Market surveillance by Member States is now covered separately in a new Article 39a that leaves the detail to each Member State.

- • **Closer link between the EUSA certification of Union-owned spacecraft and the** **Member States of establishment of their operators.**

o A new Article 44(2b) specifies that, in respect of Union space operators of Union-owned spacecraft, the rules of procedure are to include case attribution rules within the Compliance Board that take as their point of departure the place or places of establishment of the applicant for EUSA certification.

- • **Third-country launch operators.**

o Pursuant to the amended Article 15(2), third-country launch operators now must comply only with the same requirements for ‘Space debris mitigation for launch vehicles’ as those laid down in Article 61 in respect of Union launch services.

o Third-country launch operators are thus no longer caught by Art.75a on cybersecurity requirements, or by Title IV, Chapter II, on environmental sustainability for space activities.

- • **Role of ‘the Agency’.** Adjustments include the following.

o A new Article 7b redrafts the procedure under the previous Article 7a for logical flow and practicability (details in the table below).

o Article 17 is edited to avoid the Agency appearing to pre-judge the Commission’s decision on EUSA verification in respect of third-country space operators.

o Article 48 drops the possibility of a delegation to the Agency, for the purposes of technical assessments, of the Commission’s investigatory powers.

- • **Equivalent or mutually recognised non-EU jurisdictions**.

o Changes in Article 17a clarify requirements applicable to relevant third-country space operators.

o Article 105 now specifies that equivalence of non-EU regimes is to be assessed on the basis of overall outcomes.

- • **Provisions concerning international organisations**. Article 18, on the EUSA registration of international organisations, is deleted.
