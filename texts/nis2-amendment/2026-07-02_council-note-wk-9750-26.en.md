ES (Comments):

Although, formally, the amendment to Article 21(5) may appear limited, its substantive scope is significant, as it directly affects the balance between European harmonisation and the regulatory capacity of Member States in the field of cybersecurity.

The current version of the NIS2 Directive empowers the Commission to establish technical and methodological requirements for a limited set of digital providers, characterised by a markedly cross-border dimension. The new wording potentially extends that power to all essential and important entities falling within the scope of the Directive.

This extension, combined with the prohibition on Member States imposing additional technical, methodological or sectoral requirements, could have an excluding effect on existing national frameworks. Therefore, the proposed amendment does not seek to call into question the usefulness of European harmonisation, but rather to prevent it from resulting in the complete replacement of national frameworks. The objective is to preserve a model compatible with the NIS2 Directive, based on multi-level cooperation, progressive convergence and the recognition of national instruments that already contribute effectively to raising the level of cybersecurity.
