# COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING REGULATION (EU) …/... of XXX laying down rules for the application of Regulation (EU) 2024/1735 of the European Parliament and of the Council as regards the template for Member States’ reporting

COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING REGULATION (EU) …/...

of XXX

laying down rules for the application of Regulation (EU) 2024/1735 of the European Parliament and of the Council as regards the template for Member States’ reporting

(Text with EEA relevance)

THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION,

Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union,

Having regard to Regulation (EU) 2024/1735 ^(1) of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 on establishing a framework of measures for strengthening Europe’s net-zero technology manufacturing ecosystem and amending Regulation (EU) 2018/1724, and in particular Article 42(5) thereof,

After consulting the Net-zero technologies Committee,

Whereas:

## Recitals

<a id="rec-1"></a>
(1) Regulation (EU) 2024/1735 sets out as general objective to improve the functioning of the internal market by establishing a framework to ensure the Union’s access to a secure and sustainable supply of net-zero technologies.

<a id="rec-2"></a>
(2) Under Article 42(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1735, the Commission is to monitor the Union’s progress in achieving that objective. For that purpose, Member States are to collect and report to the Commission the data listed in Article 42(2), second subparagraph, of that Regulation.

<a id="rec-3"></a>
(3) To ensure a consistent approach across Member States in collecting and providing such data, it is necessary to provide a template for that report. The template should contain the structure of the report, clarify the requested information and provide explanations to be used for submitting the report to ensure a harmonised reporting and comparable data across Member States.

<a id="rec-4"></a>
(4) The measures provided for in this Regulation are in accordance with the opinion of the Net-zero technologies Committee,

HAS ADOPTED THIS REGULATION:

<a id="art-1"></a>
### Article 1

The template for the Member States’ report referred to in Article 42 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1735 is laid down in the Annex to this Regulation.

<a id="art-2"></a>
### Article 2

This Regulation shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in the Official Journal of the European Union.

This Regulation shall be binding in its entirety and directly applicable in all Member States.

Done at Brussels,

For the Commission The President Ursula VON DER LEYEN

## Annex

This Annex provides the template for the reports referred to in Article 42(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1735. It defines the information, structure, and technical details to be collected and provided by Member States on net-zero technologies, with a table for each of the types of data referred to in Article 42(2), second paragraph, of that Regulation.

For the purposes of this Annex, the following instructions apply:

1. The green cells in the tables identify the reporting fields for which the Member States shall provide data.

2. The list of net-zero technologies included in the reporting tables in the Annex is non-exhaustive and covers only a subset of the massmanufactured net-zero technologies listed in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1178. Member States are invited to provide data on other net-zero technologies not included in the list where they have relevant information. To provide information for additional net-zero technologies, Member States shall insert additional rows at the end of the table, indicating clearly the technology and, where applicable, the component concerned. Where a Member State has no data to report for a net-zero technology included in the tables, it shall indicate “N/A”. This convention applies to all tables in the Annex.

3. YEAR Y is the most recent year covered by the data submission; YEAR Y-1 is the year immediately preceding YEAR Y; and YEAR Y-2 is the year immediately preceding YEAR Y-1. This convention shall apply to all the following tables where several years are indicated. For example, for the first reporting deadline of 15 March 2027, the three-year reference period shall in principle cover 2024, 2025 and 2026, with 2026 corresponding to YEAR Y, 2025 to YEAR Y-1, and 2024 to YEAR Y-2. With regard to 2024, Member States are not required to report the data for the period before 29 June 2024, which is the date from which Regulation (EU) 2024/1735 applies. Member States may, however, report such data on a voluntary basis. To ensure clarity, Member States shall replace, in all relevant tables, the placeholders ‘YEAR Y’, ‘YEAR Y-1’ and ‘YEAR Y-2’ with the actual calendar years to which the data provided refers

4. Member States shall collect and provide data and evidence to the Commission in a way that does not entail, or entails minimal, administrative burden on businesses. For that purpose, Member States are strongly encouraged to make use of readily available information, such as from relevant national or European sectoral associations, as well as from industrial alliances. Before requesting information to individual undertakings, Member States shall thoroughly assess whether the same or comparable data is already collected under any existing reporting or monitoring frameworks, including, where relevant, the National Energy and Climate Plans, in order to avoid duplication of data collection and reporting requirements. Member States shall limit direct requests of information from individual undertakings to cases where the relevant sectoral or project-specific information cannot reasonably be obtained from publicly available sources.

- (a) Single market obstacles (that is, obstacles affecting the functioning of the EU internal market, including those arising from divergent or fragmented rules, procedures or practices across Member States): divergent technical specifications or standards; fragmented authorisation, permitting or administrative procedures; delays in customs, import clearance or border-related procedures across Member States affecting inputs or final goods; public procurement requirements that create market fragmentation, divergent national rules on labelling, information or traceability.
