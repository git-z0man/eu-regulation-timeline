# COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING DECISION of 5.8.2022 on a standardisation request to the European Committee for Standardisation and the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation as regards radio equipment in support of Directive 2014/53/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 (Only the English, French and German texts are authentic) THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION, Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, Having regard to Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 25 October 2012 on European standardisation, amending Council Directives 89/686/EEC and 93/15/EEC and Directives 94/9/EC, 94/25/EC, 95/16/EC, 97/23/EC, 98/34/EC, 2004/22/EC, 2007/23/EC, 2009/23/EC and 2009/105/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council and repealing Council Decision 87/95/EEC and Decision No 1673/2006/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(1), and in particular Article 10(1) thereof,

COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING DECISION

of 5.8.2022

on a standardisation request to the European Committee for Standardisation and the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation as regards radio equipment in support of Directive 2014/53/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30

(Only the English, French and German texts are authentic)

THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION,

Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union,

Having regard to Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 25 October 2012 on European standardisation, amending Council Directives 89/686/EEC and 93/15/EEC and Directives 94/9/EC, 94/25/EC, 95/16/EC, 97/23/EC, 98/34/EC, 2004/22/EC, 2007/23/EC, 2009/23/EC and 2009/105/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council and repealing Council Decision 87/95/EEC and Decision No 1673/2006/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(1), and in particular Article 10(1) thereof,

Whereas:

## Recitals

<a id="rec-1"></a>
(1) Directive 2014/53/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(2) establishes a regulatory framework for the making available on the market and putting into service in the Union of radio equipment. The essential requirements for such equipment are laid down in Article 3(3), first paragraph, of that Directive. Pursuant to Article 3(3), second paragraph, of Directive 2014/53/EU, the Commission is to specify which categories or classes of radio equipment are concerned by each of the essential requirements set out in Article 3(3), first subparagraph, points (a) to (i), of that Directive.

<a id="rec-2"></a>
(2) Article 3(3), points (d), (e) and (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU aim at ensuring that the concerned radio equipment protect the user from cyber security risks. Article 3(3), point (d), of Directive 2014/53/EU provides that radio equipment is not to harm the network or its functioning nor misuse network resources, thereby causing an unacceptable degradation of service. Article 3(3), point (e), of Directive 2014/53/EU provides that radio equipment is to incorporate safeguards to ensure that the personal data and privacy of the user and of the subscriber are protected. Article 3(3), point (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU provides that radio equipment is to support certain features ensuring protection from fraud.

<a id="rec-3"></a>
(3) Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 ^(3) specifies that the essential requirement set out in Article 3(3), point (d), of Directive 2014/53/EU is applicable to internet-connected radio equipment.

<a id="rec-4"></a>
(4) Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 specifies that the essential requirement set out in Article 3(3), point (e), of Directive 2014/53/EU is applicable to the following categories of radio equipment capable of processing, within the meaning of Article 4, point (2), of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(4), personal data, as defined in that Regulation, or traffic data and location data, as defined in Directive 2002/58/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(5): radio equipment designed or intended exclusively for childcare; radio equipment covered by Directive 2009/48/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(6); radio equipment designed or intended, whether exclusively or not exclusively, to be worn on, strapped to, or hung from any part of the human body, including the head, neck, trunk, arms, hands, legs and feet, or any clothing, including headwear, hand wear and footwear, which is worn by human beings; internet-connected radio equipment, other than the equipment previously referred in this paragraph.

<a id="rec-5"></a>
(5) Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 specifies that the essential requirement set out in Article 3(3), point (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU is applicable to any internet-connected radio equipment, if that equipment enables the holder or user to transfer money, monetary value or virtual currency as defined in Article 2, point (d), of Directive (EU) 2019/713 of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(7).

<a id="rec-6"></a>
(6) In accordance with Article 16 of Directive 2014/53/EU, radio equipment which is in conformity with harmonised standards or parts thereof the references of which have been published in the Official Journal of the European Union is to be presumed to be in conformity with the essential requirements of that Directive covered by those standards or parts thereof.

<a id="rec-7"></a>
(7) Harmonised standards will help ensure the protection of the network, of personal data and privacy and protection against fraud throughout the Union and thus contribute to the free movement of certain radio equipment in the Union. Given that such standards are to be technology neutral and performance-based, they will also contribute to ensuring equal conditions of competition among relevant economic operators, in particular small and medium-sized enterprises. Indirectly those standards will also contribute to lower production costs, benefitting in particular the consumers, and to ensuring technical interoperability.

<a id="rec-8"></a>
(8) The intention to request drafting of harmonised standards in support of Directive 2014/53/EU is stated in point 65 of the table of the Annex to the annual Union work programme for European standardisation for 2022 ^(8).

<a id="rec-9"></a>
(9) The European Committee for Standardisation (CEN) and the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation (CENELEC) have indicated that the work covered by the standardisation request falls within their area of competence.

<a id="rec-10"></a>
(10) The Commission has issued two standardisation requests in support of Directive 2014/53/EU. By means of Implementing Decision C(2015) 5376 ^(9), the Commission made a request to the CENELEC and the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) for the drafting and revision of harmonised standards for radio equipment in support of Article 3 of Directive 2014/53/EU and by means of Implementing Decision C(2020) 6628 ^(10), the Commission made a request to ETSI for the drafting of harmonised standards for certain hand-held mobile phones in support of Article 3(3), point (g) of Directive 2014/53/EU in conjunction with Article 1 of Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/320 ^(11). However, these two Implementing Decisions do not cover the essential requirements set out in Article 3(3), points (d), (e) and (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU.

<a id="rec-11"></a>
(11) It is therefore appropriate to request CEN and CENELEC to draft new harmonised standards in support of Article 3(3), points (d), (e) and (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU for the radio equipment of the categories and classes specified by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30.

<a id="rec-12"></a>
(12) The harmonised standards should include detailed technical specifications, including appropriate test methods or equivalent verifiable and reproducible approaches, in support of the essential requirements set out in Article 3(3), points (d), (e) and (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU regarding the radio equipment concerned by those essential requirements pursuant to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30. The harmonised standards should also indicate clearly the correspondence between the technical specifications and the essential requirements they aim to cover. They should be based on risk assessment and risk reduction methodologies and reflect the generally acknowledged state of the art. Harmonised standards should also indicate clearly the relevant identified risks that are not addressed.

<a id="rec-13"></a>
(13) CEN and CENELEC should ensure that the technical solutions that they select in the respective harmonised standards that they develop are equally effective in addressing the same risks.

<a id="rec-14"></a>
(14) The technical solutions laid down in the harmonised standards should be proportionate to the risk that they aim to address and mitigate, taking into account the outcome of the risk analysis and the effectiveness of the technical decisions taken by the CEN and CENELEC, in view of the fulfilment of the essential requirements laid down by Directive (EU) 2014/53/EU, as supplemented by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30.The harmonised standards developed in support of the essential requirements laid down in Article 3(3), points (d), (e) and (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU should lay down technical solutions which would not prejudice compliance with other Union legal acts in the area of cybersecurity, processing of personal data and privacy of users and protection against fraud, including Regulations (EU) 910/2014 ^(12) and (EU) 2019/881 ^(13) of the European Parliament and of the Council, Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and Directive (EU) 2016/1148 of the European Parliament and of the Council. ^(14) It is therefore necessary to require that CEN and CENELEC ensure consistency with the upcoming regulatory developments and establish arrangements for close cooperation between CEN and CENELEC and the Commission for that purpose.

<a id="rec-15"></a>
(15) Information as to which essential requirements are covered by a harmonised standard is necessary when assessing, in accordance with Article 10(5) of Regulation (EU) 1025/2012, the compliance of the documents drafted by the European standardisation organisations. Such information is also necessary before publication of references of harmonised standards in the Official Journal of the European Union in accordance with Article 10(6) of Regulation (EU) 1025/2012. In each harmonised standard CEN and CENELEC should therefore describe the extent to which that standard aims to cover one or several essential requirements set out in Article 3(3), points (d), (e) and (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU in conjunction with Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30.

<a id="rec-16"></a>
(16) In order to ensure transparency and facilitate the execution of the requested standardisation activities CEN and CENELEC should prepare a work programme and submit it to the Commission.

<a id="rec-17"></a>
(17) In order to enable the Commission to better monitor the requested standardisation work, CEN and CENELEC should provide the Commission with access to an overall project plan containing detailed information on the execution of the standardisation request.

<a id="rec-18"></a>
(18) Experience shows that during execution of the standardisation request, it may be necessary to adjust the scope of the request or the deadlines set therein. CEN and CENELEC should therefore promptly report to the Commission if they consider that more time is required to draft the standards than initially foreseen or that it is appropriate to adapt the scope of the request, in order to allow the Commission to take appropriate action.

<a id="rec-19"></a>
(19) In accordance with Article 10(3) of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 each standardisation request is subject to acceptance by the relevant European standardisation organisation. It is therefore necessary to provide for the rules on validity of this request if it is not accepted by CEN or CENELEC.

<a id="rec-20"></a>
(20) In order to ensure legal certainty as to the validity of the request after its execution, it is appropriate to provide for a date of expiry of this Decision.

<a id="rec-21"></a>
(21) The European standardisation organisations, the European stakeholders' organisations receiving Union financing, the Commission Expert Group on Radio Equipment and the Telecommunication Conformity Assessment and Market Surveillance Committee established by Article 45 of Directive 2014/53/EU have been consulted.

<a id="rec-22"></a>
(22) The measures provided for in this Decision are in accordance with the opinion of the Committee established by Article 22 of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012.

<a id="rec-23"></a>
(23) The European Cybersecurity Certification Group has been established by the Regulation (EU) 2019/881 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 17 April 2019. Cooperation with this group on cybersecurity matters will be pursued.

<a id="rec-24"></a>
(24) Cooperation on enforceability of the technical specifications with the Administrative Cooperation group of market surveillance authorities for radio equipment (ADCO RED) will be pursued,

HAS ADOPTED THIS DECISION:

<a id="art-1"></a>
### Article 1 – Requested standardisation activities

1. The European Committee for Standardisation (CEN) and the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation (CENELEC) are requested to draft the harmonised standards listed in Annex I to this Decision in support of the essential requirements set out in Article 3(3), points (d), (e) and (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU, for the radio equipment of the categories and classes specified by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 by the deadlines set in that Annex I.

2. The standards, referred to in paragraph 1, shall meet the requirements set out in Annex II.

3. CEN and CENELEC shall provide the Commission with the titles of the requested harmonised standards in all the official languages of the Union.

<a id="art-2"></a>
### Article 2 – Work programme

1. CEN and CENELEC shall prepare a work programme indicating all the standards listed in Annex I, the responsible technical bodies and a timetable for the execution of the requested standardisation activities in line with the deadlines set out in that Annex.

2. CEN and CENELEC shall submit the draft work programme to the Commission by 7 October 2022. CEN and CENELEC shall inform the Commission of any amendments to the work programme.

3. CEN and CENELEC shall provide the Commission with access to an overall project plan.

4. The project plan shall include arrangements ensuring cooperation between CEN and CENELEC and shall ensure cooperation with the Commission for the execution of the requested standardisation activities.

<a id="art-3"></a>
### Article 3 – Reporting

1. CEN and CENELEC shall report annually to the Commission on the execution of the request referred to in Article 1, indicating the progress made in implementation of the work programme referred to in Article 2.

2. The report shall refer to technical requirements adopted at Union level which CEN and CENELEC have taken into consideration or which they intend to take into consideration for the development of the harmonised standards in accordance with this Decision, including cybersecurity certification schemes referred to in Regulation (EU) 2019/881 and certification mechanisms and codes of conduct referred to respectively in Articles 42 and 40 of Regulation (EU) 2016/679.

3. CEN and CENELEC shall submit the first annual report to the Commission by 31 December 2022. Subsequent annual reports shall be submitted by 31 December each year.

4. CEN and CENELEC shall provide the Commission with the final report by 31 December 2027.

5. CEN and CENELEC shall promptly report to the Commission any major concerns relating to the scope of the request referred to in Article 1 or the deadlines set out in Annex I.

<a id="art-4"></a>
### Article 4 – Validity of the standardisation request

Where, in accordance with Article 10(3) of Regulation (EU) 1025/2012, CEN or CENELEC indicates that it does not accept the request referred to in Article 1(1) of this Decision, that request may not constitute a basis for the standardisation activities referred to in that Article 1 of this Decision for the standardisation organisation concerned.

This Decision shall expire on 30 September 2024.

<a id="art-5"></a>
### Article 5 – Addressee

This Decision is addressed to the European Committee for Standardisation and the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation.

Done at Brussels, 5.8.2022

<a id="annex-ii"></a>
## Annex II – Requirements for the harmonised standards referred to in Article 1

Part A. General requirements for the harmonised standards listed in Annex I

1. Legal requirements to be supported by the harmonised standards

1.1. Each harmonised standard shall include the following elements:

- (a) detailed technical specifications in support of the essential requirements set out in Article 3(3), points (d), (e) and (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU for the categories and classes specified by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 with the purpose of allowing compliance with the essential requirements set out in those points;

- (b) test methods or equivalent approaches and conditions to verify compliance of the radio equipment with the corresponding specifications.

The elements listed in points (a) and (b) shall be described in an exhaustive manner and objectively and independently verifiable.

1.2. The harmonised standards shall only provide technical solutions in support of the relevant essential requirements set out in Article 3(3), points (d), (e) and (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU for the purpose of benefiting from a presumption of conformity in accordance with Article 16 of that Directive.

1.3. The harmonised standards shall contain only product-specific provisions, including methods for the verification of compliance with such provisions. The harmonised standards shall not:

- (a) make, in their normative part, any references to any of the requirements or procedures set out in Directive 2014/53/EU or delegated acts adopted pursuant to Articles 3(3), 4(2) or 5(2) of that Directive or implementing acts adopted pursuant to Articles 2(2), 4(3), 5(3), 8(2), 10(10), 33(4), 41(1) or 42(4) of that Directive;

- (b) reproduce, in their normative part, any of the requirements or procedures set out in Directive 2014/53/EU or delegated acts adopted pursuant to Articles 3(3), 4(2) or 5(2) of that Directive or implementing acts adopted pursuant to Articles 2(2), 4(3), 5(3), 8(2), 10(10), 33(4), 41(1) or 42(4) of that Directive;

- (c) use terms in a meaning departing from that given to those terms in the definitions set out in Directive 2014/53/EU or delegated acts adopted pursuant to Articles 3(3), 4(2) or 5(2) of that Directive or implementing acts adopted pursuant to Articles 2(2), 4(3), 5(3), 8(2), 10(10), 33(4), 41(1) or 42(4) of that Directive;

- (d) define any terms used but not defined in Directive 2014/53/EU or delegated acts adopted pursuant to Articles 3(3), 4(2) or 5(2) of that Directive or implementing acts adopted pursuant to Articles 2(2), 4(3), 5(3), 8(2), 10(10), 33(4), 41(1) or 42(4) of that Directive;

- (e) contradict any provisions of Directive 2014/53/EU or delegated acts adopted pursuant to Articles 3(3), 4(2) or 5(2) of that Directive or implementing acts adopted pursuant to Articles 2(2), 4(3), 5(3), 8(2), 10(10), 33(4), 41(1) or 42(4) of that Directive;

- (f) make conformity with the standards dependent on requirements of an administrative or organisational nature.

1.4. The structure of the harmonised standards shall be such that a clear distinction can be made between those clauses and sub-clauses of the standards which need to be complied with in order to benefit from the presumption of conformity and those the compliance with which does not allow to benefit from this mechanism. Where a standard contains clauses or sub-clauses, compliance with which does not allow to benefit from a presumption of conformity, such clauses or sub-clauses shall not compromise or prejudice the compliance with any of the clauses or sub-clauses, which enable to benefit from the mechanism of presumption of conformity.

1.5. Each harmonised standard shall clearly indicate its scope and the products which fall under its scope. Where a harmonised standard does not cover all the essential requirements which are applicable to the products falling under its scope, the standard shall indicate the essential requirements not covered. Where a harmonised standard does not mitigate major risks identified after an exhaustive analysis, which relate to one of the essential requirements it aims to cover and which apply to the products falling under its scope, the standard shall indicate the major risks not mitigated.

1.6. Each harmonised standard shall include a clear and precise description of the relationship between its content and the essential requirements set out in Article 3 (3), point (d), (e) or (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU, that it aims to cover.

1.7. Each harmonised standard shall refer to this Decision.

Part B. Specific requirements for the harmonised standards listed in Annex I

1. Requirements for all harmonised standards

1.1. The harmonised standards shall reflect the generally acknowledged state of art.

1.2. The technical solutions laid down in the harmonised standards shall be proportionate to the risk that they aim to address. The harmonised standards shall be drafted and revised by applying the iterative process of risk assessment and risk reduction.

1.3. Test methods shall be verifiable, objective and reproducible to ensure comparable verification of the technical specifications for all the products covered by the harmonised standards. Alternative mechanisms to test methods shall be developed only in case there is a demonstrated technical impossibility to implement test methods and when duly justified. If deemed appropriate, a harmonised standard may define different provisions for different subcategories of radio equipment.

1.4. When Union legal acts address aspects related to the essential requirements laid down in Article 3(3), points (d), (e) and (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU, CEN and CENELEC shall select in the harmonised standard those technical solutions that are also compatible with the provisions of those Union legal acts laying down specific requirements regarding cybersecurity, the protection of personal data and privacy of users or the protection from fraud. Compliance with the harmonised standards should not prejudice the compliance with the provisions laying down specific requirements regarding cybersecurity, the protection of personal data and privacy of users or the protection from fraud of other Union legal acts, which address aspects related to the essential requirements laid down in Article 3(3), points (d), (e) and (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU.

1.5. Where European cybersecurity certification schemes provided for pursuant to Article 49(7) of Regulation (EU) 2019/881 cover any of the categories or classes of radio equipment, which fall within the scope of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30, the harmonised standards which concern those categories or classes of radio equipment shall be developed in a coherent manner with the product-specific content of those schemes.

1.6. Where codes of conduct which have been subject to an opinion of the European Data Protection Board pursuant to Article 40(7) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and made publicly available pursuant to Article 40(11) of that Regulation have as their subject matter rules for controllers and processors that process personal data using any of the categories or classes of radio equipment, which fall within the scope of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30, the harmonised standards which concern those categories or classes of radio equipment shall be developed in a coherent manner with the product-specific content of those codes of conduct.

1.7. Where established common certification mechanisms on the basis of criteria approved by the European Data Protection Board under Article 42(5) of Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and which have been made publicly available pursuant to Article 42(8) of that Regulation have as their subject matter the processing of personal data using any of the categories or classes of radio equipment, which fall within the scope of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30, the harmonised standards which concern those categories or classes of radio equipment shall be developed in a coherent manner with the product-specific content of those common certification mechanisms.

1.8. CEN and CENELEC shall ensure that when they develop technical solutions aimed at addressing the same risks, their respective technical solutions have the same level of effectiveness in addressing the relevant risk.

2. Requirements for specific standards

2.1. Harmonised standards in support of the essential requirement set out in Article 3(3), point (d), of Directive 2014/53/EU for the categories and classes specified by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 shall contain technical specifications that ensure at least that those radio equipment, where applicable:

- (a) include elements to monitor and control network traffic, including the transmission of outgoing data;

- (b) are designed to mitigate the effects of ongoing denial of service attacks;

- (c) implement appropriate authentication and access control mechanisms;

- (d) are provided, on a risk basis, with up-to-date software and hardware at the moment of placing on the market that do not contain publicly known exploitable vulnerabilities as regards harm to the network or its functioning or misuse of network resources;

- (e) are provided with automated and secure mechanisms for updating software or firmware that allow, when necessary, the mitigation of vulnerabilities that if exploited may lead to the radio equipment harming the network or its functioning or the misuse of network resources;

- (f) protect the exposed attack surfaces and minimise the impact of successful attacks.

2.2. Harmonised standards in support of the essential requirement set out in Article 3(3), point (e), of Directive 2014/53/EU for the categories and classes specified by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 shall contain technical specifications that ensure at least that those radio equipment, where applicable:

- (a) protect stored, transmitted or otherwise processed personal data against accidental or unauthorised processing, including storage, access, disclosure, destruction, loss or alteration or lack of availability;

- (b) implement appropriate authentication and access control mechanisms;

- (c) are provided, on a risk basis, with up-to-date software and hardware at the moment of placing on the market that do not contain publicly known exploitable vulnerabilities as regards data protection and privacy;

- (d) are provided with automated and secure mechanisms for updating software or firmware that allow, when necessary, the mitigation of vulnerabilities that if exploited may lead to unauthorised processing, including storage, access, disclosure, destruction, loss or alteration or lack of availability of personal data;

- (e) include functionalities to inform the user of changes that may affect data protection and privacy;

- (f) log the internal activity that can have an impact on data protection and privacy;

- (g) allow users to easily delete their stored personal data, enabling the disposal or replacement of equipment without the risk of exposing personal data;

- (h) protect the exposed attack surfaces and minimise the impact of successful attacks.

2.2.1. In addition to requirements laid down in point 2.2, harmonised standards in support of the essential requirement set out in Article 3(3), point (e), of Directive 2014/53/EU for the categories and classes specified by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 shall contain technical specifications that ensure at least that:

- (a) equipment, including hand-held mobile telephones with features similar to those of a computer in terms of capability to process data, which is subject to the requirements laid down in Articles 8.3, 29, 30 and 39 of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, complies with the essential requirement set out in Article 3(3), point (e), when it applies to such radio equipment pursuant to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30. CEN and CENELEC shall select in the harmonised standards those technical solutions that do not prejudge the deployment of electronic identification systems and the provision of services regulated under Regulation (EU) No 910/2014. Such requirements shall be taken into consideration in order to ensure that the compliance of such radio equipment with the essential requirement set out in Article 3(3), point (e), would not be adversely affected;

- (b) radio equipment designed or intended exclusively for childcare and radio equipment covered by Directive 2009/48/EC, as defined in Article 1 of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30, to which the essential requirement set out in Article 3(3), point (e), applies pursuant to that Regulation, shall avoid any hazards related to remote communication, observation and control of the child and unauthorised communications or interactions to their user;

- (c) smart meters used for decentralised smart grids in the field of energy and 5G network equipment used by providers of public electronic communications networks and publicly available electronic communications services within the meaning of Directive (EU) 2018/1972 to which the essential requirement set out in Article 3(3), point (e), applies pursuant to Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 do not undermine the high level of security requested at national level.

2.3. Harmonised standards in support of the essential requirement set out in Article 3(3), point (f), of Directive 2014/53/EU for the categories and classes specified by Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 shall describe technical specifications that ensure at least that those radio equipment, where applicable:

- (a) protect stored, transmitted or otherwise processed financial or monetary data against accidental or unauthorised processing, including storage, access, disclosure, destruction, loss or alteration or lack of availability;

- (b) implement appropriate authentication and access control mechanisms;

- (c) are provided, on a risk basis, with up-to-date software and hardware at the moment of placing on the market that do not contain publicly known exploitable vulnerabilities as regards financial or monetary data;

- (d) are provided with automated and secure mechanisms for updating software or firmware that allow, when necessary, the mitigation of vulnerabilities that if exploited may lead to unauthorised processing, including storage, access, disclosure, destruction, loss or alteration or lack of availability of financial or monetary data;

- (e) log the internal activity that can have an impact on financial or monetary data;

- (f) protect the exposed attack surfaces and minimise the impact of successful attacks.
