# COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING DECISION of 26.2.2025 on a standardisation request to the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation as regards charging interface and charging communication protocol for radio equipment capable of being recharged by means of wireless charging, in support of Directive 2014/53/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council

COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING DECISION

of 26.2.2025

on a standardisation request to the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation as regards charging interface and charging communication protocol for radio equipment capable of being recharged by means of wireless charging, in support of Directive 2014/53/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council

(Text with EEA relevance)

(Only the English, French and German texts are authentic)

THE EUROPEAN COMMISSION,

Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union,

Having regard to Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 25 October 2012 on European standardisation, amending Council Directives 89/686/EEC and 93/15/EEC and Directives 94/9/EC, 94/25/EC, 95/16/EC, 97/23/EC, 98/34/EC, 2004/22/EC, 2007/23/EC, 2009/23/EC and 2009/105/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council and repealing Council Decision 87/95/EEC and Decision No 1673/2006/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(1) and in particular Article 10(1) thereof,

Having regard to Directive 2014/53/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 16 April 2014 on the harmonisation of the laws of the Member States relating to the making available on the market of radio equipment and repealing Directive 1999/5/EC ^(2), and in particular Article 3(4), sixth subparagraph, thereof,

Whereas:

## Recitals

<a id="rec-1"></a>
(1) Directive 2014/53/EU establishes a regulatory framework for the making available on the market and putting into service in the Union of radio equipment. The essential requirements for such equipment are laid down in Article 3 of that Directive.

<a id="rec-2"></a>
(2) Article 3(4) of Directive 2014/53/EU provides that radio equipment falling within categories or classes of radio equipment listed in Part I of Annex Ia to that Directive is to be constructed in such a way that it complies with the specifications relating to charging capabilities set out in that Annex.

<a id="rec-3"></a>
(3) Annex Ia to Directive 2014/53/EU contains specifications for radio equipment capable of being recharged by means of wired charging, harmonising the charging receptacle and the charging communication protocol for specific categories or classes of radio equipment.

<a id="rec-4"></a>
(4) The objectives of the harmonised charging solution for wired charging are to ensure a minimum common interoperability between radio equipment and its charging devices, as well as to improve consumer convenience, to reduce environmental waste and to avoid market fragmentation.

<a id="rec-5"></a>
(5) Over the years, different wireless charging (or wireless power transfer) technologies have emerged and continue to develop, such as capacitive power transfer (CPT), inductive power transfer (IPT), magnetic resonance power transfer (MRPT), radio frequency radiation power transfer (RFRPT), laser power transfer (LPT), and acoustic energy transfer (AET). However, to this day, there are no European or international standards for those technologies.

<a id="rec-6"></a>
(6) The IPT, MRPT and RFRPT technologies have gained a substantial market acceptance with an increasing number of industrial as well as consumer products embracing such technologies. The acceptance of those technologies results from their reasonable characteristics in terms of charging, such as the transfer distance range, the power levels, and the existing technical specifications.

<a id="rec-7"></a>
(7) While it is still premature to impose technical specifications on harmonised charging interfaces and charging communication protocols for radio equipment capable of being recharged by wireless charging at this stage, it is appropriate to develop specifications that could form the basis for their future harmonisation.

<a id="rec-8"></a>
(8) Article 3(4) of Directive 2014/53/EU requires the Commission to request, in accordance with Article 10(1) of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012, and by 28 December 2024, one or more European standardisation organisations to draft harmonised standards laying down technical specifications for the charging interface(s) and charging communication protocol(s) for radio equipment capable of being recharged by wireless charging.

<a id="rec-9"></a>
(9) However, there are currently no specific requirements, in Directive 2014/53/EU, for the harmonisation of charging interfaces and charging communication protocols for radio equipment capable of being recharged by wireless charging. Therefore, it is currently not possible to request harmonised standards, as provided for in Article 3(4) of Directive 2014/53/EU. It is therefore appropriate to request the drafting of European standards.

<a id="rec-10"></a>
(10) The intention to request drafting of European standards in support of Directive 2014/53/EU is stated in point 69 of the table entitled ‘Actions for the development and revision of European standards or European standardisation deliverables supporting the internal market for services and products’ set out in the Commission Notice ‘The 2024 annual Union work programme for European standardisation’ ^(3).

<a id="rec-11"></a>
(11) The European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation (CENELEC) has indicated that the work covered by the request falls within their area of competence.

<a id="rec-12"></a>
(12) It is therefore appropriate to request CENELEC to draft European standards laying down technical specifications for charging interface and charging communication protocol for radio equipment capable of being recharged by means of wireless charging.

<a id="rec-13"></a>
(13) The selected technical solutions should take into account the degree of market acceptance of those solutions, the resulting consumer convenience and the extent of the reduction of environmental waste and market fragmentation that can be expected from such technical solutions.

<a id="rec-14"></a>
(14) The European standards should include detailed technical specifications for a common and interoperable wireless charging interface and wireless charging communication protocol, including appropriate test methods or equivalent verifiable and reproducible approaches. The European standards should also indicate clearly the correspondence between the technical specifications and the aspects of this request they aim to cover. They should be based on risk assessment and risk reduction methodologies and reflect the generally acknowledged state of the art.

<a id="rec-15"></a>
(15) CENELEC should ensure that the selected technical solutions in the respective European standards that they develop are equally effective in addressing the objectives of the degree of market acceptance of the technical solutions, the resulting consumer convenience and the extent of the reduction of environmental waste and market fragmentation that can be expected from such technical solutions.

<a id="rec-16"></a>
(16) The technical solutions laid down in the European standards should be proportionate to the objectives they aim to address, taking into account the outcome of the risk analysis and the effectiveness of the technical decisions taken by CENELEC, in view of the fulfilment of this request. The European standards developed in support of this request should lay down technical solutions which would not prejudice compliance with other Union legal acts in the area of wireless charging. It is therefore necessary to require that CENELEC ensure consistency with the upcoming regulatory developments and establish arrangements for close cooperation between CENELEC and the Commission for that purpose.

<a id="rec-17"></a>
(17) In addition, the technical solutions laid down in the European standards should not compromise the compliance of the radio equipment with the applicable Union legal acts, including the equal performance regarding the applicable essential requirements set out in those Union acts, across the Union’s diverse population, i.e., regardless of gender, age, height as well as other anthropometric and biomechanical considerations.

<a id="rec-18"></a>
(18) The Commission together with the European standardisation organisations are to assess the compliance of the documents drafted by the European standardisation organisations with the initial request of the Commission. In each European standard, CENELEC should therefore describe the extent to which that standard aims to cover the request of the Commission.

<a id="rec-19"></a>
(19) CENELEC has agreed to follow the Guidelines for the execution of standardisation requests ^(4).

<a id="rec-20"></a>
(20) In accordance with Article 10(5) of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012, the European standardisation organisations are to inform the Commission about the activities undertaken for the development of the requested documents.

<a id="rec-21"></a>
(21) In order to ensure transparency and to facilitate the execution of the requested standardisation activities CENELEC should prepare a work programme and submit it to the Commission.

<a id="rec-22"></a>
(22) In order to enable the Commission to better monitor the requested standardisation work, CENELEC should provide the Commission with access to an overall project plan containing detailed information on the execution of the standardisation request.

<a id="rec-23"></a>
(23) Experience shows that during the execution of the standardisation request, it may be necessary to adjust the scope of the request, or the deadlines set therein. CENELEC should therefore promptly report to the Commission if they consider that more time is required to draft the standards than initially expected or that it is appropriate to adapt the scope of the request, in order to allow the Commission to take appropriate action.

<a id="rec-24"></a>
(24) In accordance with Article 10(3) of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012, each standardisation request is subject to acceptance by the relevant European standardisation organisation. It is therefore necessary to provide for the rules on validity of this request if it is not accepted by CENELEC.

<a id="rec-25"></a>
(25) In order to ensure legal certainty as to the validity of the request after its execution, it is appropriate to provide for a date of expiry of this Decision.

<a id="rec-26"></a>
(26) If the European standards adopted in response to the standardisation request set out in this Decision are referenced in a Union act, they may be subject to access to documents requests in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1049/2001 of the European Parliament and of the Council ^(5). In its judgement of 5 March 2024 ^(6), Public.Resource.Org and Right to Know CLG v. Commission and Others, the Court of Justice recognised that there is an overriding public interest, within the meaning of Article 4(2) of Regulation (EC) No 1049/2001, justifying the disclosure of standards, which produce legal effects under Union law.

<a id="rec-27"></a>
(27) The European standardisation organisations, the European stakeholders’ organisations receiving Union financing, the Commission Expert Group on Radio Equipment and the Telecommunication Conformity Assessment and Market Surveillance Committee established by Article 45 of Directive 2014/53/EU have been consulted.

<a id="rec-28"></a>
(28) The measures provided for in this Decision are in accordance with the opinion of the Committee on Standards established by Article 22 of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012,

HAS ADOPTED THIS DECISION:

<a id="art-1"></a>
### Article 1 – Requested standardisation activities

1. The European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation (CENELEC) is requested to draft the European standards listed in Annex I in support of Directive 2014/53/EU for charging interface and charging communication protocol for radio equipment capable of being recharged by means of wireless charging, by the deadlines set in that Annex.

2. The European standards referred to in paragraph 1 shall meet the requirements set out in Annex II.

3. CENELEC shall provide the Commission with the titles of the requested European standards in all the official languages of the Union.

<a id="art-2"></a>
### Article 2 – Work programme

1. CENELEC shall prepare a work programme indicating all the European standards listed in Annex I, the responsible technical bodies and a timetable for the execution of the requested standardisation activities in line with the deadlines set out in that Annex.

2. CENELEC shall submit the draft work programme to the Commission by 4 June 2025 and shall inform the Commission of any amendments to the work programme.

3. CENELEC shall provide the Commission with access to an overall project plan.

4. The project plan shall include arrangements ensuring cooperation between CENELEC and the Commission for the execution of the requested standardisation activities.

<a id="art-3"></a>
### Article 3 – Reporting

1. CENELEC shall report annually to the Commission on the execution of the request referred to in Article 1, indicating the progress made in the implementation of the work programme referred to in Article 2.

2. The report shall refer to provisions of the Union legislation that CENELEC has taken into consideration or which they intend to take into consideration for the development of the European standards in accordance with this Decision.

3. CENELEC shall submit the first annual report to the Commission by 4 March 2026. Subsequent annual reports shall be submitted to the Commission by 4 March each year.

4. CENELEC shall provide the Commission with the final report by 4 September 2027.

5. CENELEC shall promptly report to the Commission any major concerns relating to the scope of the request referred to in Article 1 and the deadlines set out in Annex I.

<a id="art-4"></a>
### Article 4 – Validity of the standardisation request

Where, pursuant to Article 10(3) of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012, CENELEC indicates that it does not accept the request referred to in Article 1(1) of this Decision, that request may not constitute a basis for the standardisation activities referred to in Article 1 of this Decision.

This Decision shall expire on 5 September 2027.

<a id="art-5"></a>
### Article 5 – Addressee

This Decision is addressed to the European Committee for Electrotechnical Standardisation.

Done at Brussels, 26.2.2025

## Annex II – Requirements for the European standards referred to in Article 1

Part A. Non-technical requirements for the European standards listed in Annex I

1. General requirements

1.1. The European standards shall include the following elements, which shall be described in an exhaustive manner and shall be objectively and independently verifiable:

- (a) detailed technical specifications in relation to a common and interoperable charging interface and charging communication protocol for the radio equipment described in the Table, points 1 and 2, of Annex I;

- (b) test methods or equivalent approaches and conditions to verify compliance of the radio equipment described in the Table, points 1 and 2, of Annex I with the corresponding specifications.

1.2. The European standards shall contain only product-specific provisions, including methods for the verification of compliance with such provisions.

1.3. The European standards shall meet the requirements set out in this request and shall not include specifications that do not relate to this request. The essential requirements set out in Article 3(1), 3(2) and 3(3) of Directive 2014/53/EU do not relate to this request and shall not be covered by the specifications in the European standards. However, the clauses or subclauses of those standards shall not undermine the conformity with any of those essential requirements or any of the requirements set out in other Union legislation.

1.4. The European standards shall not:

- (a) in their normative part, make any references to or reproduce any of the requirements or procedures set out in Directive 2014/53/EU or in delegated or implementing acts adopted pursuant to that Directive;

- (b) reproduce any definitions set out in Directive 2014/53/EU or in delegated or implementing acts adopted pursuant to that Directive, or modify the meaning of those definitions;

- (c) define any legally relevant terms used but not defined in Directive 2014/53/EU or in delegated or implementing acts adopted pursuant to that Directive;

- (d) contradict any provisions of Directive 2014/53/EU or of delegated or implementing acts adopted pursuant to that Directive;

- (e) make conformity with the standards dependent on requirements of an administrative or organisational nature.

1.5. Normative references included in European standards shall be specific and clear and ensure identification of all specifications covered by the European standards. Where European standards refer to other standards, or clauses in those standards, and those standards or clauses contain further normative references (i.e. normative reference chain), the whole normative reference chain shall be clear and specific. Normative reference chains shall however be avoided whenever possible.

2. Specific requirements 2.1. Each European standard shall clearly indicate its scope and the products which fall under its scope.

2.2. Each European standard shall include a clear and precise description of the relationship between its content and the aspects of this request that it aims to cover.

2.3. If a European standard does not fully address the aspects of this request that it aims to cover in relation to the products falling within its scope, the standard shall indicate the aspects of this request that are not addressed in relation to such products.

2.4. Each European standard developed on the basis of the request referred to in Article 1 of this Decision shall refer to this Decision.

Part B. Technical requirements for the European standards listed in Annex I

1. General requirements

1.1. The European standards shall reflect the generally acknowledged state of the art.

1.2. The technical solutions laid down in the European standards shall be proportionate to the aspects that they aim to address.

1.3. When European standards include one or several test methods, these shall be verifiable, objective and reproducible to ensure comparable verification of the technical specifications for all the products covered by the European standards. Alternative mechanisms to test methods shall be developed only in case there is a demonstrated technical impossibility to implement test methods and when duly justified. If deemed appropriate, a European standard may define different provisions for different subcategories of radio equipment.

1.4. When Union legal acts address aspects related to this request, CENELEC shall select in the European standards those technical solutions that are also compatible with the provisions of those Union legal acts laying down specific requirements in relation to charging interface and charging communication protocol as regards radio equipment capable of being recharged by means of wireless charging. Compliance with the European standards should not prejudice the compliance with the provisions laying down specific requirements regarding wireless charging interface and communication protocol of other Union legal acts, which address aspects related to this request.

1.5. CENELEC shall ensure that when they develop technical solutions aimed at addressing this request, their respective technical solutions have the same level of effectiveness in addressing this request.

2. Specific requirements

2.1. The European standards listed in the Table, point 1, of Annex I shall lay down the technical specifications for a common and interoperable charging interface and specify its scope as regards radio equipment covered by this request which is capable of being recharged by means of wireless charging.

2.2. The European standards listed in the Table, point 2, of Annex I shall lay down the technical specifications for a common and interoperable charging communication protocol and specify its scope as regards radio equipment covered by this request which is capable of being recharged by means of wireless charging.

2.3. CENELEC shall develop European standards based on validated technical solutions and including definitions, requirements, test methods and rationale, in order to be in compliance with this request.

2.4. The European standards shall, where necessary, be drafted by applying the iterative process of risk assessment and risk reduction.

2.5. The validated technical solutions shall have an established level of market penetration; have proven its technological performance; be suitable, performant, and appropriate for charging; allow interoperability between different types of transmitters and receivers for the categories of radio equipment covered by this request; and be energy efficient.
