Question for written answer E-001843/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

José Cepeda (S&D), Elio Di Rupo (S&D), Nacho Sánchez Amor (S&D), Sven Mikser (S&D)

Subject: European regulatory gap regarding offensive AI capabilities

On 7 April 2026, the US company Anthropic announced Claude Mythos Preview, a general-purpose artificial intelligence (AI) model capable of autonomously discovering and exploiting thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities across major operating systems and web browsers.

This case reveals a significant structural gap in the European regulatory framework: Article 2(3) of the AI Act^(2)1 expressly excludes from its scope AI systems used for military, defence or national security purposes, regardless of whether the operator is a public or private entity. This means that AI models with state-level offensive capabilities – including those that could potentially be deployed by private actors in defence-related contexts – may operate in a largely unregulated space at EU level.

This exclusion creates a regulatory grey area, particularly for dual-use AI systems developed by private actors, whose initial purpose is not military, but which can be integrated into offensive cyber operations or intelligence capabilities.

In the light of the above, does the Commission intend to address the gap in Article 2(3) of the AI Act with regard to AI systems with offensive cyber capabilities developed by private actors, in particular when such systems are deployed in decision-making chains affecting the security of the EU and its Member States?
