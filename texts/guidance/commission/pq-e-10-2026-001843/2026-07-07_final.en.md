EN E-001843/2026 Answer given by Executive Vice-President Virkkunen on behalf of the European Commission (7.7.2026)

Pursuant to Article 2(3) of the AI Act^(1), the AI Act does not apply to AI systems where and in so far they are placed on the market, put into service, or used with or without modification or where the output is used in the EU exclusively for military, defence or national security purposes, regardless of the type of entity carrying out those activities. Hence, these exemptions apply regardless of whether the deployer is a public or a private entity.

Concerning dual-use AI systems, the purpose is not exclusively for military, defence or national security purposes, so the AI Act applies for these systems. This is clarified by Recital 24 of the AI Act: ‘AI systems placed on the market or put into service for an excluded purpose, namely military, defence or national security, and one or more non-excluded purposes, such as civilian purposes or law enforcement, fall within the scope of this Regulation and providers […] should ensure compliance with this Regulation’.

In the Guidelines on Prohibited AI Practices^(2), paragraphs 24 and 25 refer to dual-use AI systems falling within the scope of the AI Act and present an example:‘if a company offers a remote biometric identification system for various purposes, including law enforcement and national security, that company […] must ensure its compliance with the requirements in the AI Act’.

According to the information available to the Commission, Mythos Preview has been made available to a range of business customers and cyber-defensive organisations in the course of commercial activity and is not placed on the market exclusively for military, defence or national security purposes within the meaning of Article 2(3) AI Act.
