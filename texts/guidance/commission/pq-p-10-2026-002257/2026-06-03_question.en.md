Priority question for written answer P-002257/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

Jörgen Warborn (PPE)

Subject: Geographical scope of the Union equivalence clause in the Industrial Accelerator Act

The Commission’s proposal for a set of measures to accelerate industrial capacity (‘Industrial Accelerator Act’, 2026/0068 (COD)) includes requirements for contracting authorities to reach certain minimum EU origin quotas in selected product categories, an approach known as ‘European preference’. However, in order to honour international obligations in the field of trade policy, there is an equivalence clause that treats goods from partner countries with which the Union has concluded a customs union or free trade agreement, or from countries that are parties to the Agreement on Government Procurement, as being equivalent to goods of Union origin. This would cover around 90 countries in total.

However, at the joint meeting on the Industrial Accelerator Act organised by Parliament’s committees on trade, industry and the internal market on 2 June 2026, the Commission’s Executive Vice-President Stéphane Séjourné argued that the equivalence clause on EU origin would apply to only a very limited group of countries – no more than around 20. As the equivalence clause is an integral part of the act in question, with significant implications for individual companies as well as for contracting authorities and for the EU’s relationship with global partners, there is an urgent need to clarify the exact geographical scope of the clause.

In the light of the foregoing:

Which third countries would – on the basis of their current status under bilateral or multilateral agreements in force – enjoy ‘equivalent to Union origin’ status pursuant to the equivalence clause laid down in Article 8 of the proposal for an Industrial Accelerator Act?
