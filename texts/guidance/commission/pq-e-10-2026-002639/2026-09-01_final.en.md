EN E-002639/2026 Answer given by Executive Vice-President Virkkunen on behalf of the European Commission (1.9.2026)

European Standardisation Organisations (ESOs) are bound by the provisions on transparency of the Regulation on Standardisation^(1). If these provisions are not met, the Commission can decide not to cite offered harmonised standards in the Official Journal.

There are however no legal requirements on ESOs to disclose to the public information on participants and their contributions, but this information is available for participants of the respective technical committee. Sharing information on specific contributions goes beyond the reporting obligations of ESOs to the Commission.

The Commission’s request to the ESOs to develop harmonised standards for the AI Act^(2) contains, in line with the Regulation on Standardisation^(3), requirements to facilitate the appropriate representation and effective participation of relevant stakeholders. The ESOs are required to report on the fulfilment of this and other requirements to the Commission every three months.

The AI Act lays down a single set of rules for AI in the EU, creating a level playing field for all companies that want to do business in the EU. Only AI systems classified as high-risk AI need to fulfil the requirements for high-risk AI systems. Standards play a key role to provide technical solutions to providers to ensure compliance with the AI Act, to promote innovation as well as competitiveness and growth in the single market. All companies that aim to develop trustworthy AI can benefit from using these standards. Standards will thus help the development of AI in the EU.
