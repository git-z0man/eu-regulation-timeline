EN E-001470/2026

Answer given by Executive Vice-President Séjourné on behalf of the European Commission (10.6.2026)

Under Regulation (EU) 2019/1020^(1), market surveillance authorities (MSAs) have a range of tools to intercept non-compliant personal mobility devices (PMDs) sold online. These include powers to do mystery shopping online, to order the removal of online listings, to suspend the release of products at the EU's external borders, and to require the withdrawal or recall of products presenting a risk to health and safety on the basis of a general safety assessment^(2) or Regulation (EU) 2023/988^(3). Furthermore, Member States undertake coordinated enforcement action across the Single Market. However, the absence of harmonised road safety requirements for PMDs limits the effectiveness of these tools in practice.

Although the EU machinery legislation^(4) is the main safety regulatory act applying to machinery, including mobile machinery such as PMDs, and addressing safety when the machinery is at work, its essential requirements do not cover the safety aspects of that machinery when circulating on public roads.

In 2024, a study^(5), prepared on behalf of the Commission, examined the challenges and opportunities associated with PMDs. The mid-term report on the implementation of the EU road safety framework^(6) committed to drawing conclusions on the potential benefits of harmonising technical specifications for on-road use of PMDs^(7).

The 2024 study also showed that PMD riders’ behaviour plays a significant role for safety. Member States have an important role to play in setting out adequate traffic rules and properly enforce them. The Commission has committed to prepare guidance to Member States and regional and local administrations on what issues should be taken into account to ensure the safety of riders and other road users during the use of PMDs^(8).
