Question for written answer E-001470/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

Stefano Cavedagna (ECR), Denis Nesci (ECR)

Subject: Consumer safety and market surveillance for personal mobility devices

Millions of personal mobility devices (PMDs), such as e-scooters, are sold across the EU, yet fall outside the scope of vehicle type-approval (Regulation (EU) No 168/2013^(1)). The Machinery Directive^(2) governs their placement on the market, but does not address road safety requirements such as braking, lighting, speed limitation or anti-tampering.

A November 2024 TRL/fka study, conducted on behalf of the Commission, documented fraudulent CE certifications and recommended a universal EU PMD approval scheme. In December 2025, 16 Member States called for an EU-wide framework. Meanwhile, the European and Italian markets face a growing influx of electric bicycles that are sold as pedal-assisted cycles but function as unregistered mopeds. These have top speeds of 50-85 km/h, but are not subject to requirements concerning insurance, helmets or licences. Seizures by law enforcement in cities across Italy confirm the systemic scale of the problem.

1. Since there are no harmonised EU technical requirements, what tools do market surveillance authorities possess, under Regulation (EU) 2019/1020^(3), to intercept PMDs sold online that fail to meet road safety standards?

2. Does the Commission consider the Machinery Regulation adequate consumer protection for devices used daily on public roads by millions of Europeans, including minors?

3. Will the Commission include harmonisation on PMD in its 2027 work programme, given the TRL/fka study recommendation and the call made by 16 Member States in the Transport Council?

Submitted: 10.4.2026
