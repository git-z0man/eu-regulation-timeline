Answer given by Executive Vice-President Séjourné

on behalf of the European Commission

(13.11.2026)

The Commission recognises procurement and expenditure data as strategic assets for the management, administrative oversight and control of public spending, but also as instruments to inform policy makers in steering procurement towards high-quality purchases. High-quality and comprehensive data covering the full procurement life cycle is also essential to enable effective monitoring of irregularities, increasing ex post accountability as a rail-guard for more flexible procedures.

In its legislative proposal for a Public Procurement Act^(1) (hereafter ‘the proposal’), in which more flexible procedures are an essential simplification element, the Commission therefore proposed to increase transparency and accountability through the establishment of National Public Procurement Data Spaces in each Member State, interconnected at EU level under common technical standards to create an EU-wide Public Procurement Data Space.

The existing proposal does not amend the existing EU remedies framework^(2) but reinforces Member States’ monitoring of their own public procurement markets, including to tackle fraud, favouritism and conflicts of interest. It would require them to identify shortcomings, take appropriate follow-up measures, develop action plans where necessary, and use the findings to inform strategic decision-making and investment policies. In this context, Member States would designate a single national authority to ensure effective coordination and strengthen cooperation and learning.
