Question for written answer E-002386/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

Ioan-Rareş Bogdan (PPE)

Subject: Digital sovereignty stress test: Who will foot the bill for the EU’s technological ambitions?

The European Technological Sovereignty Package acknowledges major dependence, with the EU spending EUR 264 billion a year on third-party software and cloud products, and proposes that data centre capacity be trebled. At the same time, the objectives of the Cloud and AI Development Act and the Chips Act 2.0 put huge financial and logistical pressure on the Member States.

1. Through which financial mechanisms will the Commission support startups and local authorities in redirecting part of the EUR 264 billion to the European open-source ecosystem, preventing the funds from being absorbed once again by large international integrators?

2. How will the Commission ensure the fair distribution and EU co-financing of the massive costs of modernising national electricity grids, given the logistical burden that the trebling of data centre capacity will place on the Member States?

3. How will the Commission reconcile the implementation of the ‘cloud and AI sovereignty assessment framework’ with the promise to keep the market open to like-minded partners without creating operational bottlenecks or major compliance costs for EU SMEs?

Submitted: 10.6.2026
