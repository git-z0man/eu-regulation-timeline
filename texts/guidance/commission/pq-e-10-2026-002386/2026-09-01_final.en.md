EN E-002386/2026 Answer given by Executive Vice-President Virkkunen on behalf of the European Commission (1.9.2026)

Strengthening tech sovereignty requires sustained financial funding. Discussions on the Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF) 2028-2034 are ongoing, including on funding EU competitiveness through instruments like the future European Competitiveness Fund^(1). Procurement promoting open standards and reusable solutions can also help local authorities buy from Europe’s open-source ecosystem and reduce reliance on large international providers^(2). The European Parliament has a key role in achieving this ambition, notably in the MFF and Tech Sovereignty Package^(3) negotiations.

Increased data centre (DC) capacity will require major grid investments. To increase grid capacity in a cost-effective manner, the Commission has taken action including through the Grids Action Plan^(4), the European Grids package^(5) and the Connecting Europe Facility^(6) to make better use of existing infrastructure, reinforce cross-border coordination and cross-sectoral planning, and accelerate permit-granting procedures and access to funding. In addition, the proposal to future-proof electricity bills^(7) aims to lower electricity costs through efficient network charges and smart electricity grid and network access.

The sovereignty framework under the proposed Cloud and AI Development Act^(8) is risk-based and proportionate, with requirements increasing where justified by the sensitivity of the service. Under the proposal, procurement of cloud and AI services remains open to like-minded partners that meet security, transparency and control requirements. A single EU framework is expected to reduce fragmentation, costs and bottlenecks. Small and medium-sized enterprises would benefit from simpler procedures and improved access to public procurement for cloud services.
