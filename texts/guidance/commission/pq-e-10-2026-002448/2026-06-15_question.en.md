Question for written answer E-002448/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

Sarah Knafo (ESN), Adrian-George Axinia (ECR), Nikola Bartůšek (ECR), Markus Buchheit (ESN), Petr Bystron (ESN), Christophe Gomart (PPE), Fernand Kartheiser (NI), Kateřina Konečná (NI), Ondřej Krutílek (ECR), Alvise Pérez (NI), Katarína Roth Neveďalová (NI), Volker Schnurrbusch (ESN), Georgiana Teodorescu (ECR), Auke Zijlstra (PfE)

Subject: Nuclear power and data centres

On 3 June 2026, the Commission presented the EU’s Cloud and AI Development Act, which aims to triple the EU’s data centre capacity. Article 4(1)(e) explicitly identifies small modular reactors as an energy source to power these facilities.

Meanwhile, Article 11 requires that data centres deployed in acceleration zones apply the performance indicators specified in Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1364. These include the renewable energy factor, which puts facilities powered by nuclear energy at a disadvantage, despite them being carbon‑free.

On 26 May 2026, ten Member States, including France, wrote to the Commission condemning this departure from the principle of technological neutrality and demanding that nuclear power be recognised as sustainable in the same way as solar and wind power.

1. How does the Commission justify promoting nuclear power to supply data centres in Article 4 of the EU’s Cloud and AI Development Act, while including indicators in Article 11 which specifically penalise data centres powered by that same energy source?

2. Does the Commission intend to recognise nuclear power as a sustainable energy source in future labelling of data centres, in line with the principle of technological neutrality and the request of the ten Member States that signed the letter of 26 May 2026?
