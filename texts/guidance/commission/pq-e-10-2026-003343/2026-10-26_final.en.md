EN E-003343/2026 Answer given by Executive Vice-President Séjourné on behalf of the European Commission (21.10.2026)

National preferences as such are in contradiction with the very idea of the Single Market and the fundamental freedoms of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. This is why the current Public Procurement Directives^(1) like the recent Commission proposal for a Public Procurement Act^(2) and the proposal for an Industrial Accelerator Act^(3) unequivocally enshrine the principle of non-discrimination within the EU.

This principle does however not apply towards economic operators, goods, services and works originating in third countries, unless a given procurement falls within the scope of the EU’s commitments towards that third country in the context of the World Trade Organization Government Procurement Agreement or in other international agreements of the EU. The Commission proposal for a Public Procurement Act, as well as the proposal for an Industrial Accelerator Act, therefore contain clear rules to the effect that public buyers may impose restrictions on such non-covered economic operators, goods, services or works. Also buyers may, and are encouraged within the proposal, to establish significant quality award criteria. The latter would naturally lead to choosing high-quality European offers. Finally, EU citizens, when doing their individual purchases, have full freedom to buy the products they choose, be it from their own Member State or other countries.
