E-002079/2026

Answer given by Mr Dombrovskis

on behalf of the European Commission

(6.11.2026)

Under the Recovery and Resilience Facility, payments are disbursed once a Member State has satisfactorily fulfilled the milestones and targets set out in the Annex to the Council Implementing Decision. In assessing fulfilment, the Commission verifies that all relevant requirements in the Annex have been met. Investments 1, 2, 3 and 6 of Component 11 of the Spanish Recovery and Resilience Plan (RRP) concern the digital transformation of the public administration and the reinforcement of its cybersecurity capabilities^(1). None of the milestones and targets linked to these investments set requirements on equipment suppliers.

In the Communication on implementing the EU 5G Toolbox^(2), the Commission assessed Huawei and ZTE as posing materially higher risks than other 5G suppliers. In addition, on 20 January 2026, the Commission adopted a proposal to revise the Cybersecurity Act^(3). This proposal sets out an objective, risk-based framework to address risks from high-risk suppliers in critical-sector Information and Communications Technology (ICT) supply chains. It also proposes excluding such suppliers from activities under EU funding programmes and instruments implemented in direct and indirect management, in accordance with Article 136 Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2024/2509 and EU sector-specific rules, and from EU funding activities under shared management where they provide ICT components, or components containing ICT, for use in key ICT assets identified under this Regulation. The Cybersecurity Act 2 is under negotiation by co-legislators.

Under the Spanish RRP (milestone 235), Spain had to adopt a law on 5G cybersecurity containing selected features of the recommendation on the EU 5G toolbox. The milestone was assessed positively as part of the fourth payment request.
