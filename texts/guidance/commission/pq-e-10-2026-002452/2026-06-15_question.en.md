Question for written answer E-002452/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

José Cepeda (S&D)

Subject: Mobiles as critical endpoints for citizens’ cybersecurity and European digital resilience

Mobile phones are regularly used to access essential public services, e-government facilities, digital credentials, professional communication systems and corporate systems. However, while frameworks such as the NIS2 directive, DORA and the Cyber Resilience Act strengthen the risk management, operational resilience and security of products with digital elements, mobiles still only appear indirectly within the European regulatory ecosystem.

This situation can create uncertainty for citizens, businesses and administrations as to what minimum security standards should be required from mobile devices, especially when they act as endpoints connected to critical services or contain sensitive credentials, such as biometric profiles.

With this in mind:

1. Is the Commission planning to request that the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) provide a specific guide on the protection of mobile devices as endpoints, including risk management, updates, encryption, authentication, applications and BYODs?

2. How will the Commission ensure that the review of the Cyber Resilience Act adequately covers risks linked to mobile phones, operating systems, pre-installed applications and update cycles?

3. Will the Commission, along with ENISA, the European Data Protection Supervisor and the European Data Protection Board, develop common guidelines that reconcile mobile cybersecurity, data protection and users’ rights?
