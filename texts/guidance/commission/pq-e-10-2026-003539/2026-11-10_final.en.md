EN E-003539/2026 Answer given by Executive Vice-President Virkkunen on behalf of the European Commission (10.11.2026)

Access to reliable healthcare information is essential to the protection of the health, safety and fundamental rights of people within Europe, including freedom of expression and information.

To the extent that chatbots are involved in accessing such information, the AI Act^(1) and the Digital Services Act^(2) (DSA) are the key legislative instruments which the Commission has to ensure this protection.

Many of the most widely used chatbots are currently built on general-purpose AI models with systemic risk. Under the AI Act, the providers of such models must assess and mitigate the systemic risks posed by their models, which may include the dissemination of misleading or inaccurate healthcare information.

The Commission is aware of the AlgorithmWatch investigation. Whilst its findings alone do not establish the existence of a systemic risk under the AI Act, the Commission is engaged in regulatory dialogue with leading providers, including in relation to their post-market monitoring, and taking formal actions as needed.

Following designation of ChatGPT as a very large online search engine under the DSA, its provider will also be required from January 2027 to assess and mitigate systemic risks presented by it, including risks related to actual or foreseeable negative effects on the protection of public health and minors and serious negative consequences to physical and mental well-being.

The Commission is committed to using its full supervisory and enforcement powers to ensure compliance with both regulations.

The European Democracy Shield also plays an important role in countering foreign information manipulation and interference and disinformation, with the safeguarding of the integrity of the information space as one of its three main pillars^(3).
