EN E-003567/2026 Answer given by Executive Vice-President Séjourné on behalf of the European Commission (3.11.2026)

With the upcoming European Product Act (EPA) proposal, the Commission plans to ensure that in the case of products sold in the EU, including through online channels, there will be an economic operator established in the EU that can be traced back and which will need to fulfil the relevant statutory compliance tasks. Depending on the case, this may be the manufacturer, the importer, the authorised representative, or another relevant operator under the applicable EU product legislation. The EPA proposal aims to close accountability gaps and strengthen enforcement of the rules by introducing an identification and verification system that builds on the existing Digital Product Passport framework^(1), and that allows for the demonstration of compliance.

In the context of the EPA, the Commission is also considering whether the obligations on providers of online marketplaces are commensurate with the role they play in the supply chain. Any such measures need to remain coherent with the existing EU legal framework, including the Digital Services Act^(2) (DSA). Enforcement action by the Commission under the DSA has already led to fines on Temu (EUR 200 million) and AliExpress (EUR 550 million), and proceedings are ongoing against Shein.
