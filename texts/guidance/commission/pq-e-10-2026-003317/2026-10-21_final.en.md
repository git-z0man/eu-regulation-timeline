EN

E-003317/2026

Answer given by Executive Vice-President Virkkunen on behalf of the European Commission

(21.10.2026)

The AI Act covers autonomous AI systems and AI agents in accordance with the definitions of AI system and general-purpose AI model^(1). Providers of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk should ensure that systemic risks, including cyber risks and loss of control, are continuously assessed and mitigated across the entire model lifecycle, starting from the large pre-training run^(2).

The AI Office has requested and received serious incident reports in relation to recent loss of control incidents and has asked further questions to the providers.

Based on its assessment of providers’ replies, the AI Office will determine whether further enforcement action is necessary. In accordance with the AI Act, the AI Office may issue requests for information by decision, if providers refuse to answer or provide incomplete answers; request access to models to conduct evaluations; require providers to implement appropriate mitigating measures; and where necessary, restrict the making available of a model on the market. The AI Office may also issue fines if providers provide incorrect or misleading information.

The Commission is ready to take the necessary steps to make sure companies comply with their obligations. The enforcement powers of the Commission with regard to the AI Act^(3) entered into application on 2 August 2026.

Building on Europe’s unique legal framework for AI, which is at once risk-based and containing comprehensive AI and cybersecurity rules, on 7 July 2026, the Commission adopted an Action Plan on Cybersecurity and AI with the aim to ensure, amongst other things, that frontier AI models are safe, accessible and deployable for cybersecurity in Europe, and to prepare the EU’s cyber ecosystem for the challenges and opportunities of increasingly cybercapable AI^(4).
