Question for written answer E-000198/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

Elena Kountoura (The Left)

Subject: The Commission must intervene to combat non-consensual production of sexual and deepfake AI content on social media

According to recent revelations^(2)1, the Grok AI system, used on the social networking platform X, has been producing non-consensual nude and sexualised content of women and minors, including deepfake images of real people.

These practices constitute a serious violation of fundamental rights, grossly violate human dignity and pose a risk to the security of citizens in digital environments, while being particularly harmful to victims. Therefore, they should not be technically feasible in the EU^(3)2.

Given the provisions of the European legislative framework, specifically:

— the Digital Services Act, which imposes the rapid removal of illegal content and the obligation to assess and mitigate systemic risks^(4)3,

— the Artificial Intelligence Act, which provides for transparency obligations and prohibits misleading practices^(5)4,

— the General Data Protection Regulation, in particular when personal data is used to create deepfake products,

— Directive (EU) 2024/1385 on combating violence against women^(6)5,

The Commission is asked:

1. Why has no formal investigation or sanction procedure been initiated so far, despite the severity and systemic nature of the violations?

2. Does the Commission intend to take measures to protect victims, in particular minors, to ensure the prompt removal of illegal material?

3. Does the Commission intend to propose the explicit classification of the non-consensual creation, manipulation or reproduction of intimate images by means of artificial intelligence systems as a prohibited practice under the Artificial Intelligence Act, in order to ensure legal clarity, enhance effective enforcement and prevent similar incidents?
