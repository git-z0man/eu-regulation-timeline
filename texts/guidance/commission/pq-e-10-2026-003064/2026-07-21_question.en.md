Question for written answer E-003064/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

Maria Guzenina (S&D), Biljana Borzan (S&D), Maria Grapini (S&D), Mario Furore (The Left), Manuela Ripa (PPE), Magdalena Adamowicz (PPE), Stéphanie Yon-Courtin (Renew), Dimitris Tsiodras (PPE), Caterina Chinnici (PPE), Heléne Fritzon (S&D), Evin Incir (S&D), Adnan Dibrani (S&D), Sofie Eriksson (S&D), Johan Danielsson (S&D)

Subject: Implementation of the recommendations of the Commission’s Special Panel on children in the digital world

On 13 July 2026, the Commission’s Special Panel on protecting and empowering minors in the digital world presented its report to President von der Leyen. Its recommendations address key areas including age-appropriate design, digital literacy, platform accountability, support for parents and educators, children’s participation and the governance of digital services.

Despite progress under the Digital Services Act, AI Act and Better Internet for Kids Strategy, children navigate an evolving ecosystem of platforms, social media, messaging, gaming, immersive environments and AI-powered services. This raises the question whether the EU framework is sufficiently coherent and future-proof to address the full range of risks and opportunities affecting children online.

In the light of the above:

1. Which recommendations in the Special Panel’s final report does the Commission intend to translate into concrete legislative and non-legislative initiatives, and what is the envisaged timeline for their implementation?

2. Is the Commission considering proposing a dedicated legislative instrument establishing a comprehensive framework for the protection, empowerment and participation of children in the digital ecosystem?

3. Does the Commission intend to develop a more holistic and future-proof approach to children’s digital rights, ensuring that legislation addresses the full digital ecosystem shaping children’s lives – including online platforms, messaging services, gaming environments, AI systems, immersive technologies and future digital services, among others – rather than regulating individual sectors in isolation?
