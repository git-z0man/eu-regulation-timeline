Question for written answer E-001948/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

Alessandro Zan (S&D), Giuseppe Lupo (S&D), Alessandra Moretti (S&D), Georgia Tramacere (S&D), Pina Picierno (S&D), Sandro Ruotolo (S&D), Irene Tinagli (S&D), Brando Benifei (S&D), Marco Tarquinio (S&D), Camilla Laureti (S&D), Cecilia Strada (S&D), Annalisa Corrado (S&D), Elisabetta Gualmini (Renew), Gaetano Pedulla' (The Left), Leoluca Orlando (Verts/ALE), Ilaria Salis (The Left)

Subject: The compatibility with EU law of the Italian 2026 Security Law’s video surveillance measure

Also known as the 2026 Security Law, Decree-Law No 23/2026 allocates EUR 19 million to the expansion of the interconnected video surveillance networks that are used by municipalities, local police forces, provincial police headquarters (questure) and Italy’s gendarmerie (Carabinieri) and financial police (Guardia di Finanza). The new law does not, however, lay down any minimum IT security requirements (such as strong authentication procedures, centralised access tracking and network segmentation) or data protection measures for these networks which, given the absence of implementing decrees, will not be governed by an appropriate protection and guarantee framework.

We believe this surveillance measure to be at odds with Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (particularly Articles 32, 24, 25 and 35 thereof) and also have concerns about the role allocated to the Data Protection Authority^(2)1 and the Security Law's compliance with the principles of data security, accessibility and protection. Finally, we fear that Decree-Law No 23/2026 could turn out to be vague and disproportionate, and that these expanded surveillance systems could therefore be vulnerable to unauthorised access, be complemented by inadequate organisational and technical measures and be used in unjustified profiling initiatives and in other ways that exceed their stated purpose.

Having regard to the EU’s cybersecurity framework, the NIS2 Directive and the provisions of Articles 7 and 8 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights, and considering the need to ensure the full implementation of the NIS2 Directive, we ask the Commission:

1. Is it aware of the 2026 Security Law’s surveillance measure and its inadequate protection and guarantee framework?

2. Does it consider the provisions outlined above to be compatible with EU law? If not, what further measures does it think are needed to bring this surveillance measure in line with European law and ensure that it fully upholds fundamental rights?

3. Will it take action to ensure compliance with EU law?
