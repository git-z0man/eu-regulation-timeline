Question for written answer E-001157/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

Tobiasz Bocheński (ECR)

Subject: Third-party litigation funding in the EU and the Product Liability Directive

The European Parliament adopted its resolution of 13 September 2022 on responsible private funding of litigation^(2)1 with a large majority, calling on the Commission to introduce a harmonised regulatory framework for third-party litigation funding (TPLF).

The volume of mass litigation is rising strongly and recent legislative developments, including the revised Product Liability Directive (PLD)^(3)2 and the Representative Actions Directive (RAD)^(4)3, will further increase litigation exposure for companies, including small and medium-sized enterprises, in the EU. The Commission’s study of 21 March 2025 entitled ‘Mapping Third Party Litigation Funding in the European Union’ confirms the rapid development of the multi-billion euro TPLF market and identifies transparency gaps, risks of conflicts of interest and divergent regulatory approaches in the Member States.

1. Will the Commission issue guidance to ensure consistent PLD implementation by Member States and prevent unintended results, such as a reversal of the burden of proof, in order to protect EU competitiveness and the single market?

2. Despite broad support from Parliament for EU-level regulation of TPLF, why has the Commission not yet proposed any initiative on governance, transparency or financial oversight for litigation funders?

3. How does the Commission evaluate the combined effects of the new PLD, collective redress (RAD), and TPLF growth on legal certainty, insurance, compliance costs and EU competitiveness and simplification?
