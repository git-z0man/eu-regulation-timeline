Question for written answer E-003060/2025

to the Commission

Rule 144

Jorge Buxadé Villalba (PfE)

Subject: Risks to European security posed by the contract between Pedro Sánchez’s Government and the Chinese company Huawei

The Spanish Government has contracted Huawei, which has links with China’s intelligence apparatus, to provide servers for storing court-authorised wiretaps from SITEL (Integrated Telecommunications Interception System). Many Western States consider Huawei to be a high-risk critical infrastructure supplier^(1).

Despite warnings from the Commission and other international allies, Spain’s socialist Government has decided to ignore the security implications this could have for judicial and police cooperation both nationally and at European level.

This decision not only violates the principle of national security as part of a robust EU strategy, but also lays the Spanish judicial system’s most sensitive data open to potential foreign interference. In addition, it goes against the objectives of the EU toolbox for 5G security and the NIS 2 Directive.

1. Does the Commission consider this contract to be in line with EU recommendations for 5G security and the implementation of the NIS 2 Directive?

2. Will the Commission assess the security risks posed by the fact that Spanish surveillance and court-authorised wiretapping systems are using Chinese hardware?

3. What action will the Commission take to ensure that Pedro Sánchez’s unilateral decisions do not compromise EU security?

Submitted: 23.7.2025
