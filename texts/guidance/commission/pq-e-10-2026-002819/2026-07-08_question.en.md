Question for written answer E-002819/2026/rev.1

to the Commission

Rule 144

Andreas Schwab (PPE)

Subject: Protection of European analogue cultural heritage against destructive scanning and circumvention of AI Act obligations by AI developers

Press reports^(1) indicate that artificial intelligence (AI) developers are using shell companies to execute automated bulk purchases of rare, out-of-print physical books from European second-hand bookshops. These books undergo ‘destructive scanning’, where bindings are destroyed for high-speed digitisation, and are subsequently shredded. This predatory practice permanently removes rare books from secondary markets, exploiting an analogue loophole to mask data provenance.

Under Article 53 of the AI Act^(2), general-purpose AI (GPAI) providers must comply with EU copyright law, respect text and data mining opt-outs under Directive (EU) 2019/790^(3), and publish detailed training data summaries, regardless of whether sources are digital-native or physically digitised.

1. What concrete enforcement actions will the Commission take to ensure that GPAI providers utilising physical-to-digital pipelines fully disclose these works in transparency summaries, preventing the masking of data provenance following physical acquisition?

2. Does the Commission agree that digitising legally purchased books for commercial AI training without a licence constitutes unauthorised reproduction under EU law, regardless of the physical original’s destruction?

3. What initiatives will the Commission introduce to help libraries and second-hand bookshops protect out-of-print regional heritage from commercial buyouts and physical destruction?

Submitted: 8.7.2026
