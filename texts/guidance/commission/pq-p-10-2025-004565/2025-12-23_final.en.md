EN P-004565/2025 Answer given by Executive Vice-President Virkkunen on behalf of the European Commission (23.12.2025)

According to Regulation (EU) No 910/2014^(1), where a relying party intends to rely upon European Digital Identity Wallets (EUDI Wallets) for the provision of public or private services by means of digital interaction, the relying party shall be registered in the Member State where it is established, it will declare the intended use of EUDI Wallets, including an indication of the data to be requested by the relying party from users, and it shall not request users to provide any data other than those indicated. Furthermore, in order to protect the user against illegal information requests, the regulatory framework for the EUDI Wallets sets out which information can be requested from EUDI Wallet users. Additionally, the technical infrastructure of the EUDI Wallets has been designed to ensure data minimisation as well as the unlinkability and unobservability of the various transactions carried out with the wallet.

The Commission proposed well-targeted amendments to the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)^(2), ^(3). They do not touch on the fundamentals of the GDPR but aim to harmonise, clarify, and simplify the application of the GDPR and do not negatively impact individuals’ fundamental rights, including their right to data protection. One of the amendments clarifies the compatibility of further processing of personal data for certain privileged purposes currently set out in Article 5(1)(b) GDPR, i.e. for scientific, historic research, statistics and archiving in the public interest. The envisaged clarification is not applicable to any other purpose pursued by the controllers.

The proposed amendments do not alter specific rules in other EU law, such as the rules of Regulation (EU) 910/2014 and in the Commission’s proposal on digital travel credentials^(4).
