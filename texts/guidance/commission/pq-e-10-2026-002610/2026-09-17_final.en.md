EN E-002610/2026 Answer given by Executive Vice-President Virkkunen on behalf of the European Commission (17.9.2026)

The Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)^(1), applying in full from 11 December 2027, requires manufacturers of products with digital elements^(2) to keep up-to-date technical documentation at least during the product’s support period. That documentation shall include, where applicable, a description of the system architecture explaining how software components build on or feed into each other and integrate into the overall processing. This may include identifying relevant application programming interfaces (APIs) and control server addresses. Furthermore, where a product is substantially modified as defined in Article 3(30) of the CRA after placement on the market, it is required to undergo a new conformity assessment.

The Radio Equipment Directive^(3) does not specifically require the manufacturer to disclose control server addresses and APIs. It requires manufacturers to draw up a technical documentation, which, where appropriate, should include versions of software or firmware affecting compliance with relevant essential requirements set out in that Directive.

In the framework of the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation^(4) and the 2025-2030 Work Programme^(5), the Commission has been working on the ecodesign of servers and data storage products, as well as on addressing, where relevant, aspects linked to software, durability and upgradability.

Finally, the proposed revision of the Cybersecurity Act^(6) puts forward a framework to address strategic Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) supply chain risks and monitor dependencies in several critical ICT supply chains including cloud services. Within this framework, a range of targeted and proportional derisking measures, also transparency-related, could be taken.
