Priority question for written answer P-000438/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

Christine Anderson (ESN)

Subject: Digital Omnibus III – Inconsistency of Commission answers of 23 December 2025 and 28 January 2026 with Member State objections raised in the Council

The Commission stated, in response to my previous questions, that the Digital Omnibus introduces only well-targeted clarifications to the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)^(1), does not affect its fundamentals and cannot enable cross-system profiling or function creep, including in relation to digital identity wallets, digital travel credentials and other interoperable infrastructures^(2).

Subsequently, comments from the Member States discussed in the Council explicitly contest these assessments, warning that proposed changes to the definition of personal data, pseudonymisation, secondary processing and device access may lower the level of protection under Article 8 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, create enforcement fragmentation and enable strategic reclassification of data outside GDPR safeguards^(3).

In the light of these concerns and objections:

1. Does the Commission consider the legal concerns raised by Member States to be unfounded, and if so, can it specify which elements of the Member States’ analyses it disputes and on what legal basis?

2. How does the Commission reconcile its assertion that the omnibus cannot enable cross-system profiling or function creep with warnings by the Member States that horizontal GDPR amendments may have indirect effects across interoperable identity, travel, border and platform systems, irrespective of whether sectoral legislation is formally amended?

3. Given that several Member States explicitly question whether the proposal complies with the Charter, does the Commission maintain that no additional or revised fundamental rights assessment is required?

Submitted: 4.2.2026
