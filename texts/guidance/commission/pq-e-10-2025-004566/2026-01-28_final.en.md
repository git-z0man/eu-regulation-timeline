EN E-004566/2025

Answer given by Mr McGrath on behalf of the European Commission (28.1.2026)

The Commission proposed well-targeted amendments to the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)^(1)^(2). They do not touch on the fundamentals of the GDPR but aim to harmonise, clarify, and simplify its application, and do not negatively impact individuals’ fundamental rights, including their right to data protection. The proposed amendments are fully compliant with the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights^(3) and, for some parts, codify the existing case law of the Court of Justice of the European Union.^(4) They help controllers to better understand GDPR requirements and should therefore lead to improved data protection. They reflect the findings of the Commission in the GDPR Implementation Dialogue and the second report on the application of the GDPR^(5). The proposal for a Digital Omnibus is accompanied by a Staff Working Document^(6) presenting the assessment of the impacts expected from the proposed amendments.

One of the amendments clarifies the compatibility of further processing of personal data for certain privileged purposes currently set out in Article 5(1)(b) GDPR, like scientific or historic research. The envisaged clarification is not applicable to any other purposes.

The proposed amendments do not in any way permit profiling across various services and systems nor alter specific rules in other EU laws, such as the rules in Regulation (EU) 910/2014 limiting the use of personal data across various services^(7), the prohibition under Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 on providers of online platforms to present advertisements based on profiling using special categories of personal data or personal data when the recipient of the service is a minor^(8), the data protection rules applicable to large-scale IT systems^(9), those set-out in the Interoperability Regulations^(10) and in the Commission’s proposal on digital travel credentials^(11).
