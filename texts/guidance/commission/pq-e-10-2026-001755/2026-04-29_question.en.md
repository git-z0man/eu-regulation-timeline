Question for written answer E-001755/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

Giuseppe Antoci (The Left)

Subject: Risks from uncontrolled re-entry of space debris on EU territory

In recent months, there have been several sightings of things in the skies over Europe, including a recent luminous trail reported over Sicily, which can likely be traced back to space debris re‑entering the atmosphere.

According to data from the European Space Agency, the number of objects in orbit is increasing, with potential risks to public safety, the environment and infrastructure. Space debris is a growing form of orbital pollution, with possible impacts also on the territory of the European Union.

Currently, the European regulatory framework appears fragmented, and the EU mainly deals with monitoring and coordination, for example through the EU Space Surveillance and Tracking system, without a fully harmonised regime on prevention, mitigation and liability. However, it is encouraging that the Commission recently presented the proposal for the EU Space Act, which would be a first step towards a more coherent and binding regulatory framework on space activities and orbital sustainability.

In the light of the above:

1. Can the Commission confirm the timeline and state of play of the EU Space Act legislative initiative?

2. Will the Commission include legally binding obligations for the prevention and mitigation of space debris throughout the life cycle of satellites in the future EU Space Act?

3. Will it propose a harmonised EU liability regime for damage caused by space debris on EU territory or to EU infrastructure?

Submitted: 29.4.2026
