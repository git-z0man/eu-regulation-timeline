EN E-001755/2026 Answer given by Mr Kubilius on behalf of the European Commission (1.7.2026)

The EU Space Act^(1) was tabled by the Commission on the 25 June 2025 to strengthen the competitiveness and resilience of the European space sector by establishing an internal market for space. The ordinary legislative procedure is currently ongoing and the Commission services are committed to providing all necessary support to both co-legislators.

Given the disparities in national approaches to regulating space activities, the proposal for the EU Space Act lays down harmonised rules for key requirements in safety, resilience and environmental sustainability addressed in the authorisations for space activities. The safety requirements are envisaged to address the risks posed by the congestion of the orbits caused by the increasing proliferation of satellites constellations. The Act includes a set of collision avoidance measures, space debris mitigation rules, requirements to the trackability of spacecrafts and lay down key elements regarding the orbital traffic, and the coordination with air and maritime authorities to prevent and mitigate the risk of collision leading to the generation of debris both in space and on Earth.

The proposal for the EU Space Act preserves the national prerogatives of Member States as regards the authorisation and supervision of space activities therefore liability aspects of space activities are outside the scope of the proposal.
