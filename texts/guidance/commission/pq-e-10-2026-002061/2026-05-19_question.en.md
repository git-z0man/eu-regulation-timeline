Question for written answer E-002061/2026

to the Commission

Rule 144

Markéta Gregorová (Verts/ALE), Leila Chaibi (The Left), David Cormand (Verts/ALE)

Subject: Facial recognition feature on French police officers’ phones

The media organisation Disclose has revealed that French police officers were equipped with ‘NEO’ mobile phones containing software that allows for real-time biometric identification. The software can be used for investigations related to crimes, offences and even certain minor offences. The facial recognition feature does not seem to have had any safeguards in place regarding its use: police officers have used the software for mere identity checks. The software connects directly to the ‘TAJ’ database, which is estimated to contain 17 million files regarding suspects and 48 million files regarding victims, with an estimated 9 million facial images^(2)1. The database can contain very sensitive information, such as religion, sexual orientation, union membership, etc. The TAJ database has been heavily criticised by the French Data Protection Authority for its lack of accuracy^(3)2.

1. Does the Commission consider the system as being a real-time remote biometric identification system and does it consider that the system complies with the temporal, geographic and personal limitations established under the AI Act?

2. Does the Commission consider the requirement for prior judicial or independent administrative authorisation to be met?

3. Does the Commission consider the crimes and offences covered to be compliant with Annex II to the AI Act?
